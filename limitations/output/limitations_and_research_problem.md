## Research gaps and limitations

- **[L1] Attention-mask annealing is dropped at the deployment scale on a justification the paper's own ablation contradicts.**
  Annealing is removed entirely for the 7B adaptation, justified as having 'minimal impact' and as simplifying the flash-attention-2 implementation. The supporting ablation measures the opposite trend: the benefit grows with scale, +2.1 accuracy points at 124M and +2.5 at 355M. The paper therefore extrapolates 'minimal' roughly twentyfold past its largest tested model, in the direction its own data argues against, and adopts implementation convenience as the deciding criterion. Subsequent work has since isolated attention handling as the load-bearing problem in AR-to-diffusion adaptation, which is precisely the component removed here.
  *Provenance: author-stated, inferred, literature-grounded. Agent sources: EXT-6, ANA-1, CIT-1.*
- **[L2] The shift operation is the larger effect and receives the smaller share of scrutiny.**
  Removing the shift operation costs 11.9 to 15.2 accuracy points, roughly five times the annealing effect that the paper does discuss at length. The shift is inherited directly from autoregressive training dynamics, making it the component most likely to carry AR-specific assumptions into the diffusion model, yet its behaviour at scale is never examined the way annealing's is.
  *Provenance: inferred. Agent sources: ANA-2.*
- **[L3] The [MASK] token is an admitted workaround, applied inconsistently and confounded with model size.**
  The authors state the vocabulary should be expanded but reuse an existing word for implementation reasons, then apply this inconsistently: DiffuGPT-S and DiffuLLaMA reuse tokens 10541 and 811 while DiffuGPT-M receives a genuinely new token 50257. DiffuGPT-M is also the strongest performer at its scale. Mask-token treatment is therefore perfectly confounded with model size and never disentangled, and the 'low frequency' justification for the reused tokens is asserted without any corpus measurement.
  *Provenance: author-stated, inferred. Agent sources: EXT-7, ANA-3.*
- **[L4] The adaptation recipe is selected on a proxy task and never validated on adaptation training itself.**
  The authors state that direct ablation on adaptation training is too costly and substitute cheaper finetuning experiments on GSM8K-symbolic to choose the recipe. Every recipe conclusion therefore rests on the untested assumption that the proxy ranks components the same way the real 30B-to-65B-token procedure would. No check on that assumption is reported, so the recipe that defines the paper's method was never measured under the conditions in which it is used.
  *Provenance: author-stated, inferred. Agent sources: EXT-8, ANA-4.*
- **[L5] Instruction tuning is deferred despite the paper's own evidence that its absence is causing failures.**
  Chain-of-thought prompting reduces accuracy, which the authors attribute to the absence of instruction tuning while citing prior work showing diffusion LMs benefit from it -- and then defer it to future work. Concurrent work performing adaptation plus instruction tuning reports that both the adaptation training and the choice of base model are decisive, indicating the deferred component is not a refinement but part of what makes the approach competitive.
  *Provenance: author-stated, literature-grounded. Agent sources: EXT-4, CIT-4.*
- **[L6] A large accuracy headroom is measured, diagnosed, and then left uninvestigated.**
  Hit-rate over generated candidates substantially exceeds single-answer accuracy across reasoning benchmarks. The authors read this correctly as high answer uncertainty and call the shortfall 'temporarily suboptimal', implying more training will resolve it. The measurement localises the deficit in answer selection rather than answer knowledge, but no calibration analysis, reranking, or verifier is attempted, and the implication that training alone closes the gap is never tested.
  *Provenance: author-stated, inferred. Agent sources: EXT-5, ANA-6.*
- **[L7] Every reported number is an unconverged lower bound, and the budget mismatch with the baseline is disclosed only in the appendix.**
  The authors state the models are undertrained, that the model shows no sign of saturation, and that DiffuLLaMA falls short of the LLaMA2 model it was adapted from, attributing that shortfall to the data subset without testing the attribution. The appendix further concedes the training budget does not match GPT2's roughly 200B tokens while the main-body tables present the comparison as direct. The showcased generation samples in Table 12 are largely incoherent, which sits uneasily beside the fluency and diversity numbers reported in the body and is never discussed.
  *Provenance: author-stated, inferred. Agent sources: EXT-1, EXT-2, EXT-9, ANA-8.*
- **[L8] The code and infilling claims are broader than the evaluation that supports them.**
  Code generation is advertised in the abstract and introduction, but the only code evaluation is HumanEval single-line infilling with prefix and suffix supplied, and that number belongs to a separately trained Diffu-CodeLLaMA rather than the released checkpoint. The authors additionally flag their own infilling comparison as potentially unfair because suffix information is withheld from baselines, and never run a corrected version. The evaluation also supplies the span length, whereas later work identifies infilling at flexible length and position without ground-truth positional data as the critical difficulty.
  *Provenance: author-stated, peer-review, literature-grounded. Agent sources: EXT-3, REV-1, CIT-6.*
- **[L9] The efficiency claim is not compute-normalised, and later measurement of adapted models contests it.**
  Decoding latency is reported at batch size 1 against a single autoregressive configuration, with no FLOPs- or forward-pass-normalised comparison, despite diffusion requiring full self-attention over the entire sequence at every denoising step with no key-value caching. No optimised AR baseline such as speculative decoding is included. Subsequent work measuring models adapted from AR checkpoints specifically reports limited speedup relative to AR baselines, attributed to a pretrain-to-posttrain mismatch -- the exact regime this paper operates in and does not measure.
  *Provenance: inferred, literature-grounded. Agent sources: ANA-5, CIT-5.*
- **[L10] One adaptation route, two model families, and no comparison against cheaper or alternative routes.**
  Every result derives from GPT2 or LLaMA2 and none exceeds 7B, so the central claim that AR checkpoints convert into competitive diffusion models is supported only for the two architectures tested. The paper also commits to one route -- continued denoising training into masked diffusion -- without comparing against alternatives that the surrounding literature has since shown are available: representation alignment instead of retraining, adaptation to uniform-noise diffusion where every token stays editable rather than permanently committed, and giving autoregressive models masked infilling directly without diffusion adaptation at all.
  *Provenance: inferred, literature-grounded. Agent sources: ANA-7, CIT-2, CIT-3, CIT-8.*
- **[L11] Statistical and reproducibility rigor is insufficient to support the recipe decisions.**
  No result carries a seed, variance estimate, or significance test, including the ablations whose 2.1-to-2.5-point differences drive the recipe decisions. The 7B run consumes 65B tokens on multi-node hardware with no released data manifest or sampling seed. Decoding configurations are unstated for both sides of key comparisons despite the paper's own figures showing sharp sensitivity to step count. The released inference path defaults to eager attention and builds an all-zeros four-dimensional mask that is a mathematical no-op while blocking the flash-attention kernel the annealing decision was justified by. Continual pre-training is additionally known to be sensitive to warmup and learning-rate schedule, neither of which is ablated.
  *Provenance: peer-review, literature-grounded. Agent sources: REV-2, REV-3, REV-4, REV-5, CIT-7.*
- **[L12] Data provenance, contamination, and release transparency are not addressed.**
  The adaptation corpus mixes web-scraped text with Starcoder source code carrying heterogeneous software licences, with no licensing or data statement. No benchmark decontamination is reported despite continual pre-training on tens of billions of web and code tokens followed by evaluation on public benchmarks, leaving contamination unexcluded as a partial explanation of performance. Open weights are released without a model card, intended-use statement, or dual-use discussion.
  *Provenance: peer-review. Agent sources: REV-6, REV-7, REV-8.*

## Research problem

DiffuLLaMA's central claim is that continual pre-training converts a pretrained autoregressive
model into a competitive diffusion language model. That claim rests on an adaptation recipe --
attention-mask annealing, the inherited shift operation, and a reused `[MASK]` token -- whose
components were never measured under the conditions in which they are actually used. The recipe
was selected on a cheaper proxy task rather than on adaptation training itself (L4); its costliest
component was removed entirely at the 7B scale the headline results depend on, justified as having
"minimal impact" by an ablation whose own numbers show the benefit *growing* with scale (L1); the
component with roughly five times the measured effect, the shift operation, receives the least
scrutiny of the three and is the one most likely to carry autoregressive assumptions forward (L2);
and the third, the `[MASK]` token, is applied inconsistently in a way that leaves the
best-performing model in the paper perfectly confounded with the only genuinely new token (L3).
Each individual choice is defensible as engineering. Together they mean that the recipe defining
the method has never been isolated from the convenience that shaped it, at the scale that matters.

The literature retrieved for this analysis has since converged on exactly this seam. Work published
after the base paper isolates attention handling -- not weight reuse -- as the load-bearing problem
in autoregressive-to-diffusion adaptation, and studies it at the level of attention specifically
(L1). Other work proposes adaptation routes the base paper never compares against: representation
alignment instead of continued denoising training, adaptation to uniform-noise diffusion in which
every token remains editable rather than permanently committed once unmasked, and equipping
autoregressive models with masked infilling directly, without diffusion adaptation at all (L10).
Concurrent work performing adaptation together with instruction tuning reports that both the
adaptation training and the choice of base model are decisive, which bears directly on a component
this paper defers despite its own evidence that the absence is causing failures (L5), and on a
recipe validated across only two model families (L10). The surrounding literature is therefore not
merely adjacent to the base paper's gaps; it is actively testing the assumptions the base paper
left untested.

**The continuation problem this motivates is to establish what autoregressive-to-diffusion
adaptation actually requires, as opposed to what was adopted for implementation convenience, and
to determine whether the resulting model carries measurable autoregressive residue that the recipe
was supposed to remove.** Concretely, this means two linked questions. First, a recipe audit:
holding data, budget and evaluation fixed, isolate the contribution of annealing, the shift
operation and mask-token treatment across a scale ladder, disentangling mask-token treatment from
model size -- the confound the base paper leaves standing -- and testing whether the proxy-task
ranking that selected the recipe survives contact with adaptation training itself. Second, an
adaptation-residue audit: determine whether models adapted from autoregressive checkpoints retain
measurable autoregressive inductive bias at inference relative to comparable models trained from
scratch, since the recipe's purpose is precisely to remove that bias, and the base paper never
verifies that it did. The first question asks whether the recipe's components earn their cost; the
second asks whether the recipe achieves what it was designed to achieve. Neither has been answered
for full-sequence diffusion adaptation at the scale the base paper deploys.

**Scoping note.** The audit's strongest form -- re-running 7B adaptation with and without annealing
-- is not achievable on the compute available to this project: the base paper's own appendix
reports 65B tokens on multi-node A100/GH200 hardware, and a faithful reproduction of even the
124M/355M ablation grid is on the order of a thousand GPU-hours. The tractable forms are a scale
ladder at 124M-355M with the mask-token confound broken, which is a training study, and the
adaptation-residue audit, which is inference-only on released checkpoints and therefore achievable
on a single GPU. The second is the cheaper entry point and the one that can produce a result
without any training run.
