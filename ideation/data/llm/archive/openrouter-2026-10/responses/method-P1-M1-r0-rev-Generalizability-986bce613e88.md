Review: The method exhibits moderate generalizability within the discrete diffusion language model (DLM) paradigm. The protocol is explicitly model-agnostic across AR-derived DLMs (DiffuGPT, DiffuLLaMA, Dream, LLaDA, DiffuCoder), employs standard benchmarks, and allows substitution of preservation metrics (CKA, linear probing, Procrustes) and lightweight scorers. However, its applicability is constrained to models converted from AR backbones and may not extend to continuous diffusion models, flow-matching approaches, or DLMs trained from scratch without AR initialization. The reliance on a fixed benchmark suite (HumanEval, GSM8K, SIQA, WinoGrande) limits coverage to reasoning and commonsense domains, excluding code synthesis, multilingual generation, or long-form text. Additionally, the fixed step budgets {16, 32, 64, 128} and the specific AR-to-DLM conversion paradigm bound the operational context.

Feedback: To strengthen generalizability, validate the pipeline on non-AR-derived DLMs (e.g., models initialized with AR weights but distinct architectures) and continuous diffusion models to test boundary conditions. Expand the benchmark suite to include multilingual tasks and diverse code datasets beyond HumanEval. Explicitly test whether CKA preservation scores generalize across different tokenizer vocabularies and sequence lengths. Finally, clarify whether the unified quality score (UQS) averaging across heterogeneous tasks obscures task-specific generalizability patterns; report per-task results alongside UQS to reveal whether preservation predicts reranking gains uniformly across domains.
