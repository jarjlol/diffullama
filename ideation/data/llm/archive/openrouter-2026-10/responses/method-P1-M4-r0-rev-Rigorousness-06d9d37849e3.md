**Review:**

The proposed AR-Guided Selective Denoising (AGSD) method demonstrates a well-structured and systematic approach to investigating the quality-compute trade-off in diffusion language models. The method logically progresses from model preparation through metric definition, the AGSD intervention, Pareto construction, and statistical analysis, directly addressing the unresolved issues (L6, L9) identified in the target paper. The inclusion of generalization checks, sensitivity analysis, and baseline comparisons strengthens the experimental design. However, several precision gaps undermine full rigorousness: (1) the selective refinement mechanism lacks technical detail on how masked attention is implemented during the S₁ diffusion steps, (2) the FLOPs estimation formula is overly simplistic and doesn't account for architectural differences or the non-linear cost of partial-token updates, (3) the assumption that AR-model entropy reliably identifies tokens needing refinement is unvalidated, and (4) the unified quality metric's reliance on min-max normalization across conditions introduces relativity that could obscure absolute performance differences. The 10-week timeline with selective denoising implementation appears optimistic given the technical complexity.

**Feedback:**
To enhance rigorousness, the authors should: (a) provide detailed pseudocode or architectural modifications for the selective refinement pass, specifically how attention masks are constructed for frozen vs. refined tokens; (b) validate the FLOP estimate empirically for selective denoising (since partial updates may not scale linearly); (c) include a ablation studying whether AR uncertainty actually correlates with diffusion error on a sample of prompts; (d) consider using a fixed reference point for min-max normalization rather than condition-relative normalization to ensure metric stability; and (e) clarify the diffusion sampler type and temperature parameters used throughout. These additions would substantially improve reproducibility and methodological precision.
