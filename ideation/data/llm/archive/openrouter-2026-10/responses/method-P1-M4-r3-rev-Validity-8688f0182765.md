Review: The CFAR method directly addresses the research problem by proposing a structured trade-off between candidate exploration and refinement, explicitly targeting the accuracy headroom (L6) and compute-normalization gap (L9) identified in the target paper. The experimental design with Pareto frontiers and bootstrap significance testing is methodologically sound. However, the method's validity is compromised by its reliance on AR model NLL as a proxy for task-specific quality—particularly for code generation (HumanEval) and mathematical reasoning (GSM8K) where likelihood does not correlate with correctness—and by an oversimplified FLOP model that ignores hardware-level efficiency differences between diffusion and AR architectures.

Feedback: While the two-stage coarse-to-fine schedule is innovative, the assumption that candidates generated with only 4–16 denoising steps remain "refinable" by additional diffusion steps is questionable for low-quality initial samples that may have collapsed to poor modes. Additionally, using GPT-2-small/medium or LLaMA-7B as "lightweight" scorers may not constitute a lightweight overhead relative to the 7B DLMs being evaluated. The unified quality metric (UQM) averaging across disparate benchmarks (code, math, commonsense) may obscure domain-specific trade-offs and mask scenarios where the AR scorer fails. To strengthen validity, the authors should: (1) validate the AR-scorer correlation with actual benchmark performance on a held-out set before full deployment; (2) report wall-clock latency measurements rather than purely theoretical FLOP estimates to account for architectural efficiency differences; and (3) test whether the coarse-stage outputs are indeed recoverable by the fine stage, or whether very low step counts produce irrecoverable samples that degrade the Pareto frontier.

Rating (1-5): 3
