**Review:**  
The AGSD method directly addresses the research problem by proposing token-wise selective refinement guided by AR-model entropy to improve quality at fixed compute. However, its validity is compromised by three critical flaws: (1) the use of AR-model token entropy as a proxy for diffusion-model uncertainty is scientifically unjustified—there is no guarantee that the AR base model’s confidence correlates with the diffusion model’s generation quality, especially across diverse benchmarks like code and commonsense reasoning; (2) the implementation of “selective denoising” with fixed unmasked tokens likely requires architectural modifications (e.g., custom attention masking or gradient stopping) that may not be supported by standard released DLM checkpoints, threatening the strict inference-only constraint; and (3) the Unified Quality Metric (UQM) obscures domain-specific trade-offs by aggregating heterogeneous benchmarks into a single scalar, potentially masking failures in critical areas like mathematical reasoning. Consequently, the claimed Pareto improvements may reflect artifacts of the uncertainty heuristic rather than genuine algorithmic gains, and the compute-normalized efficiency claims rest on an unverified assumption that full-sequence processing costs are identical between uniform and selective steps.

**Feedback:**  
- **Validate the uncertainty signal**: Empirically measure the correlation between AR-token entropy and diffusion-model calibration error (e.g., ECE) before relying on it for masking; consider using the diffusion model’s own prediction entropy if available.  
- **Clarify implementation feasibility**: Specify whether partial denoising requires modifying the diffusion model’s attention mask or codebase; if so, the “released checkpoint only” constraint is violated, and the method’s generality is limited.  
- **Disaggregate metrics**: Report per-benchmark results alongside UQM to prevent masking of failures, and consider using a geometric mean or benchmark-specific Pareto fronts instead of a single scalar.  
- **Contextualize against existing inference-time methods**: Directly compare against Jacobi Forcing and TESS 2 reward guidance to demonstrate whether selective refinement offers a genuine advantage over existing guidance mechanisms.  
- **Audit FLOP accounting**: Confirm that selective refinement does not actually reduce FLOPs (since the full sequence is processed); if FLOPs are identical to uniform baselines, reframe the hypothesis as “guided refinement improves quality at equal compute” rather than “improves efficiency,” and ensure the Pareto analysis controls for this.
