Review: The proposed method is a rigorous empirical benchmarking framework that systematically investigates the quality-compute trade-off in DLMs. Its primary novelty lies in the adaptive two-stage allocation strategy (explore-then-refine with AR scoring) and a fixed-reference normalization scheme for fair comparison. However, the core components—candidate reranking, Pareto front construction, GAM-based marginal gain analysis, and bootstrap significance testing—are standard techniques assembled into a comprehensive experimental design. The method stays inference-only, which constrains novelty by design. The adaptive budget allocation heuristic is the most novel element, but it is grounded in straightforward principles rather than a fundamentally new algorithmic insight.

Feedback: To strengthen innovativeness, consider: (1) grounding the adaptive two-stage strategy in a theoretical convergence guarantee or regret bound that explains when and why it outperforms static allocation; (2) introducing a learnable allocation policy (e.g., a lightweight controller that dynamically adjusts S per candidate based on intermediate AR scores) rather than a fixed floor/top-k rule; (3) exploring whether the AR scorer itself can be distilled into a tiny specialized selector that goes beyond likelihood; (4) explicitly contrasting with Jacobi Forcing's training-time approach to highlight what inference-only reallocation can and cannot achieve. The current method is a thorough empirical study but risks being viewed as a careful benchmarking exercise rather than a conceptual advance.
