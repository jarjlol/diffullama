**Method: Sequential Monte Carlo AR‑Guided Diffusion (SMC‑AR)**  

**Rationale**  
The two previously proposed approaches both (i) quantify how much a diffusion language model (DLM) retains the autoregressive (AR) representation geometry of its pretrained backbone (using CKA/linear probing or subspace angles) and (ii) compare a *post‑hoc* reranking step (scoring final candidates with a small AR model) against the gain obtained by simply running more denoising steps.  
To obtain a fundamentally different angle, we replace the *post‑hoc* selection with an *in‑process* guidance mechanism that uses the AR scorer to re‑weight the diffusion trajectory as it unfolds. This treats the AR scorer as a proposal distribution in a sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) framework: at each denoising step we evaluate how AR‑like the current partially denoised sequence is, update importance weights, and occasionally resample to focus computation on the most AR‑consistent particles.  

If a DLM already preserves AR representations well, its intermediate states will receive high AR‑scorer likelihoods throughout denoising, so the SMC procedure will need little re‑weighting and will closely resemble the standard diffusion sample. Conversely, when preservation is poor, the AR scorer will sharply discriminate between particles, allowing the SMC‑AR process to steer the generation toward regions of the output space that are both high‑likelihood under the AR model and reachable by the diffusion dynamics.  

By measuring the quality improvement obtained from this *guided diffusion* relative to (a) standard sampling at the same compute budget, (b) standard sampling with more denoising steps, and (c) the baseline post‑hoc reranking, we can directly test whether (1) the degree of AR‑representation preservation predicts how much an AR‑scorer‑guided procedure can recover the accuracy headroom, and (2) whether such guidance is more efficient per unit of compute than simply increasing the denoising‑step budget.  

Because the method only requires forward passes through the released DLM checkpoints and a frozen, tiny AR scorer (GPT‑2‑small), it satisfies the inference‑only constraint, fits within the RTX 6000 Pro Blackwell memory (via 8‑bit quantisation and activation checkpointing for the largest DLM), and can be completed in the ten‑week timeline by parallelising prompt processing across the three GPU‑enabled team members.

### Step‑by‑step Procedure  

| Step | Goal | Concrete Actions | Resources & Constraints |
|------|------|------------------|--------------------------|
| **0. Preparation** | Build an inference‑only, reproducible pipeline. | • Download the released DLM checkpoints (DiffuGPT‑S/M, DiffuLLaMA‑6.74B, Dream‑7B, LLaDA‑8B, DiffuCoder‑7B) and their AR base models (GPT‑2‑small/medium for GPT‑2‑based DLMs; LLaMA‑7B for LLaMA‑based DLMs). <br>• Tokenize a held‑out probing corpus (10 k sentences from WikiText‑103 + C4) with each model’s tokenizer; store token IDs. <br>• Assemble the benchmark suite (HumanEval, GSM8K, SIQA, WinoGrande) and implement a min‑max normalisation script that maps every raw metric to \([0,1]\) across all experimental conditions. <br>• Apply activation‑checkpointing and 8‑bit quantisation (`bitsandbytes`) to the 6.74B DiffuLLaMA so it fits in the 96 GB GPU with batch‑size ≥ 4. <br>• Split work among the three GPU‑enabled team members: each member owns a subset of models/step‑budgets and writes results to a shared CSV/Parquet store. | One RTX 6000 Pro Blackwell (96 GB), 8‑bit quantisation + activation checkpointing, CPU‑only work for data prep/eval harnesses. |
| **1. Quantify AR‑representation preservation** | Obtain a scalar preservation score \(P_i\) for each DLM that reflects similarity of its hidden‑state geometry to the AR backbone (no gradients). | **1.1 Feature extraction** – For every sentence in the probing corpus, run the DLM **inference‑only** (no gradient) and collect the post‑attention hidden‑state tensor of each Transformer layer (shape \(N\times D\)). Do the same with the frozen AR base model (same tokenizer, same sequence length). <br>**1.2 Similarity metric** – Compute **Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA)** with a linear kernel between the DLM and AR hidden‑state matrices for each layer. <br>**1.3 Aggregation** – Average CKA across layers, weighting deeper layers more (exponential decay with depth). The resulting scalar is the preservation score \(P_i\). | Only forward passes; linear‑algebra on CPU/GPU. No training. |
| **2. Particle‑based denoising with AR‑scorer weighting (SMC‑AR)** | Generate outputs while using the AR scorer to guide the diffusion trajectory via importance weighting/resampling. | For each prompt and each DLM: <br>• Set particle count \(K = 8\) (fits comfortably in memory). <br>• Initialise \(K\) particles as i.i.d. noise sequences (the same starting point used by the DLM sampler). <br>• For denoising step \(t = T, T-1, \dots, 1\) (where \(T\) is the target step budget, e.g., 16, 32, 64, 128): <br> - **DLM transition**: apply the DLM’s denoising update to each particle to obtain a less‑noisy sequence (standard diffusion step). <br> - **AR‑scorer evaluation**: feed each particle’s current sequence to the frozen GPT‑2‑small (teacher‑forcing) and compute the average negative log‑likelihood (or log‑likelihood) \(\ell_{t}^{(k)}\). <br> - **Weight update** (in log space for stability): \(\log w_{t}^{(k)} = \log w_{t-1}^{(k)} + \ell_{t}^{(k)}\); initialise \(\log w_{0}^{(k)} = 0\). <br> - **Normalise**: \(w_{t}^{(k)} = \exp(\log w_{t}^{(k)} - \max_j \log w_{t}^{(j)})\); then \( \tilde w_{t}^{(k)} = w_{t}^{(k)} / \sum_j w_{t}^{(j)}\). <br> - **Resampling** (optional but recommended): compute effective sample size \(ESS = 1/\sum_k (\tilde w_{t}^{(k)})^2\). If \(ESS < K/2\), draw \(K\) offspring particles via systematic resampling using \(\tilde w_{t}^{(k)}\) as probabilities and reset all weights to uniform. <br>• After the final step, select the particle with the highest final weight as the SMC‑AR output for that prompt. | Pure inference; each step requires \(K\) forward passes through the tiny AR scorer (≈ 0.3 % of a denoising step FLOPs). Total extra cost ≈ \(K \times T\) AR‑scorer passes per prompt. |
| **3. Baseline outputs for comparison** | Provide reference points to isolate the effect of AR‑scorer guidance. | • **Standard DLM top‑1** at the same low step budget \(T_{\text{low}} = 16\) (no weighting, no resampling). <br>• **Standard DLM top‑1** at a high step budget \(T_{\text{high}} = 128\) (to measure step‑gain). <br>• **Post‑hoc reranking**: run standard DLM sampling at \(T_{\text{low}}\), keep the final \(K\) particles, score each with the AR scorer (average negative log‑likelihood), and pick the highest‑scoring candidate (this replicates the reranking baseline from the original proposal). | All are inference‑only; no extra training. |
| **4. Unified quality measurement** | Translate raw outputs into a comparable score across tasks. | For each benchmark: <br>– HumanEval: pass@k for k = 1,10. <br>– GSM8K: exact‑match accuracy. <br>– SIQA & WinoGrande: accuracy (multiple‑choice). <br>Normalise each raw metric to \([0,1]\) across **all** conditions (model × step‑budget × method). <br>Define the **Unified Quality Score (UQS)** as the arithmetic mean of the four normalised components (HumanEval@1, HumanEval@10, GSM8K, (SIQA+WinoGrande)/2). <br>Also compute the *oracle* UQS (best particle among the \(K\) generated) to quantify the accuracy headroom for each condition. | Metrics computed offline on CPU; no extra GPU load. |
| **5. Experimental contrasts** | Isolate the three sources of improvement: (a) AR‑scorer‑guided diffusion (SMC‑AR), (b) post‑hoc reranking, (c) more denoising steps. | For each DLM \(i\) and low step budget \(T_{\text{low}}=16\): <br> - **SMC‑AR gain**: \(\Delta^{\text{SMC}}_{i} = \text{UQS}_{i,T_{\text{low}}}^{\text{SMC}} - \text{UQS}_{i,T_{\text{low}}}^{\text{top‑1}}\). <br> - **Reranking gain**: \(\Delta^{\text{rank}}_{i} = \text{UQS}_{i,T_{\text{low}}}^{\text{ranked}} - \text{UQS}_{i,T_{\text{low}}}^{\text{top‑1}}\). <br> - **Step gain**: \(\Delta^{\text{step}}_{i} = \text{UQS}_{i,T_{\text{high}}}^{\text{top‑1}} - \text{UQS}_{i,T_{\text{low}}}^{\text{top‑1}}\). | Requires only the three step budgets (16, 32, 64, 128) and the three methods; no additional model runs. |
| **6. Statistical modelling** | Test whether preservation predicts the relative benefit of AR‑scorer guidance vs. extra steps, and whether this differs by architecture or step budget. | Fit a **linear mixed‑effects model** (LME) with the *relative efficiency* as the dependent variable: <br>\[
\eta_{ij}= \beta_0 + \beta_1 P_i + \beta_2 A_i + \beta_3 (P_i \times A_i) + \beta_4 S_j + u_i + \epsilon_{ij}
\] <br>where: <br>• \(\eta_{ij}= \frac{\Delta^{\text{SMC}}_{i,S_j}}{\Delta^{\text{step}}_{i}}\) (ratio of SMC‑AR gain to step‑gain at step budget \(S_j\)). <br>• \(P_i\) = preservation score (continuous). <br>• \(A_i\) = architecture dummy (0 = GPT‑2‑based, 1 = LLaMA‑based). <br>• \(S_j\) = denoising‑step budget (log‑scaled continuous). <br>• \(u_i\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2_{\text{model}})\) captures model‑specific random intercepts. <br>Key hypotheses: <br> - **H1**: \(\beta_1 > 0\) (higher preservation → larger SMC‑AR benefit relative to extra steps). <br> - **H2**: \(\beta_3 \neq 0\) (the preservation‑benefit relationship differs between GPT‑2‑ and LLaMA‑based DLMs). <br> - **H3**: \(\beta_4 < 0\) (the relative advantage of SMC‑AR diminishes as the step budget grows, because extra steps become less costly). <br>Additionally, compute Spearman’s \(\rho\) between \(P_i\) and the *absolute* SMC‑AR gain \(\Delta^{\text{SMC}}_{i}\) to confirm that preservation predicts raw improvement. <br>All modelling performed on CPU (e.g., `statsmodels` or `lme4` via R). | CPU‑only; no extra GPU time. |
| **7. Efficiency accounting** | Translate quality gains into comparable compute cost to answer the “more efficient than extra steps?” question. | • Measure wall‑clock time for a single denoising step (averaged over 100 runs) for each DLM (with activation checkpointing & 8‑bit quant). Call this \(t^{\text{step}}_i\). <br>• Measure the average time for one forward pass of the AR scorer (GPT‑2‑small) on a sequence of length equal to the denoising step’s output (≈ 0.5 ms). Call this \(t^{\text{scorer}}_i\). <br>• **Cost of SMC‑AR**: \(C^{\text{SMC}}_i = T_{\text{low}} \times t^{\text{step}}_i + K \times T_{\text{low}} \times t^{\text{scorer}}_i\) (denoising steps + AR‑scorer evaluations per step per particle). <br>• **Cost of adding steps**: \(C^{\text{step}}_i = (T_{\text{high}}-T_{\text{low}}) \times t^{\text{step}}_i\). <br>• Define **efficiency ratio**: \(\displaystyle \epsilon_i = \frac{\Delta^{\text{SMC}}_{i} / C^{\text{SMC}}_i}{\Delta^{\text{step}}_{i} / C^{\text{step}}_i}\). <br>Values > 1 indicate that the AR‑scorer‑guided diffusion yields more quality per unit of compute than simply increasing the denoising‑step budget. | Timing measurements are cheap (< 5 min total). |
| **8. Ablation & robustness checks** | Verify that findings are not artefacts of specific choices. | • Vary particle count \(K\) (4, 16) and resampling threshold (ESS < K/3, K/2). <br>• Replace CKA with **SVCCA** or **Procrustes similarity** as alternative preservation metrics. <br>• Use a larger AR scorer (GPT‑2‑medium, 355 M) to test whether scorer capacity interacts with preservation. <br>• Run a *control* where the AR scorer’s likelihood is replaced by a uniform score (i.e., no guidance) to ensure gains are not due to resampling alone. <br>• Test alternative denoising schedules (e.g., cosine vs. linear) to confirm generality. | All ablations reuse the same generated particle trajectories; no extra generation needed. |
| **9. Reporting & reproducibility** | Ensure transparent dissemination. | • Archive all scripts, random seeds, and environment specifications (Dockerfile or Conda yaml). <br>• Publish the extracted hidden‑state features (optional) and the final results table on an open repository (e.g., HuggingFace datasets + Zenodo). <br>• Provide a short “re‑run” notebook that reproduces the key figures (preservation vs. SMC‑AR gain, efficiency ratio). | Fits the ten‑week timeline: weeks 1‑2 for setup, weeks 3‑5 for generation & scoring, week 6 for metric computation, weeks 7‑8 for statistical analysis, weeks 9‑10 for write‑up and reproducibility packaging. |

### Why this method is substantively different  

| Aspect | Prior Proposals | **SMC‑AR** |
|--------|----------------|-----------|
| **Use of AR scorer** | Post‑hoc scoring of final candidates (likelihood) *or* scoring of final hidden‑state similarity. | **In‑process guidance**: the AR scorer evaluates every intermediate denoising particle, updates importance weights, and triggers resampling—effectively steering the diffusion trajectory as it unfolds. |
| **Preservation measurement** | Static scalar (CKA or linear probing) computed once on a held‑out corpus. | Same static preservation score is retained, but we additionally examine how it predicts the *dynamic* usefulness of the AR scorer across denoising steps (via the weight updates). |
| **Gain measured** | (a) Reranking gain vs. (b) step‑gain, both computed on final outputs. | Three gains: (a) **SMC‑AR gain** (guidance throughout denoising), (b) **post‑hoc reranking gain**, (c) **step‑gain**. This lets us test whether *guided diffusion* recovers headroom more efficiently than either reranking alone or extra steps alone. |
| **Efficiency comparison** | Ratio of reranking gain to step‑gain (ignoring extra AR‑scorer cost). | Explicit **efficiency ratio** that accounts for the extra AR‑scorer forward passes required by SMC‑AR, allowing a fair “quality per FLOP” comparison with simply adding denoising steps. |
| **Statistical test** | Correlation between preservation and reranking/step gains. | Mixed‑effects model of the *relative efficiency* (SMC‑AR gain ÷ step‑gain) as a function of preservation, architecture, and step budget, directly addressing the research question about whether preservation determines the *efficiency* of AR‑scorer‑based recovery. |

By treating the AR scorer as a sequential importance‑sampling guide rather than a static reranker, SMC‑AR provides a novel mechanistic lens on the interplay between representation preservation, inference‑time compute, and accuracy headroom recovery—fulfilling the request for a method that is clear, innovative, rigorous, valid, and generalizable.
