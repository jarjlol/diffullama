Review: The proposed TADAG method addresses a legitimate gap in the literature—quantifying the quality-compute trade-off in DLMs—but its core innovation (token-level freezing based on AR confidence) suffers from fundamental scientific weaknesses. First, there is no theoretical or empirical justification that AR likelihood correlates with DLM denoising quality; AR and diffusion models optimize different objectives, and high AR confidence may simply indicate AR-style artifacts rather than correct DLM outputs. Second, the computational overhead is underestimated: scoring the AR model at every denoising step for every token (N × S_max evaluations) likely dominates the diffusion FLOPs, contradicting the claim of "negligible" overhead and making the comparison to uniform-S baselines unfair. Third, the freezing mechanism creates a commitment bias—once a token is frozen based on potentially misleading AR confidence, errors propagate irreversibly through subsequent denoising steps. The method also lacks critical ablation studies: what fraction of tokens freeze? Does freezing actually improve quality, or merely reduce compute at a quality cost? The generalization checks (alternative scorers like BERT-MLM) are scientifically dubious since MLM likelihood operates in a different space entirely. While the experimental design (Pareto fronts, AUPC, bootstrap testing) is rigorous, the underlying premise—that AR guidance provides a new orthogonal trade-off axis—remains unvalidated and likely conflates correlation with causation in the AR-DLM relationship.

Feedback: Before proceeding, the authors must: (1) validate that AR confidence scores actually predict DLM token accuracy on held-out data; (2) perform a rigorous FLOP accounting that includes the per-step AR scoring cost in the baseline comparisons; (3) analyze the distribution of frozen tokens across steps to ensure the mechanism isn't simply collapsing to a trivial solution (e.g., freezing everything at step T or nothing); (4) compare against a "fair" baseline where the AR scorer is used only at the end (as in the original proposal) to isolate the benefit of token-adaptive guidance from simple reranking; (5) address the theoretical mismatch between AR autoregressive conditioning and DLM parallel denoising. Without these controls, the method risks measuring the benefits of additional AR inference compute rather than genuine quality improvements from token-adaptive diffusion sampling.
