Review: The method is a well-designed, rigorous empirical study that asks a novel research question — whether AR-representation preservation (measured via CKA) predicts the effectiveness of lightweight reranking versus additional denoising steps in DLMs. The individual techniques (CKA, linear probing, multi-candidate generation, tiny AR scorer, gain-ratios) are all established, but their specific combination and the efficiency-comparison framework (η_i and ε_i) constitute a fresh approach. The cross-family scorer ablation and the bridging of representation-alignment literature with inference-time trade-off methods add conceptual novelty. However, the method does not introduce new algorithms, architectures, or training procedures — it is fundamentally an observational/analytical study using existing tools.

Feedback: Consider explicitly framing the contribution as a "diagnostic study" rather than a methodological one, which would set appropriate expectations. The ε_i (compute-normalised efficiency ratio) is the most novel analytical contribution — highlight this more prominently. One concern: with only 5 models, the Spearman correlations in Step 6 are underpowered; the Bayesian regression helps but doesn't fully resolve this. Also, the random-scorer control in ablation is a good sanity check but should be a primary analysis row, not buried in ablation.

Rating (1-5): 3
