**Review:**

The method demonstrates a moderate level of generalizability through its systematic testing across five distinct DLM architectures spanning two AR families (GPT-2 and LLaMA), multiple benchmark domains (coding, mathematical reasoning, commonsense), and alternative lightweight scorers (TinyLLaMA-60M, BERT-base MLM). The inclusion of held-out prompts (HumanEval-plus, MBPP, BBH), a 3B DLM from the dLLM zoo, and a sequence-length ablation (L=128 vs. L=256) further strengthens the claim that observed trade-offs are not artifacts of a single model or setting. The adaptive compute-allocation strategy is conceptually framework-agnostic and could theoretically extend to other generation paradigms.

However, several substantive limitations constrain generalizability: (1) all experiments are confined to a single GPU architecture (RTX 6000 Pro Blackwell), making hardware-dependent efficiency claims non-transferable; (2) the method is restricted to English-only benchmarks and discrete diffusion models, excluding continuous diffusion/flow-matching approaches (e.g., YAN/MoE-FM) and cross-lingual scenarios; (3) the inference-only constraint prevents assessment of whether the adaptive strategy generalizes to fine-tuned or instruction-tuned DLMs; (4) the fixed maximum sequence length (128 tokens, with only a 20% ablation at 256) limits applicability to long-form generation; and (5) the AR NLL scorer's effectiveness is validated only via Spearman correlation on held-out prompts, without testing whether the ranking quality transfers across fundamentally different DLM architectures (e.g., flow-matching-based models). The FLOP multiplier α_i is profiled per-model but assumes linearity that may break for architectures with different layer configurations or sparse attention patterns.

**Feedback:**

To improve generalizability, consider: (a) testing on at least one non-English benchmark or multilingual task to assess language-transfer; (b) including one continuous diffusion or flow-matching model (e.g., YAN) to verify that the quality-compute trade-off patterns hold beyond discrete diffusion; (c) validating the adaptive strategy on fine-tuned/instruction-tuned DLMs (e.g., Dream-Instruct, DiffuCoder-Instruct) to ensure the reranker generalizes beyond base models; (d) profiling on a second GPU architecture to confirm FLOP-to-latency mapping robustness; (e) extending the sequence-length ablation to L=512 or L=1024 to assess scalability; and (f) replacing token-overlap with semantic diversity metrics (e.g., embedding cosine distance) for the candidate independence check.

**Rating (1-5): 3**
