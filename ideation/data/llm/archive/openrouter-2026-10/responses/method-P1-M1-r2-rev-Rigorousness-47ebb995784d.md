Review: The method presents a highly systematic, nine-step protocol that meticulously operationalizes the research question into measurable constructs (representation preservation via CKA/probing, selection gain vs. step gain, compute-normalized efficiency). It demonstrates excellent precision in specifying hardware constraints, random seeds, and ablation variables, and maintains consistency by adhering to the inference-only constraint throughout.

Feedback: Despite its thoroughness, the protocol exhibits three key limitations that temper its rigorousness: First, with only five DLM checkpoints, the statistical modeling (Spearman ρ, OLS regression) is severely underpowered; the bootstrap CIs quantify uncertainty but cannot overcome the fundamental lack of generalizability, making the primary hypothesis test descriptive rather than confirmatory. Second, the representation preservation is measured against a general-domain corpus (WikiText/C4) while the quality gains are assessed on specialized benchmarks (code, math, commonsense); the generation-domain probing ablation is a partial remedy but remains secondary. Third, the unified quality score assumes equal importance across fundamentally different task distributions, which may mask architecture-specific strengths. Additionally, the gain-ratio η_i conflates two different computation budgets (reranking K=8 candidates vs. 112 extra denoising steps), requiring careful interpretation. The method would be strengthened by treating the n=5 limitation as a constraint on inference rather than a minor caveat, and by reporting prompt-level rather than model-level analyses to increase statistical leverage.

Rating (1-5): 4
