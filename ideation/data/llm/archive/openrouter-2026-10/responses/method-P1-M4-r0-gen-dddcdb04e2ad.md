**Rationale:**  
The two previously proposed methods quantify representation preservation through static similarity measures (CKA, SVCCA, linear probes) or via an alignment‑efficiency metric derived from layer‑wise hidden‑state curves. While informative, these approaches assess *representational geometry* independently of the model’s *generative behavior* during denoising.  

Our method directly measures how closely the DLM’s **token‑level predictions** at each denoising step match the token distribution of its autoregressive backbone, using the forward KL divergence between the two distributions. This yields a **dynamic, process‑aligned deficit (TD‑AD)** that captures:  

1. **Step‑wise alignment** – whether the model’s predictions become AR‑like early or late in the denoising trajectory.  
2. **Cumulative misalignment** (AUC) – the total amount of “AR‑knowledge” that must be recovered through additional denoising steps.  
3. **Initial and asymptotic divergence** – diagnostic extremes that help distinguish cases where the model starts far from the AR prior versus cases where it never fully converges.  

Because the KL divergence is computed from the logits that the diffusion model already emits during sampling, the measurement adds negligible overhead and can be collected concurrently with the generation required for QET evaluation.  

If a DLM preserves AR representations strongly, its token distribution will be close to the AR prior even after few denoising steps, resulting in a low TD‑AD AUC. In that regime, we expect **diminishing returns from extra denoising steps** (small ΔQET_denoise) and **greater relative benefit from sampling more candidates and reranking** with a lightweight AR scorer (large ΔQET_sample). Conversely, weak preservation (high TD‑AD AUC) should imply that additional denoising steps are needed to reduce the token‑distribution mismatch, yielding larger ΔQET_denoise and smaller ΔQET_sample.  

By correlating TD‑AD (and its components) with the marginal QET gains across multiple model families and step budgets, we directly test the hypothesis that **the extent of AR representation preservation governs the optimal allocation of inference compute between denoising and selection**. The mixed‑effects and bootstrap analyses provide rigorous statistical inference while accounting for prompt difficulty and model‑specific random effects.  

Finally, the derived decision rule (threshold on TD‑AD) translates the finding into a practical, inference‑time guideline: practitioners can cheaply compute the TD‑AD score (e.g., on a validation subset) and then allocate their compute budget accordingly, without requiring any additional training or architectural changes. This bridges the representation‑alignment literature (REPR‑ALIGN, PreDiff‑LM) with inference‑time efficiency work (Jacobi Forcing, TESS 2) through a **behavior‑centric, lightweight diagnostic** that is fundamentally different from the static similarity‑based approaches already proposed.
