Review:
The method presents a well-structured, computationally feasible pipeline that addresses a genuinely interesting research question. Its core contribution lies in reframing the accuracy-headroom problem: rather than treating DLM quality as a function of denoising budget alone, it proposes that the *intrinsic fidelity* of AR-representation preservation governs how effectively a cheap post-hoc AR scorer can recover that headroom. This bridging of representation-alignment research (REPR-ALIGN, PreDiff-LM) with pure inference-time trade-off methods (Jacobi Forcing, TESS 2) is a meaningful conceptual advance. The factorial experimental design cleanly isolates reranking gain from denoising-step gain, and the efficiency ratio provides a practical decision metric. However, the *methodological* innovativeness is tempered by the fact that every component—CKA, linear probing, GPT-2-small scoring, nucleus sampling, mixed-effects modeling—is standard and well-established. No new technique, architecture, or training objective is introduced. The novelty resides primarily in the *question framing* and *experimental decomposition* rather than in the method itself. The ablation and robustness checks, while thorough, are routine. The study would benefit from a more sharply defined hypothesis about *why* preservation predicts reranking utility (e.g., relating to the geometry of the model's output distribution) rather than treating it as an empirical correlation to be discovered.

Feedback:
1. **Sharpen the theoretical mechanism**: The rationale mentions that prior work shows preservation improves training efficiency, but the leap to "preservation makes output distributions more amenable to AR scoring" needs a clearer mechanistic argument (e.g., does higher preservation imply the DLM's token logits are already closer to AR ordering, making reranking more effective?). Without this, the study risks being an empirical correlation hunt.
2. **Justify the choice of GPT-2-small as scorer**: Why this specific model size? A sensitivity analysis across scorer capacities (already planned in ablation) should be elevated to a core experimental condition, not an afterthought.
3. **Address the multiple-comparison problem**: With 5 DLMs × 4 step budgets × 2 reranking conditions × 4 benchmarks, the statistical model should include multiplicity correction or a pre-registered analysis plan.
4. **Consider a stronger baseline for "efficiency"**: Comparing against *optimal* denoising-step allocation (e.g., early-stopping at the sweet spot) rather than a fixed 128-step budget would make the efficiency claim more rigorous.
5. **The unified quality score (UQS)** lumps very different tasks (code generation, math reasoning, commonsense) into one metric; consider reporting task-specific results alongside UQS to avoid masking divergent patterns.

Rating (1-5): 3
