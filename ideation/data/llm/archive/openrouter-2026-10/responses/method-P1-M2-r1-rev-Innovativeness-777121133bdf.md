Review: The proposed AR-MHDS method presents a rigorous empirical framework for analyzing the quality–compute Pareto frontier of diffusion language models by casting candidate selection as a Metropolis–Hastings process guided by autoregressive likelihoods. While the methodological design is sound and directly addresses the identified gaps (accuracy headroom and compute normalization), the core algorithmic components—Metropolis–Hastings sampling, Gaussian noise proposals in latent space, and external AR scoring—are established statistical and machine learning techniques rather than novel contributions. The innovation is primarily in the specific instantiation and the systematic comparison of denoising-step investment versus proposal-count investment, rather than in introducing new theoretical principles or algorithmic paradigms.

Feedback: To elevate the innovativeness beyond a moderate level, consider whether the proposal distribution could be adapted to the local geometry of the data (e.g., using learned score functions or momentum-based transitions) rather than a fixed Gaussian random walk, which would distinguish it from standard MCMC. Additionally, clarify the precise distinction from existing “reward guidance” or “classifier guidance” mechanisms (e.g., TESS 2’s reward guidance or Jacobi Forcing’s distillation), as the current MH formalism appears to be an application of known inference techniques to a new model class rather than a conceptual advance. Finally, ensure that the AR scorer’s role as a Metropolis–Hastings acceptance criterion is theoretically justified for the discrete token space, as standard MH assumes detailed balance with respect to the target distribution, which may not hold exactly with a noisy diffusion proposal.
