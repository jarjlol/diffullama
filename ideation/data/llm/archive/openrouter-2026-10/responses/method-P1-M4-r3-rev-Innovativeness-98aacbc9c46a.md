Review:
The ASS-SG method proposes adaptive step-skipping in discrete diffusion language models using a frozen AR scorer's token-level confidence as a gating signal. The method is well-articulated, with rigorous FLOP accounting and a systematic experimental design. However, assessed strictly on innovativeness, the core idea—using a cheap model to gate expensive computations—is a well-established efficient-inference paradigm (early exiting, cascaded models, adaptive computation time). The specific instantiation (AR confidence → skip diffusion step) is a meaningful combination applied to a new context, but it does not introduce new model architectures, training objectives, or theoretical frameworks. The method is positioned as an inference-time optimization alternative to gradient-based guidance and static reranking, which is a legitimate contribution, yet the individual building blocks are largely known. The generalization checks and marginal-gain analyses strengthen the work but do not elevate the core mechanism itself to a higher novelty tier.

Feedback:
The method would benefit from a more honest assessment of where the novelty lies: it is a novel *application* and *combination* of existing efficient-inference principles to DLM decoding, not a fundamentally new technique. To strengthen the innovativeness argument, the authors should more explicitly differentiate their confidence-based skipping from prior work on adaptive computation in transformers and early-exit diffusion models, and ideally provide ablation evidence showing that the specific AR-confidence signal (vs. the diffusion model's own confidence or a random gate) is the critical factor. Additionally, the theoretical justification for why AR-model confidence correlates with diffusion-step necessity could be deepened. The experimental design is thorough, but the novelty claim in §"Why ASS-SG Is Different" overstates the case when the individual components are established—reframing this as a novel *inference strategy* rather than a novel *technique* would be more defensible and still compelling.

Rating (1-5): 3
