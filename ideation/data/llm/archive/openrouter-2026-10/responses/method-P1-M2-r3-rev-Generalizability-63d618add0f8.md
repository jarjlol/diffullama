Review:
The TADAG method demonstrates a thoughtful approach to the quality-compute trade-off in diffusion language models, introducing token-level adaptive freezing based on AR confidence scores. However, its generalizability is constrained by several factors: (1) the method is tightly coupled to discrete diffusion models adapted from AR checkpoints, limiting applicability to models trained from scratch (e.g., Dream 7B, LLaDA) or continuous diffusion/flow-matching approaches; (2) the reliance on AR likelihood as a confidence proxy assumes access to the original AR base model and may not transfer to models with different training objectives; (3) the FLOP estimation model is architecture-specific and may not generalize to MoE or hybrid architectures; (4) evaluation is restricted to English NLP benchmarks without cross-lingual or domain-specific validation; and (5) the binary freezing mechanism may require re-tuning for different model scales or architectures. While the generalizability checks section proposes testing on additional models and scorers, these remain within the DLM paradigm and do not demonstrate broad applicability across generative modeling approaches.

Feedback:
To improve generalizability, consider: (1) testing on DLMs trained from scratch without AR bases, (2) validating the confidence mechanism with alternative signals (e.g., diffusion model's own uncertainty estimates), (3) extending to cross-lingual or domain-specific benchmarks, (4) providing a more flexible FLOP model that accounts for architectural variations, and (5) exploring whether the token-freezing concept applies to continuous-time diffusion or flow-matching models. The current evaluation design is solid within its scope but would benefit from explicit discussion of failure modes and boundary conditions where the AR likelihood proxy breaks down.
