**Method: Moment‑Based Representation Alignment for Hybrid Reranking (MoRA‑HR)**  

**Rationale**  
The two previously proposed approaches quantify how closely a diffusion language model (DLM) mimics its autoregressive (AR) backbone by comparing static hidden‑state geometries (CKA/subspace angles or Procrustes similarity).  Those measures capture *representational similarity* but do not directly reflect how the *generative process* of the DLM deviates from the AR next‑token prediction dynamics that underlie the model’s linguistic knowledge.  To address the research question from a complementary angle, we propose to quantify preservation of AR representations by aligning the *first‑ and second‑order moments* (mean and covariance) of the layer‑wise hidden‑state distributions.  Moment matching is a training‑free, distribution‑level similarity metric that is sensitive to both the orientation and the scale of the representation space, yet it is computationally cheap because it reduces to a few vector‑and‑matrix operations per layer.  

We then use this moment‑based preservation score to *bias* a lightweight AR scorer during reranking: candidates that are both likely under the AR model **and** lie close to the AR moment distribution receive a higher hybrid score.  By comparing the quality gain obtained from this hybrid reranking to the gain obtained from simply increasing the denoising‑step budget, we can test whether (i) higher moment‑based preservation predicts larger reranking gains, (ii) this relationship differs between GPT‑2‑based and LLaMA‑based DLMs, and (iii) the hybrid reranking is more efficient (quality per FLOP) than extra denoising steps.  

Because the method relies only on forward passes through the released DLM checkpoints and the frozen AR base models, uses only publicly available probing data, and adds negligible overhead (a few milliseconds per candidate), it satisfies the inference‑only, compute, timeline, and team constraints.

### Step‑by‑Step Procedure  

| Step | Goal | Concrete Actions | Resources & Constraints |
|------|------|------------------|--------------------------|
| **0. Preparation** | Build a reproducible, inference‑only pipeline that respects hardware and timeline limits. | • Download the released DLM checkpoints (DiffuGPT‑S/M, DiffuLLaMA‑6.74B, Dream‑7B, LLaDA‑8B, DiffuCoder‑7B) and their AR base models (GPT‑2‑small/medium for GPT‑2‑based DLMs; LLaMA‑7B for LLaMA‑based DLMs). <br>• Tokenize a held‑out probing corpus (10 k sentences from WikiText‑103 + C4) with each model’s tokenizer; store token IDs. <br>• Assemble the benchmark suite (HumanEval, GSM8K, SIQA, WinoGrande) and implement a min‑max normalisation script that maps every raw metric to \([0,1]\) across all experimental conditions. <br>• Enable activation‑checkpointing and 8‑bit quantisation (via `bitsandbytes`) for the 6.74B DiffuLLaMA so it fits in the 96 GB GPU with batch‑size ≥ 4. <br>• Split work among the three GPU‑enabled team members: each member owns a subset of models/step‑budgets and writes results to a shared CSV/Parquet store. | One RTX 6000 Pro Blackwell (96 GB), 8‑bit quantisation + activation checkpointing, CPU‑only work for data preparation and evaluation. |
| **1. Moment‑Based AR‑Representation Preservation** | Obtain a scalar preservation score \(P_i\) that reflects how closely the DLM’s hidden‑state distribution matches that of its AR backbone, using only first‑ and second‑order moments. | **1.1 Feature extraction (frozen forward pass)** – For every sentence in the probing corpus, run the DLM **inference‑only** (no gradient) and collect the post‑attention hidden‑state tensor \(\mathbf{H}^{\text{DLM}}_\ell \in \mathbb{R}^{N\times D}\) for each Transformer layer \(\ell\). Do the same with the frozen AR base model (same tokenizer, same sequence length) to obtain \(\mathbf{H}^{\text{AR}}_\ell\). <br>**1.2 Moment estimation** – For each layer \(\ell\): <br> • Compute the mean vector \(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\text{DLM}}_\ell = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n}\mathbf{H}^{\text{DLM}}_{\ell,n}\) and \(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\text{AR}}_\ell\) analogously. <br> • Compute a *regularised* covariance matrix (diagonal + \(\epsilon I\) for numerical stability): \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{\text{DLM}}_\ell = \text{diag}\big(\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{n}(\mathbf{H}^{\text{DLM}}_{\ell,n}-\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\text{DLM}}_\ell)^2\big) + \epsilon\mathbf{I}\) (same for AR). <br>**1.3 Symmetric Gaussian KL divergence** – Assuming each layer’s hidden states are approximately Gaussian, the divergence between DLM and AR at layer \(\ell\) is: <br> \[
\text{KL}_\ell = \frac{1}{2}\Big[ \log\frac{|\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{\text{AR}}_\ell|}{|\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{\text{DLM}}_\ell|} - D + \text{tr}\big((\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{\text{AR}}_\ell)^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{\text{DLM}}_\ell\big) + (\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\text{AR}}_\ell-\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\text{DLM}}_\ell)^\top (\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{\text{AR}}_\ell)^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\text{AR}}_\ell-\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\text{DLM}}_\ell) \Big].
\] <br> Compute the same KL with DLM↔AR swapped and average to obtain a symmetric divergence \(\overline{\text{KL}}_\ell\). <br>**1.4 Aggregation** – Weight deeper layers more heavily (exponential decay with depth, \(w_\ell \propto \exp(\lambda \cdot \text{depth}_\ell)\)) and average: <br> \[
P_i = -\sum_{\ell} w_\ell \,\overline{\text{KL}}_\ell .
\] <br>Higher \(P_i\) indicates better preservation (lower divergence). | No gradient updates; only forward passes and cheap linear‑algebra (mean, diagonal covariance, small matrix inverses). All steps run on CPU or the same GPU used for generation. |
| **2. Multi‑candidate Generation** | Produce a diverse set of outputs for each prompt at several denoising‑step budgets. | • Select a prompt set covering the three benchmark families (e.g., 200 HumanEval prompts, 200 GSM8K questions, 200 SIQA/WinoGrande items). <br>• For each DLM \(i\) and each denoising‑step budget \(S \in \{16,32,64,128\}\): <br> - Sample **K = 8** candidates per prompt using nucleus sampling (top‑p = 0.9). <br> - Store the raw token strings. <br>• Use a fixed random seed across models to make candidate sets comparable. | Pure inference; batch size limited by GPU memory (≈ 4‑8 sequences per step). |
| **3. Lightweight, Training‑Free Hybrid Reranking** | Re‑order candidates using a score that combines AR likelihood and moment‑based preservation, then compute the quality gain from this selection. | **3.1 AR scorer likelihood** – Load **GPT‑2‑small (124 M)** as the scorer (frozen). For each candidate, compute the average negative log‑likelihood (or perplexity) under the scorer; lower score = higher likelihood. <br>**3.2 Candidate‑level moment deviation** – For each candidate, run the DLM **once** (the same forward pass used in Step 2) to obtain the hidden‑state tensors \(\mathbf{H}^{\text{DLM}}_\ell\). Run the **frozen AR base model** on the *same token sequence* (teacher‑forcing) to obtain \(\mathbf{H}^{\text{AR}}_\ell\). <br> - Compute the mean vectors \(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\text{DLM}}_\ell\) and \(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\text{AR}}_\ell\) across token positions (size \(D\)). <br> - Using the *global* diagonal covariances \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{\text{AR}}_\ell\) pre‑computed from the probing corpus (Step 1.2), calculate the Mahalanobis distance: <br> \[
d_\ell = \sqrt{(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\text{DLM}}_\ell-\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\text{AR}}_\ell)^\top (\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{\text{AR}}_\ell)^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\text{DLM}}_\ell-\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\text{AR}}_\ell)} .
\] <br> - Aggregate across layers with the same depth‑weights as in Step 1: \(D_{\text{cand}} = \sum_\ell w_\ell d_\ell\). <br>**3.3 Hybrid score** – Combine AR likelihood and moment deviation: <br> \[
\text{Score}_{\text{cand}} = \underbrace{-\text{NLL}_{\text{AR}}(\text{cand})}_{\text{higher is better}} \;-\; \beta \, D_{\text{cand}},
\] <br>where \(\beta \ge 0\) is a scalar hyper‑parameter (chosen on a small validation split, e.g., \(\beta = 0.5\)). Candidates are sorted descending by this score; the top‑1 after sorting is the *hybrid‑reranked* output. <br>**3.4 Cost measurement** – Record the forward‑pass time of the AR base model for one candidate (≈ 0.5 ms for GPT‑2‑small, ≈ 2 ms for 8‑bit LLaMA‑7B) and the negligible extra cost of computing means and Mahalanobis distance (sub‑millisecond). Total reranking cost per prompt is \(K \times t_{\text{AR}}\). | Only forward passes through the AR base model (no training). The AR model runs in 8‑bit quantisation with activation‑checkpointing to keep memory low. |
| **4. Quality Measurement** | Map raw outputs to a unified, comparable score. | • For each benchmark compute: <br> - HumanEval: pass@k for k = 1,10. <br> - GSM8K: exact‑match accuracy. <br> - SIQA & WinoGrande: accuracy (multiple‑choice). <br>• Normalise each raw score to \([0,1]\) across **all** model‑step‑budget‑reranking conditions (min‑max scaling). <br>• Define the **Unified Quality Score (UQS)** as the arithmetic mean of the four normalised components (HumanEval@1, HumanEval@10, GSM8K, (SIQA+WinoGrande)/2). <br>• Also compute the *oracle* UQS (the best candidate among the K generated) to quantify the accuracy headroom for each condition. | All metrics computed offline on CPU; no extra GPU load. |
| **5. Experimental Contrasts** | Isolate the two sources of improvement: (a) hybrid reranking vs. (b) more denoising steps. | For each DLM \(i\) and low step budget \(S_{\text{low}}=16\): <br> - **Hybrid reranking gain**: \(\Delta^{\text{hyb}}_{i,S_{\text{low}}} = \text{UQS}_{i,S_{\text{low}}}^{\text{hyb}} - \text{UQS}_{i,S_{\text{low}}}^{\text{top‑1}}\). <br> - **Denoising‑step gain** (no reranking): \(\Delta^{\text{step}}_{i} = \text{UQS}_{i,128}^{\text{top‑1}} - \text{UQS}_{i,16}^{\text{top‑1}}\). <br>• Compute the *relative efficiency* \(\eta_i = \frac{\Delta^{\text{hyb}}_{i,S_{\text{low}}}}{\Delta^{\text{step}}_{i}}\) (values > 1 indicate hybrid reranking yields more quality per unit of added compute). | Requires only the four step budgets; no additional model runs. |
| **6. Statistical Modelling** | Test whether preservation predicts hybrid reranking advantage and whether this differs by architecture or step budget. | Fit a **linear mixed‑effects model** (LME) with UQS as the dependent variable: <br>\[
\text{UQS}_{ijk\ell}= \beta_0 + \beta_1 P_i + \beta_2 S_j + \beta_3 R_{k} + \beta_4 (P_i \times S_j) + \beta_5 (P_i \times R_{k}) + \beta_6 (S_j \times R_{k}) + \beta_7 A_i + \beta_8 (P_i \times A_i) + u_i + \epsilon_{ijk\ell}
\] <br>where: <br>• \(P_i\) = preservation score from Step 1 (continuous). <br>• \(S_j\) = denoising‑step budget (log‑scaled continuous). <br>• \(R_k\) = reranking indicator (0 = top‑1, 1 = hybrid‑reranked). <br>• \(A_i\) = architecture dummy (0 = GPT‑2‑based, 1 = LLaMA‑based). <br>• \(u_i\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2_{\text{model}})\) captures model‑specific random intercepts. <br>Key hypotheses: <br> - **H1**: \(\beta_5 > 0\) (hybrid reranking yields larger gains when preservation is high). <br> - **H2**: \(\beta_8 \neq 0\) (the preservation‑reranking relationship differs between GPT‑2‑ and LLaMA‑based DLMs). <br> - **H3**: \(\beta_4 > 0\) (the benefit of extra steps grows with preservation). <br>• Additionally, compute Spearman’s \(\rho\) between \(P_i\) and the relative efficiency \(\eta_i\) to directly address the core research question. <br>Inference performed with `statsmodels` (REML) or `lme4` via R on CPU. | All modelling done on CPU; no extra GPU time required. |
| **7. Efficiency Accounting** | Translate quality gains into comparable compute cost. | • Measure wall‑clock time for a single denoising step (averaged over 100 runs) for each DLM (with activation checkpointing & 8‑bit quant). <br> \(C^{\text{step}}_i = t^{\text{step}}_i\). <br>• Compute **cost of hybrid reranking**: \(C^{\text{hyb}}_i = K \times t^{\text{AR}}_i\) (AR forward pass per candidate) + negligible moment‑distance cost. <br>• Define **efficiency ratio**: <br> \[
\epsilon_i = \frac{\Delta^{\text{hyb}}_{i,S_{\text{low}}} / C^{\text{hyb}}_i}{\Delta^{\text{step}}_{i} / (C^{\text{step}}_i \times (128-16))}.
\] <br>Values > 1 indicate hybrid reranking gives more quality per FLOP/second than adding denoising steps. | Timing measurements are cheap (< 5 min total). |
| **8. Ablation & Robustness Checks** | Verify that findings are not artefacts of specific choices. | • Vary the weighting \(\beta\) (0, 0.2, 0.5, 1.0) to test sensitivity of the hybrid score. <br>• Replace diagonal covariance with a *shrinkage* estimator (Ledoit‑Wolf) to assess impact of covariance modelling. <br>• Change nucleus sampling p (0.8, 0.95) and K (4, 16) to test sensitivity to candidate diversity. <br>• Run a *control* where the AR model is replaced by a random projection matrix (same dimensions) to ensure gains are not due to chance ordering. <br>• Test alternative scorers (DistilGPT‑2, MiniLM) to confirm the < 1 % FLOP claim. | All ablations reuse the same generated candidate pool; no extra generation needed. |
| **9. Reporting & Reproducibility** | Ensure transparent dissemination. | • Archive all scripts, random seeds, and environment specifications (Dockerfile or Conda yaml). <br>• Publish the extracted hidden‑state moments (means & diagonal covariances) and the final results table on an open repository (e.g., HuggingFace datasets + Zenodo). <br>• Provide a short “re‑run” notebook that reproduces the key figures (preservation vs. hybrid reranking gain, efficiency ratio). | Fits the ten‑week timeline: weeks 1‑2 for setup, weeks 3‑5 for generation & scoring, week 6 for metric computation, weeks 7‑8 for statistical analysis, weeks 9‑10 for write‑up and reproducibility packaging. |

### Why MoRA‑HR is Substantively Different  

| Aspect | Prior Method 1 (CKA/Linear Probe) | Prior Method 2 (Subspace/Procrustes) | **MoRA‑HR (Moment‑Based Hybrid Reranking)** |
|--------|-----------------------------------|--------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| **Similarity metric** | Linear CK A (second‑order statistic) or ridge‑regression \(R^2\) (predictive alignment) | Principal angles between subspaces (orientation only) | Symmetric Gaussian KL divergence between *means* and *covariances* (captures both location and scale) |
| **Use of similarity** | Only to compute a model‑level preservation score for later correlation analysis | Only to compute a model‑level preservation score for later correlation analysis | Directly incorporated into the reranking score (AR likelihood − β·moment deviation) |
| **Granularity** | Layer‑wise aggregation of a single similarity number per layer | Layer‑wise aggregation of subspace angles per layer | Layer‑wise aggregation of Mahalanobis distance of *means* (using a shared covariance) |
| **Computational profile** | Requires storing full hidden‑state matrices for CKA or solving ridge regression (still cheap) | Requires SVD per layer (moderately cheap) | Requires only means and diagonal covariances per layer (very cheap; can be updated online) |
| **Theoretical link to generation** | Indirect (static representational similarity) | Indirect (static subspace overlap) | Directly measures how close the *distribution* of a candidate’s hidden states is to the AR model’s distribution, which is a principled proxy for AR‑like next‑token behavior |
| **Novelty** | Well‑used in DLM‑AR comparison literature | Recently proposed for representation alignment | First to combine moment‑based preservation with AR likelihood in a hybrid, training‑free reranking scheme tailored to the efficiency‑vs‑quality trade‑off question |

By focusing on *distributional moments* rather than pure geometric alignment, MoRA‑HR offers a fresh perspective on the preservation‑efficiency relationship, remains fully inference‑only, fits within the stipulated compute and timeline budgets, and yields a directly actionable reranking strategy that can be compared head‑to‑head with additional denoising steps.
