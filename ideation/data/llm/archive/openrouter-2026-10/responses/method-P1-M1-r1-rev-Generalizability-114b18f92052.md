**Review:**

The method demonstrates a thoughtful design for generalizability within the DLM-AR paradigm. The protocol is explicitly model-agnostic — any DLM with an associated AR base can be plugged in — and the preservation metrics (CKA, linear probing, SVCCA, Procrustes) and scorer families (GPT-2, LLaMA) are described as interchangeable components. The ablation studies (scorer size, alternative metrics, diversity settings, probing corpus, normalization pools) further strengthen confidence that findings are not artefacts of specific choices. The fixed-effects statistical framework with bootstrap CIs is appropriately chosen given the small model count, and the cross-family scorer design mitigates circularity concerns.

However, generalizability is meaningfully constrained in several ways. Only five DLM checkpoints are tested (all GPT-2 or LLaMA-derived), leaving open whether the preservation→reranking relationship holds for fundamentally different DLM architectures (e.g., Mamba-based, flow-matching, or continuous-space diffusion models). The benchmark suite (HumanEval, GSM8K, SIQA, WinoGrande) spans code, math, and commonsense but excludes dialogue, creative writing, or domain-specific tasks where representation preservation may behave differently. The probing corpus (WikiText-103 + C4) may not capture semantic spaces relevant to all downstream domains. Only AR-based scorers are evaluated, so applicability to non-AR lightweight scorers (trained reward models, classifiers) remains unexplored. The step budget range (16–128) may miss regimes where different DLMs exhibit divergent behavior. Finally, the PCA-derived UQS weights, while data-driven, are computed on a limited validation split and the min-max normalization ties the unified metric to the observed experimental matrix, complicating comparison across studies.

**Feedback:**

To strengthen generalizability claims, the authors could: (1) explicitly discuss how the preservation-reranking relationship might differ for non-Transformer backbones or continuous diffusion models, (2) include at least one benchmark from a distinct domain (e.g., dialogue or scientific text), (3) test one non-AR scorer variant (e.g., a learned reward model) to probe scorer-family generalizability, and (4) report preservation scores on a generation-domain probing corpus as a primary analysis rather than only as an ablation. The PCA weighting scheme should be validated for stability — report how weights shift under different validation splits or with alternative dimensionality reduction methods.

**Rating (1-5): 3**

The method exhibits a reasonable level of adaptability with interchangeable components and ablation studies, suggesting applicability to related DLM contexts with modifications. However, the limited empirical scope (5 models, 4 benchmarks, AR-only scorers) prevents a stronger generalizability rating, as broad applicability across diverse DLM architectures, domains, and scorer types remains conjectural rather than demonstrated.
