**Method: Mutual‑Information‑Guided Representation Preservation (MIRP) for Inference‑Time Reranking vs. Denoising Trade‑offs**  

**Rationale**  
The core question is whether the extent to which a diffusion language model (DLM) retains the representational geometry of its autoregressive (AR) backbone predicts how much a lightweight, training‑free reranking step can recover the model’s accuracy headroom, and whether this recovery is more efficient than simply adding denoising steps. Existing proposals quantify representation preservation with static similarity measures (CKA, linear probing, subspace angles, Procrustes) and then rerank candidates using a plain AR‑scorer likelihood.  

We propose a fundamentally different angle: **(i)** estimate the *statistical dependence* between the DLM’s hidden‑state distribution and the AR model’s hidden‑state distribution using a non‑parametric, training‑free mutual‑information estimator based on k‑nearest‑neighbor (KNN) entropy; **(ii)** turn this estimate into a *representation‑preservation score* that varies with denoising step, allowing us to examine how preservation evolves as the diffusion process proceeds; **(iii)** use the same KNN‑based dependence measure at the *candidate level* to construct a *representation‑aware scoring function* that blends the AR scorer’s token‑level likelihood with a penalty for representation mismatch; and **(iv)** directly test whether the preservation score (and its step‑wise trajectory) predicts the relative efficiency of reranking versus extra denoising steps.  

Because the KNN mutual‑information estimator requires only forward passes and simple distance computations, it adds negligible overhead, respects the inference‑only constraint, and can be implemented on the same RTX 6000 Pro Blackwell used for generation. The method is generalizable to any DLM‑AR pair and yields a richer, dynamics‑aware view of representation preservation than static similarity metrics.

### Method Overview  

| Step | Goal | Concrete Actions | Resources & Constraints |
|------|------|------------------|--------------------------|
| **0. Preparation** | Build an inference‑only, reproducible pipeline that fits the hardware and timeline. | • Download released DLM checkpoints (DiffuGPT‑S/M, DiffuLLaMA‑6.74B, Dream‑7B, LLaDA‑8B, DiffuCoder‑7B) and the corresponding AR base models (GPT‑2‑small/medium for GPT‑2‑based DLMs; LLaMA‑7B for LLaMA‑based DLMs). <br>• Tokenize a held‑out probing corpus (10 k sentences from WikiText‑103 + C4) with each model’s tokenizer; store token IDs. <br>• Assemble the benchmark suite (HumanEval, GSM8K, SIQA, WinoGrande) and implement a min‑max normalisation script that maps every raw metric to \([0,1]\) across all experimental conditions. <br>• Enable activation‑checkpointing and 8‑bit quantisation (via `bitsandbytes`) for the 6.74B DiffuLLaMA so it fits in the 96 GB GPU with batch‑size ≥ 4. <br>• Split work among the three GPU‑enabled team members: each member owns a subset of models/step‑budgets and writes results to a shared CSV/Parquet store. | One RTX 6000 Pro Blackwell (96 GB), 8‑bit quantisation + activation checkpointing, CPU‑only work for data preparation and evaluation. |
| **1. Quantify AR‑representation preservation with KNN mutual information** | Obtain a scalar, step‑dependent preservation score \(P_i(S)\) that measures how much information the DLM’s hidden states share with the AR model’s hidden states after \(S\) denoising steps. | **1.1 Feature extraction (frozen forward pass)** – For every sentence in the probing corpus, run the DLM **inference‑only** with a given denoising‑step budget \(S\) and collect the post‑attention hidden‑state tensor \(\mathbf{H}^{\text{DLM}}_{\ell}(S) \in \mathbb{R}^{N\times D}\) for each Transformer layer \(\ell\). Do the same with the frozen AR base model (same tokenizer, same sequence length) to obtain \(\mathbf{H}^{\text{AR}}_{\ell}\). <br>**1.2 Flatten and layer‑wise pooling** – For each layer \(\ell\), concatenate the hidden states over all token positions and all sentences to obtain two matrices \(\mathbf{X}^{\text{DLM}}_{\ell}(S)\) and \(\mathbf{X}^{\text{AR}}_{\ell}\) of shape \((N_{\text{tok}}\times D)\). <br>**1.3 KNN‑based mutual information estimator** – Using the Kozachenko‑Leonenko / Kraskov‑Stögbauer‑Grassberger (KSG) estimator (k = 5) compute the mutual information \(I\big(\mathbf{X}^{\text{DLM}}_{\ell}(S); \mathbf{X}^{\text{AR}}_{\ell}\big)\). The estimator relies only on pairwise distances (no training). <br>**1.4 Aggregation across layers** – Weight deeper layers more (exponential decay with depth) and average: \[
P_i(S)=\sum_{\ell} w_\ell \, \frac{I\big(\mathbf{X}^{\text{DLM}}_{\ell}(S); \mathbf{X}^{\text{AR}}_{\ell}\big)}{\log\!\big(\min\{N_{\text{tok}}^{\text{DLM}},N_{\text{tok}}^{\text{AR}}\}\big)}\,
\] where the denominator normalises the MI to \([0,1]\). The result is a *representation‑preservation score* for model \(i\) at step budget \(S\). <br>**1.5 Step‑wise trajectory** – Repeat 1.1‑1.4 for each \(S\in\{16,32,64,128\}\) to obtain a preservation curve \(P_i(S)\). | No gradient updates; only forward passes and distance‑based MI estimation (implemented with efficient KD‑tree or FAISS approximate NN search on CPU). |
| **2. Multi‑candidate generation** | Produce a diverse set of outputs for each prompt at several denoising‑step budgets. | • Select a prompt set covering the three benchmark families (e.g., 200 HumanEval prompts, 200 GSM8K questions, 200 SIQA/WinoGrande items). <br>• For each DLM \(i\) and each denoising‑step budget \(S \in \{16,32,64,128\}\): <br> - Sample **K = 8** candidates per prompt using nucleus sampling (top‑p = 0.9). <br> - Store the raw token strings and, optionally, the hidden‑state tensors \(\mathbf{H}^{\text{DLM}}_{\ell}(S)\) for each candidate (needed for Step 3). <br>• Use a fixed random seed across models to make candidate sets comparable. | Pure inference; batch size limited by GPU memory (≈ 4‑8 sequences per step). |
| **3. Representation‑aware, training‑free reranking** | Re‑order candidates using a score that blends AR‑scorer likelihood with a penalty for representation mismatch, both computed without any training. | **3.1 AR‑scorer likelihood** – Load a tiny AR scorer (GPT‑2‑small, 124 M). For each candidate, compute the average negative log‑likelihood (NLL) under the scorer (teacher‑forcing). Lower NLL = higher likelihood. <br>**3.2 Candidate‑level representation mismatch** – For each candidate, we already have its DLM hidden states \(\mathbf{H}^{\text{DLM}}_{\ell}(S)\) from Step 2. Run the frozen AR base model on the same candidate token sequence (teacher‑forcing) to obtain \(\mathbf{H}^{\text{AR}}_{\ell}\). <br> - Flatten and pool per layer as in Step 1.2 to obtain vectors \(\mathbf{x}^{\text{DLM}}_{\ell}\) and \(\mathbf{x}^{\text{AR}}_{\ell}\). <br> - Compute the KSG mutual information \(I\big(\mathbf{x}^{\text{DLM}}_{\ell}; \mathbf{x}^{\text{AR}}_{\ell}\big)\) and convert to a *distance*‑like term: \[
D_{\ell}= \log\!\big(\min\{|\mathbf{x}^{\text{DLM}}_{\ell}|,|\mathbf{x}^{\text{AR}}_{\ell}|\}\big) - I\big(\mathbf{x}^{\text{DLM}}_{\ell}; \mathbf{x}^{\text{AR}}_{\ell}\big)
\] (larger \(D_{\ell}\) ⇒ worse representation match). <br> - Aggregate across layers with the same depth‑weights \(w_\ell\) used in Step 1.4 to get a scalar mismatch \(D^{\text{cand}} = \sum_\ell w_\ell D_{\ell}\). <br>**3.3 Combined scoring function** – Define a representation‑aware score: \[
\text{Score}^{\text{cand}} = -\text{NLL}_{\text{scorer}} \;-\; \lambda \, D^{\text{cand}}
\] where \(\lambda\) is a *global* temperature set to the median preservation score across all models and step budgets (i.e., \(\lambda = \text{median}\{P_i(S)\}\)). This ensures that when preservation is high (large \(P\)), the penalty for mismatch is relatively small, reflecting that the DLM already trusts its own representations; when preservation is low, the penalty grows, encouraging the reranker to favor candidates that are both likely under the AR scorer and close to the AR representation. <br>**3.4 Ranking** – Sort the K candidates in descending order of \(\text{Score}^{\text{cand}}\); the top‑1 after this sort is the *representation‑aware reranked* output. <br>**3.5 Cost measurement** – Record the forward‑pass time of the AR scorer (≈ 0.3 ms for GPT‑2‑small) and the AR base model (≈ 2 ms for LLaMA‑7B 8‑bit). The total reranking cost per prompt is \(K \times (t_{\text{scorer}} + t_{\text{AR}})\). | Only forward passes through the tiny AR scorer and the frozen AR base model; no gradient steps. The KSG MI estimator uses pre‑computed distance matrices and runs on CPU (< 50 ms per candidate). |
| **4. Quality measurement** | Map raw outputs to a unified, comparable metric. | • For each benchmark compute: <br> - HumanEval: pass@k for k = 1,10. <br> - GSM8K: exact‑match accuracy. <br> - SIQA & WinoGrande: accuracy (multiple‑choice). <br>• Normalise each raw score to \([0,1]\) across **all** model‑step‑budget‑reranking conditions (min‑max scaling). <br>• Define the **Unified Quality Score (UQS)** as the arithmetic mean of the four normalised components (HumanEval@1, HumanEval@10, GSM8K, (SIQA+WinoGrande)/2). <br>• Also compute the *oracle* UQS (the best candidate among the K generated) to quantify the accuracy headroom for each condition. | All metrics computed offline on CPU; no extra GPU load. |
| **5. Experimental contrasts** | Isolate the two sources of improvement: (a) representation‑aware reranking vs. (b) more denoising steps. | For each DLM \(i\) and low step budget \(S_{\text{low}}=16\): <br> - **Reranking gain**: \(\Delta^{\text{rank}}_{i,S_{\text{low}}} = \text{UQS}_{i,S_{\text{low}}}^{\text{ranked}} - \text{UQS}_{i,S_{\text{low}}}^{\text{top‑1}}\). <br> - **Denoising‑step gain** (no reranking): \(\Delta^{\text{step}}_{i} = \text{UQS}_{i,128}^{\text{top‑1}} - \text{UQS}_{i,16}^{\text{top‑1}}\). <br>• Compute the *relative efficiency* \(\eta_i = \frac{\Delta^{\text{rank}}_{i,S_{\text{low}}}}{\Delta^{\text{step}}_{i}}\) (values > 1 indicate reranking yields more quality per unit of added compute). | Requires only the four step budgets; no additional model runs. |
| **6. Statistical modelling** | Test whether preservation predicts reranking advantage and whether this differs by architecture or step budget. | Fit a **linear mixed‑effects model** (LME) with UQS as the dependent variable: <br>\[
\text{UQS}_{ijk\ell}= \beta_0 + \beta_1 \overline{P}_i + \beta_2 S_j + \beta_3 R_{k} + \beta_4 (\overline{P}_i \times S_j) + \beta_5 (\overline{P}_i \times R_{k}) + \beta_6 (S_j \times R_{k}) + \beta_7 A_i + \beta_8 (\overline{P}_i \times A_i) + u_i + \epsilon_{ijk\ell}
\] where: <br>• \(\overline{P}_i\) = average preservation score across the four step budgets for model \(i\) (continuous). <br>• \(S_j\) = denoising‑step budget (log‑scaled continuous). <br>• \(R_k\) = reranking indicator (0 = top‑1, 1 = representation‑aware reranked). <br>• \(A_i\) = architecture dummy (0 = GPT‑2‑based, 1 = LLaMA‑based). <br>• \(u_i\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2_{\text{model}})\) captures model‑specific random intercepts. <br>Key hypotheses: <br> - **H1**: \(\beta_5 > 0\) (representation‑aware reranking yields larger gains when preservation is high). <br> - **H2**: \(\beta_8 \neq 0\) (the preservation‑reranking relationship differs between GPT‑2‑ and LLaMA‑based DLMs). <br> - **H3**: \(\beta_4 > 0\) (the benefit of extra steps grows with preservation). <br>• Additionally, compute Spearman’s \(\rho\) between the *step‑wise* preservation slope \(\frac{dP_i}{dS}\) (estimated by a simple linear fit on the four points) and the relative efficiency \(\eta_i\) to directly address the core research question. <br>Inference performed with `statsmodels` (REML) or `lme4`; report likelihood‑ratio tests, 95 % CIs, and effect sizes. | All modelling performed on CPU (e.g., `statsmodels` or `lme4` via R). |
| **7. Efficiency accounting** | Translate quality gains into comparable compute cost. | • Measure wall‑clock time for a single denoising step (averaged over 100 runs) for each DLM (with activation checkpointing & 8‑bit quant). <br>• Compute **cost per step**: \(C^{\text{step}}_i = t^{\text{step}}_i\). <br>• Compute **cost of reranking**: \(C^{\text{rank}}_i = K \times (t^{\text{scorer}}_i + t^{\text{AR}}_i)\). <br>• Define **efficiency ratio**: \[
\epsilon_i = \frac{\Delta^{\text{rank}}_{i,S_{\text{low}}} / C^{\text{rank}}_i}{\Delta^{\text{step}}_{i} / (C^{\text{step}}_i \times (128-16))}
\] Values > 1 indicate reranking gives more quality per FLOP/second than adding denoising steps. | Timing measurements are cheap (< 5 min total). |
| **8. Ablation & robustness checks** | Verify that findings are not artefacts of specific choices. | • Vary the KSG neighbour \(k\) (3, 5, 10) to test sensitivity of the MI estimator. <br>• Replace the KSG MI with a **maximum mean discrepancy (MMD)** estimator (Gaussian kernel, linear‑time via random Fourier features) as an alternative preservation metric. <br>• Change nucleus sampling p (0.8, 0.95) and K (4, 16) to test sensitivity to candidate diversity. <br>• Run a *control* where the representation‑mismatch term \(D^{\text{cand}}\) is replaced by a random permutation of the same values (ensuring gains are not due to chance ordering). <br>• Test alternative tiny scorers (DistilGPT‑2, MiniLM) to confirm the < 1 % FLOP claim. | All ablations reuse the same generated candidate pool; no extra generation needed. |
| **9. Reporting & reproducibility** | Ensure transparent dissemination. | • Archive all scripts, random seeds, and environment specifications (Dockerfile or Conda yaml). <br>• Publish the extracted hidden‑state features (optional) and the final results table on an open repository (e.g., HuggingFace datasets + Zenodo). <br>• Provide a short “re‑run” notebook that reproduces the key figures (preservation vs. reranking gain, efficiency ratio). | Fits the ten‑week timeline: weeks 1‑2 for setup, weeks 3‑5 for generation & scoring, week 6 for metric computation, weeks 7‑8 for statistical analysis, weeks 9‑10 for write‑up and reproducibility packaging. |

### Why This Method Is Substantively Different  

| Aspect | Prior Proposals (1‑2 & listed 3‑7) | Proposed MIRP Method |
|--------|-----------------------------------|----------------------|
| **Preservation metric** | Static similarity (CKA, linear probing, subspace angles, Procrustes) computed once per model. | **Training‑free KNN‑based mutual information** that yields a *step‑dependent* preservation score \(P_i(S)\) and captures *statistical dependence* rather than mere geometric alignment. |
| **Use of preservation in reranking** | Either not used (plain AR‑scorer likelihood) or used to compute a candidate‑level similarity (Procrustes) that is directly maximised. | **Combination of AR‑scorer likelihood and a representation‑mismatch penalty** derived from the same KSG MI estimator, with a globally set trade‑off \(\lambda\) that adapts to the overall preservation level. |
| **Dynamic perspective** | No explicit modelling of how preservation changes with denoising steps. | **Explicit preservation trajectory** \(P_i(S)\) across 16, 32, 64, 128 steps, enabling a direct test of whether the *slope* of preservation predicts the relative efficiency of reranking vs. extra steps. |
| **Statistical estimator** | Relies on kernel alignment or linear probes that require solving a ridge regression (still cheap) but are deterministic similarity measures. | Uses a **non‑parametric entropy‑based estimator** (Kozachenko‑Leonenko/KSG) that estimates mutual information from distances, offering a different theoretical foundation (information‑theoretic vs. geometric). |
| **Interpretability** | Preservation interpreted as “how close the subspaces are”. | Preservation interpreted as “how much information about the AR representation is retained in the DLM’s hidden states”, directly linking to the notion of *representation headroom*. |
| **Computational profile** | Requires only forward passes and cheap linear algebra. | Requires forward passes plus distance‑based NN searches (implementable with FAISS or KD‑tree on CPU); still well within the inference‑only budget and adds < 5 % overhead. |
| **Generalisability** | Tied to specific similarity measures that may not capture higher‑order dependencies. | Mutual information captures any statistical dependence (linear, nonlinear, higher‑order), making the method applicable to a broader set of DLM‑AR pairs and potentially to other modalities (e.g., vision‑language diffusion models). |

By grounding the analysis in an information‑theoretic, step‑sensitive measure of representation preservation and by integrating that measure directly into the reranking score, MIRP offers a fresh, rigorous, and generalizable route to answer the core research question: **Does the degree to which a DLM preserves its AR backbone’s representations determine how much a lightweight, training‑free reranking step can recover accuracy headroom, and is this recovery more efficient than simply increasing the denoising‑step budget?**
