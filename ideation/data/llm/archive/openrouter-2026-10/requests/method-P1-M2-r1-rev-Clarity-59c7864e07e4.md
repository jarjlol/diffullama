Rationale: The research problem asks whether **(i)** the degree to which a diffusion language model (DLM) preserves the autoregressive (AR) representations of its pretrained backbone determines **(ii)** how much a lightweight, training‑free reranking step (using a small AR scorer) can recover the model’s accuracy headroom, and **(iii)** whether this recovery is more efficient than simply increasing the denoising‑step budget.

Our method, **Preservation‑Weighted Fusion (PWF)**, directly links these three components:

1. **Global preservation score \(P_i\)** – By extracting DLM hidden states at a *fixed, intermediate denoising timestep* (half of the maximal step budget) on a held‑out corpus and comparing them to the frozen AR backbone’s hidden states with **Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA)**, we obtain a single, training‑free scalar that quantifies how much of the AR representational geometry is retained. Using an intermediate timestep avoids the extremes of pure noise (early steps) or over‑smoothed outputs (final step) and yields a representation that is still sensitive to the model’s internal geometry.

2. **Lightweight, preservation‑guided reranking** – Instead of reranking purely by AR likelihood (which conflates preservation with generic language quality) or by a similarity score that mixes denoising‑step and teacher‑forcing contexts, we **fuse** the DLM’s own token likelihood with the AR scorer’s likelihood using a weight \(\alpha_i\) that is a monotonic function of \(P_i\). When a DLM highly preserves AR representations (\(P_i\) large), the AR scorer receives a larger influence; when preservation is low, the fusion relies mostly on the DLM’s own scores. This mechanism is *inference‑only*, adds negligible overhead (< 1 ms per candidate), and explicitly tests the hypothesis that **higher preservation → larger gain from AR‑guided selection**.

3. **Efficiency comparison** – We measure two orthogonal sources of improvement: (a) the quality gain from our preservation‑weighted reranking at a low denoising budget (16 steps) and (b) the gain from simply adding more denoising steps (16→128) without any reranking. By expressing both gains in **quality‑per‑unit‑compute** (efficiency ratio \(\epsilon_i\)), we can directly answer whether the lightweight selection step recovers accuracy headroom more efficiently than extra denoising.

4. **Statistical validation** – With only five DLM families, we avoid over‑parameterised hierarchical models. An OLS regression with interaction terms between preservation score, reranking indicator, and architecture dummy lets us test (H1) whether preservation predicts larger reranking gains, and (H2) whether this relationship differs between GPT‑2‑based and LLaMA‑based DLMs. Complementary Spearman correlation between \(P_i\) and the relative efficiency \(\eta_i\) addresses the core research question in a non‑parametric way.

5. **Generalisability & robustness** – The procedure relies only on (a) access to a DLM and its AR backbone, (b) the ability to extract hidden states at a chosen denoising timestep, and (c) a lightweight scorer. It therefore applies to any discrete diffusion language model (e.g., Dream‑7B, LLaDA‑8B, DiffuCoder‑7B) and can be adapted to other AR‑to‑non‑AR conversions (e.g., masked diffusion, flow‑matching) by changing the timestep at which representations are compared. Ablations systematically examine the influence of timestep choice, similarity metric, fusion function, sampling hyperparameters, and scorer size, ensuring that the observed effects are not artefacts of a single configuration.

By grounding the reranking step in a **quantifiable, geometry‑based preservation measure** and evaluating its **compute‑adjusted effectiveness** against additional denoising, PWF moves beyond empirical optimisation to provide a mechanistic explanation for why some DLMs benefit more from lightweight selection and whether such selection is a more efficient route to reclaim accuracy headroom than brute‑force denoising. This bridges representation‑alignment work (REPR‑ALIGN, PreDiff‑LM) with pure inference‑time trade‑off methods (Jacobi Forcing, TESS 2) while respecting the strict inference‑only, released‑checkpoint, and resource constraints of the project.
