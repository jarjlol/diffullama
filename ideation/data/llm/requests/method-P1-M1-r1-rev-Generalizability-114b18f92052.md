The linear‑probe score is retained as a complementary, interpretable check. Layer‑wise aggregation uses an explicit exponential decay (\(\lambda=0.5\)) that gives deeper semantic layers higher influence, a choice justified by prior work showing that later layers encode more task‑relevant information and is varied in ablation.

2. **Isolating the two improvement levers** – By generating multiple candidates at four denoising‑step budgets and applying a tiny AR scorer, we obtain (a) the *selection gain* (reranking over a fixed candidate set) and (b) the *denoising‑step gain* (more steps, no reranking). The gain‑ratio \(\eta_i\) and the compute‑normalised ratio \(\epsilon_i\) let us compare quality improvement per unit of added compute, directly addressing the efficiency clause of the research question.

3. **Addressing scorer‑model family confounding** – Using both GPT‑2‑small and a LLaMA‑based scorer (or distilled version) lets us test whether the preservation‑gain relationship holds when the scorer architecture differs from the DLM’s backbone. This mitigates the circularity highlighted in the reviews and enables a cross‑family analysis.

4. **Robust statistical inference with limited models** – With only five DLM checkpoints, a random‑effects term would confound with the preservation score. We therefore employ a fixed‑effects linear model with the preservation score centered, and obtain bias‑corrected bootstrap confidence intervals (10 000 resamples) for all coefficients. A Bayesian regression with weakly informative priors serves as a sensitivity check. This approach controls Type I error while still testing the key interactions (preservation × step, preservation × reranking, preservation × architecture).

5. **Unified yet transparent quality metric** – Rather than arbitrarily averaging four heterogeneous benchmarks, we derive data‑driven weights from a PCA on a validation split, ensuring the UQS reflects the dominant shared variance. We also report per‑benchmark normalised scores and the oracle UQS, so readers can inspect domain‑specific patterns.

6. **Efficiency accounting made explicit** – We provide both absolute gains and the two efficiency ratios (\(\eta_i\) and \(\epsilon_i\)), clearly defining their denominators (extra denoising steps vs. scorer compute). This removes ambiguity about what “more efficient than increasing the denoising‑step budget” means and allows practitioners to decide where to allocate a fixed inference budget.

7. **Comprehensive ablation and robustness** – We vary scorer size, alternative preservation metrics, candidate‑diversity settings, normalization pools, and even add a generation‑domain probing set to verify that preservation scores are not artefacts of the probing corpus. A random‑scorer control confirms that gains are not due to chance ordering.

8. **Adherence to inference‑only constraint** – All steps rely solely on forward passes of released checkpoints; the only learned component (ridge regression) is solved analytically on CPU and does not modify any model weights. No continual pre‑training, fine‑tuning, or adaptation is performed, satisfying the team decision D‑2026‑09‑21‑a.

9. **Feasibility within the given resources** – Activation checkpointing and 8‑bit quantisation enable the 6.74B DiffuLLaMA to run on a single 96 GB GPU with batch size ≥4. Generation is capped at 128 steps, a range where the target paper shows informative marginal returns. Parallelising across three GPU‑enabled team members keeps wall‑clock time well under the ten‑week limit, while CPU‑heavy tasks (feature extraction, probing, evaluation, statistical modelling) are distributed to the remaining four members.

10. **Generalisability** – The protocol is model‑agnostic: any DLM released with an associated AR base can be plugged in. The preservation metrics (CKA, linear probing, SVCCA, Procrustes) and the lightweight scorers (GPT‑2‑family, LLaMA‑family) are interchangeable. The unified quality metric can be recomputed with alternative benchmarks or weighted schemes, and the statistical framework accommodates additional architectures (e.g., Mamba‑based diffusion) or alternative lightweight scorers without redesign.

By tightly integrating representation‑alignment analysis with a rigorous, efficiency‑focused comparison of lightweight selection versus extra denoising, this method moves beyond empirical tuning to provide a mechanistic explanation for why some DLMs benefit more from reranking than others. It bridges the representation‑alignment literature (REPR‑ALIGN, PreDiff‑LM) with pure inference‑time trade‑off work (Jacobi Forcing, TESS 2), offering actionable insight for future DLM design and deployment.
