Review: The method proposes an Adaptive Candidate Generation via AR-Guided Early Acceptance (ACE), utilizing an AR scorer as a dynamic gate to halt candidate generation once a quality threshold is met, rather than pre-specifying a fixed number of candidates.

Feedback: While the empirical design is rigorous and directly addresses the compute-normalization gap, the innovativeness is moderate rather than groundbreaking. The core mechanism—scoring a sample and accepting or rejecting it—is analogous to standard rejection sampling or early-exit strategies, not a fundamentally new mathematical framework. The novelty lies primarily in the specific application of a monotonically increasing threshold to adaptively cap the candidate count $N$ and the empirical comparison of this "generate-until-accepted" paradigm against static grids. It does not alter the diffusion dynamics or training objectives (unlike Jacobi Forcing or gradient guidance), making it an inference-time heuristic rather than a methodological breakthrough. To strengthen the claim of innovativeness, the authors could explicitly link this thresholding strategy to optimal stopping theory or demonstrate that the adaptive $N_{eff}$ uncovers a non-linear regime in the quality-compute trade-off that static grids cannot capture.

Rating (1-5): 3
