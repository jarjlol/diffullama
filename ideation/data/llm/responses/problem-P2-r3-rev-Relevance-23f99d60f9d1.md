Feedback: The problem is a strong empirical characterization study that fills a genuine gap—no prior work systematically investigates how noise schedule choice shapes the quality-efficiency frontier across model families and scales in an inference-only manner. However, two concerns temper the assessment: (1) The "learned schedule" component introduces a minor training step that slightly contradicts the claimed inference-only framing, and its learning mechanism (training an MLP on a held-out slice using only forward passes) is underspecified. (2) The problem's breadth—spanning quality, efficiency, infilling, instruction-following, statistical rigor, and architectural generality—risks diluting findings; a tighter focus on the most impactful schedule-family interactions would strengthen the contribution. Additionally, the motivation connecting noise schedule analysis to "alternative routes" like representation alignment (#5) conflates inference-time optimizations with training-time modifications, making the comparison somewhat misleading. Despite these issues, the problem remains pertinent, well-grounded in existing literature, and offers actionable insights for practitioners deploying adapted DLMs.
