Review: The research problem is clearly formulated and addresses a genuine, underexplored question at the intersection of representation alignment and inference-time compute management for diffusion language models. The rationale is well-grounded in the existing literature, correctly identifying that (a) adapted DLMs leave accuracy headroom, (b) inference-time methods like Jacobi Forcing and TESS 2's reward guidance offer trade-offs but lack a unifying principle, and (c) the relationship between how well a DLM preserves its AR backbone's representations and how much it benefits from lightweight post-hoc selection has not been systematically studied. The experimental plan is methodologically sound, reproducible, and fits within the stated constraints.

Feedback: The problem's significance is strengthened by its practical implications: answering whether representation preservation predicts selection effectiveness would give practitioners a principled guide for allocating inference compute between denoising steps and candidate reranking. It also bridges two important threads—representation alignment (REPR-ALIGN, PreDiff-LM) and inference-time optimization (Jacobi Forcing, TESS 2)—in a novel way. However, the contribution is primarily diagnostic and empirical rather than introducing a new method or theoretical framework; it combines established techniques (CKA, reranking, benchmarking) to answer a specific question. The scope is also somewhat narrow, focusing on a single mechanism (representation preservation → selection gain) rather than broader questions about DLM capabilities or architectures. Additionally, the reliance on a small GPT-2 scorer (124M) as the "lightweight AR scorer" may limit the generality of conclusions, and the study's findings could potentially be null (i.e., no meaningful correlation), which, while informative, would temper the perceived significance.

Overall, the problem is well-defined, relevant, and addresses a real gap with practical value, but it falls short of being groundbreaking or transformative.

Rating (1-5): 4
