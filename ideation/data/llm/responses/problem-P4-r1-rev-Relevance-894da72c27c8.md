Review: The research problem is well-situated within the active and rapidly growing field of diffusion language modeling. It directly builds on the target paper's identification of the shift operation as a critical but under-examined component of AR-to-DLM adaptation, and it addresses a genuine gap in the literature: the behavior of this simple inference-only modification at scale and across architectures has never been systematically studied. The problem is clearly connected to the broader research context, as evidenced by the numerous related papers that all grapple with the challenges of adapting autoregressive checkpoints to diffusion-based generation. The experimental design is practical, leveraging publicly released checkpoints and a straightforward inference-only manipulation, making it feasible within the stated resource constraints. The cross-family and cross-scale comparison framework (GPT-2-based vs. LLaMA-based, 127M–7B) is a sensible approach to determine whether the shift operation is a universal lever or architecture-specific.

Feedback: The problem is clearly relevant and the rationale is sound, but there are a few areas to strengthen. First, the problem could benefit from a more explicit connection to how the shift operation relates to the theoretical insights from the target paper and related works (e.g., UNIFUSION's unified reverse-rate objective or REPR-ALIGN's representation alignment)—this would deepen the conceptual grounding. Second, while the problem is well-defined, it is relatively narrow in scope (studying a single hyperparameter), so the framing could emphasize more strongly why understanding this specific component is critical for the broader goal of making DLMs practical and deployable. Third, the quality-efficiency Pareto frontier analysis is a good idea, but the problem could also consider whether the shift interacts with other inference-time modifications (e.g., different denoising step budgets or sampling strategies), which would make the findings more actionable. Finally, the problem would benefit from a clearer statement of what constitutes a "notable gap" in the literature—while L2 is referenced, the broader context of how this gap fits within the landscape of related papers (e.g., Jacobi Forcing's focus on convergence speed, TESS 2's instruction tuning) could be made more explicit.
