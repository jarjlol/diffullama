Review: The research problem focuses on empirically evaluating the impact of a constant logit shift during the inference phase of diffusion language models (DLMs). While it addresses a noted limitation (L2) from the target paper regarding the under-examined behavior of the shift operation at scale, the problem itself is fundamentally an empirical ablation study rather than a theoretical or architectural innovation. It seeks to map an existing inference-time trick across different scales and families, which is a systematic and well-motivated extension, but does not introduce a novel paradigm, a new training objective, or a unique conceptual perspective on diffusion modeling. 

Feedback: The problem is well-defined and practically relevant, particularly given the significant accuracy drop (-11.9 to -15.2 points) noted when the shift is removed. However, the originality is constrained by the nature of the intervention: applying a constant shift to logits is a post-hoc, inference-only manipulation that does not alter the model's fundamental architecture or training dynamics. To elevate the originality, the study could move beyond merely sweeping static shift values and instead investigate *why* the shift behaves differently across architectures (e.g., tying it to attention patterns or positional embeddings), or explore dynamic/learned shift mechanisms that adapt per-token or per-step, rather than a uniform constant. As it stands, the problem offers valuable empirical insights but represents a moderate rather than groundbreaking contribution to the field.
