Review: The research problem is well-motivated by the target paper's observations about the hit-rate–accuracy gap and undertraining of adapted DLMs. It proposes a clear, feasible empirical study: systematically varying the number of diffusion-generated reasoning candidates and the choice of AR verifier across model families and scales, then characterizing the quality–efficiency Pareto frontier. The problem is carefully scoped, computationally feasible, and builds naturally on the target paper's released checkpoints.

Feedback: The core idea—generating multiple candidates from a diffusion model and reranking them with an autoregressive verifier—is not genuinely novel. Related Paper #7 (Diffuse Thinking) already proposes precisely this paradigm: using diffusion models as "thought proposers" and LLMs as evaluators for reasoning. The target paper itself hints at this strategy by noting that hit-rate substantially exceeds single-answer accuracy. What the proposed problem adds is a systematic empirical sweep (varying N, verifier size, model family, and scale) with formal quality–efficiency metrics, which is a valuable contribution but constitutes an empirical extension rather than a conceptual breakthrough. The framing as "how does X affect Y" is essentially an ablation study, not a new challenge or perspective. To strengthen originality, the problem could be sharpened by identifying a specific phenomenon or failure mode that no existing work explains—for instance, a surprising interaction between model scale and verifier choice that contradicts intuition, or a structural limitation of adapted DLMs that the candidate-selection strategy uniquely exposes. As stated, the problem is solid and well-defined but sits at the boundary between moderate and notably original.

Rating (1-5): 3
