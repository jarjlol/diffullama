Problem:  
How does the choice of masking strategy (e.g., independent token masking, span masking, prefix‑suffix masking, and block‑wise masking) affect the quality‑efficiency trade‑off of scaled diffusion language models adapted from autoregressive checkpoints, and does this relationship differ across model families (GPT‑2‑based vs. LLaMA‑based) and scales (127 M – 7 B)?

Rationale:  
The target paper shows that continual pre‑training of open‑source autoregressive (AR) checkpoints yields competitive diffusion language models (DLMs) such as DiffuGPT and DiffuLLaMA, and claims proficiency in fill‑in‑the‑middle (infilling) without prompt re‑ordering. However, several limitations highlighted in the paper motivate a deeper, inference‑only study of masking strategies:

1. **Narrow infilling evaluation (L8).** The paper evaluates infilling only on single‑line HumanEval with gold prefix and suffix supplied. This does not reflect real‑world infilling needs (e.g., whole‑function code generation, multi‑sentence text completion). By systematically varying how tokens are masked—random independent tokens, contiguous spans, prefix‑suffix segments, or block‑wise patterns as used in Dream 7B—we can assess whether the claimed infilling ability generalizes across realistic masking scenarios.

2. **Undertrained models (L7).** The adapted DLMs are reported to be undertrained relative to their AR counterparts, leaving a quality gap. Certain masking strategies may reduce the difficulty of the denoising task (e.g., masking fewer tokens or preserving more context) and thereby narrow this gap without additional training. Evaluating quality across strategies will reveal if a particular mask configuration mitigates the undertraining shortfall.

3. **Efficiency characterization (L9).** The original work reports wall‑clock latency without normalizing for compute. Different masking strategies change the effective number of tokens that must be predicted at each denoising step, altering the computational load. By measuring both generation quality (e.g., exact‑match, BLEU/CodeBLEU, MAUVE) and compute‑normalized efficiency (perplexity or error rate per GFLOP, approximated as model size × steps × effective token count × sequence length), we can obtain a more faithful quality‑efficiency frontier for each strategy.

4. **Architectural generality (L10).** The adaptation recipe was applied only to GPT‑2 and LLaMA families up to 7 B. Masking strategies interact with the model’s attention patterns (causal vs. bidirectional) and may benefit one family more than the other—for instance, span masking could align better with the bidirectional denoising objective of LLaMA‑based models, while independent token masking might suit GPT‑2‑based models. Testing across families and scales will uncover such interactions and inform future adaptation choices.

5. **Connection to alternative routes.** Related work such as “Enabling Autoregressive Models to Fill In Masked Tokens” (paper 4) shows that direct AR‑MLM hybrids can achieve strong infilling performance. By comparing diffusion‑based infilling under various masks to the performance reported for those hybrids, we can gauge how close adapted DLMs get to alternative approaches without retraining.

**Feasibility under the given constraints:**  
- **Inference‑only:** All experiments use the released checkpoints (DiffuGPT‑S/M, DiffuLLaMA‑6.74 B, LLaDA‑8B, Dream‑7B, DiffuCoder‑7B). No pre‑training, continual pre‑training, or AR‑to‑diffusion adaptation is required.  
- **Compute:** One NVIDIA RTX 6000 Pro Blackwell (96 GB) suffices for batched inference. We will evaluate a small, informative set of masking strategies (4) across a limited range of denoising steps (e.g., 16, 32, 64) and a fixed sequence length (e.g., 128 tokens). Approximate FLOPs are computed as model size × steps × (effective masked token count) × sequence length, enabling compute‑normalized metrics.  
- **Timeline:** Implementing a masking‑strategy harness, running sweeps, collecting automated metrics (exact‑match, BLEU/CodeBLEU, MAUVE, perplexity, GFLOPs), and analysis fits within ten weeks. Three GPU‑enabled members can parallelize runs over models and strategies; the remaining members handle CPU‑heavy tasks (metric computation, statistical analysis, write‑up).  
- **Coverage:** The listed checkpoints span the required scales and families (GPT‑2‑based: DiffuGPT‑S/M; LLaMA‑based: DiffuLLaMA, Dream‑7B, DiffuCoder‑7B; additional LLaDA‑8B provides a third LLaMA‑scale point). This ensures adequate representation for the family‑ and scale‑comparison.

**Significance:**  
Systematically quantifying how masking strategy reshapes the quality‑efficiency frontier of scaled DLMs directly addresses limitations L8 (infilling evaluation scope), L7 (undertrained models), and L9 (non‑compute‑normalized efficiency) by providing a controllable, training‑free lever to improve generation quality and alignment. It extends the adaptation paradigm introduced in the target paper, offering practitioners a simple knob to enhance infilling and general generation performance across model families and scales without costly retraining. Moreover, by revealing strategy‑dependent architectural sensitivities, the work can guide future DLM design (e.g., selecting masks that better match the model’s attention bias or the target task’s structure), thereby contributing to more effective diffusion‑based text generation.
