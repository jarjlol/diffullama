**Review:**

The research problem investigates how noise-schedule shape interacts with model-specific architectural inductive biases (rotary vs. absolute positional embeddings) in scaled diffusion language models adapted from autoregressive checkpoints, and proposes a hybrid schedule as a remedy for the removed attention-mask annealing.

**Feedback:**

**Strengths:**
- The specific intersection of noise-schedule shape × architectural inductive bias (positional encoding type) is not explicitly studied in any of the referenced works, giving the problem a genuinely novel framing. Dream 7B explores context-adaptive rescheduling, UNIFUSION examines uniform-noise objectives, and the target paper partially ablates schedules—but none systematically compare how schedule shape interacts with the rotary-vs-absolute embedding divide.
- The mechanistic hypothesis (rotary embeddings preserve relative position under noise, favoring gradual cosine schedules; absolute embeddings suffer positional drift, favoring low-noise early timesteps) provides a theoretically grounded rationale beyond a mere empirical sweep.
- The proposed hybrid schedule (linear early + cosine late) is a concrete, testable contribution with clear practical implications.

**Weaknesses:**
- Noise-schedule exploration in diffusion language models is **not** an underexplored topic: Dream 7B already introduces context-adaptive token-level noise rescheduling, UNIFUSION derives connections across corruption kernels and noise objectives, and dLLM standardizes schedule components. The problem risks being perceived as a refinement within an already-active subfield rather than a novel challenge.
- The hypothesis that noise schedules can "emulate" attention-mask annealing is conceptually appealing but somewhat intuitive—both mechanisms control the degree of bidirectional context during denoising. This connection, while useful, may not constitute a deeply novel insight.
- The problem is fundamentally inference-only, which constrains the scope of contribution. It is essentially a systematic empirical ablation with a theoretical framing, rather than a methodological or conceptual advance.
- The quality-efficiency trade-off analysis, while well-defined, echoes themes in Jacobi Forcing, MoE-Flow Matching, and other efficiency-focused works, potentially diluting the sense of novelty.
- The claim of "mechanistic understanding" may be overstated for an inference-only study; without probing representations or training dynamics, the mechanistic conclusions will remain correlational rather than causal.

**Overall Assessment:**
The problem is well-motivated, clearly defined, and addresses a gap that has not been extensively explored in the specific form presented. However, its components (schedule sensitivity, architectural comparison, efficiency analysis) each have precedents in the literature, and the contribution is best characterized as a rigorous, well-framed empirical investigation rather than a pioneering new direction. It offers incremental but valuable insights rather than transformative novelty.

**Rating (1-5): 3**

The mechanistic hypothesis adds intellectual value, but the inference-only scope and incremental nature of the contribution prevent it from reaching a higher originality tier.
