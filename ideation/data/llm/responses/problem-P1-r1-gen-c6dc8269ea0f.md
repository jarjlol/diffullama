**Problem:**  
How does lightweight candidate selection (e.g., reranking with a small autoregressive scorer) influence the quality‑efficiency trade‑off of scaled diffusion language models adapted from autoregressive checkpoints, and does this relationship vary across model families (GPT‑2‑based vs. LLaMA‑based) and denoising‑step budgets?

**Rationale:**  
The target paper shows that continual pre‑training of open‑source autoregressive (AR) models yields competitive diffusion language models (DLMs) such as DiffuGPT and DiffuLLaMA, yet its evaluation focuses on single‑answer accuracy at a fixed denoising budget and does not investigate whether the observed “accuracy headroom” (L6) can be recovered through inexpensive post‑hoc selection rather than solely by increasing denoising steps. Recent work (e.g., Jacobi Forcing, TESS 2) demonstrates that diffusion LMs can trade inference compute for quality, but it remains unclear whether allocating a modest amount of compute to a lightweight selector—such as a frozen GPT‑2‑small or LLaMA‑7B scorer—can close the gap more efficiently than simply running additional denoising steps.  

Addressing this question directly exploits the inference‑only constraint: we can generate multiple candidates per prompt at various step counts (e.g., 16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1000) using the released DLM checkpoints, then rerank those candidates with a tiny AR model that adds negligible FLOPs and memory overhead. By measuring quality (pass@k on HumanEval single‑line and whole‑function infilling, GSM8K reasoning accuracy, SIQA/WinoGrande commonsense scores) and efficiency (wall‑clock latency per token, estimated FLOPs = generation steps × model size + selection overhead, memory footprint), we can construct quality‑efficiency curves for each model and compare:  

1. **Selection vs. steps:** Whether, at a fixed compute budget, reranking yields higher quality than allocating the same budget to extra denoising steps.  
2. **Model‑family differences:** Whether GPT‑2‑based DLMs (DiffuGPT‑S/M) and LLaMA‑based DLMs (DiffuLLaMA, Dream‑7B, LLaDA‑8B, DiffuCoder‑7B) exhibit distinct selection‑step trade‑offs, revealing whether the architecture of the adapted AR backbone influences how much semantic structure is preserved in the candidate set.  
3. **Step‑budget dependence:** Whether the marginal benefit of selection diminishes at high step counts (where candidates already converge) or remains valuable even at low step budgets (e.g., ≤32 steps), thus informing practical deployment strategies for low‑latency settings.  

This study is feasible within ten weeks on a single RTX 6000 Pro Blackwell: all experiments involve only forward passes (generation + lightweight reranking), the largest checkpoint (6.74 B) fits comfortably in 96 GB VRAM at batch size 1, and the seven‑person team can parallelize model‑harness implementation, evaluation‑script development, latency measurement, and analysis.  

**Significance:**  
By providing the first systematic, compute‑normalized analysis of lightweight selection as an alternative to increasing denoising steps, the work will clarify whether the quality gains of scaled DLMs arise primarily from richer denoising trajectories or from better exploitation of the inherent diversity in their candidate sets. The results will offer concrete guidance for practitioners on how to allocate inference compute between denoising and selection when deploying diffusion LMs, and will shed light on whether the accuracy headroom identified in the target paper is a symptom of insufficient denoising or of suboptimal candidate usage—thereby advancing the fundamental understanding of how autoregressive knowledge is preserved and utilized in diffusion language models.
