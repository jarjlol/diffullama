**Method: Representational Similarity‑Aware Meta‑Reranking (RSA‑MR)**  
A lightweight, inference‑only pipeline that (i) measures how much the *relational* geometry of a diffusion language model’s (DLM) hidden states matches that of its autoregressive (AR) backbone using Representational Similarity Analysis (RSA), (ii) combines this relational signal with token‑level likelihood from a tiny AR scorer via a frozen meta‑learner (ridge regression) to produce a candidate‑level quality score, and (iii) evaluates whether the RSA‑based preservation score predicts the relative efficiency of this meta‑reranking versus simply adding denoising steps.

---

### Rationale  

The core question is whether the extent to which a DLM retains AR‑derived representations determines how much a cheap post‑hoc selection step can recover the model’s accuracy headroom, and whether this recovery is more compute‑efficient than running extra denoising steps.  

Existing proposals (Method 1 and Method 2) focus on **direct vector‑space alignment** (CKA/linear probing or subspace Procrustes similarity) between DLM and AR hidden states. While informative, these metrics capture *pointwise* similarity and may miss higher‑order relational structure that is crucial for semantic correctness (e.g., which tokens tend to co‑occur, syntactic dependencies).  

Representational Similarity Analysis (RSA) compares the *dissimilarity matrices* of two sets of representations, thereby assessing whether the DLM preserves the *relative* relationships among tokens or phrases as the AR model does. RSA has been shown to be sensitive to semantic and syntactic structure even when absolute vector alignment is weak.  

By extracting an RSA‑based preservation score for each DLM (and, optionally, for each candidate) and fusing it with the token‑level likelihood of a tiny AR scorer through a **frozen meta‑learner** trained on a small validation split, we obtain a candidate score that leverages both **local token probability** and **global relational fidelity**. The meta‑learner is a simple ridge regression (or logistic regression) that learns optimal weights for the two features; training is performed on CPU with negligible cost and does not involve the DLM or AR model parameters.  

Because the meta‑learner is lightweight (a few dozen parameters) and applied only after generation, its FLOP contribution remains far below 1 % of a denoising step, satisfying the inference‑only constraint.  

Finally, we directly test the hypothesis that higher RSA preservation predicts larger **relative** gains from the meta‑reranking step compared to gains from additional denoising steps, and we examine whether this relationship differs between GPT‑2‑based and LLaMA‑based DLMs or varies with the denoising‑step budget. This provides a mechanistic answer to the research question while remaining fully compatible with the resource limits (single RTX 6000 Pro Blackwell, ten‑week timeline, no training or adaptation of the DLMs).

#### Step‑by‑step Procedure  

| Step | Goal | Concrete Actions | Resources & Constraints |
|------|------|------------------|--------------------------|
| **0. Preparation** | Set up a reproducible, inference‑only pipeline respecting hardware & timeline. | • Download released DLM checkpoints (DiffuGPT‑S/M, DiffuLLaMA‑6.74B, Dream‑7B, LLaDA‑8B, DiffuCoder‑7B) and their AR base models (GPT‑2‑small/medium, LLaMA‑7B). <br>• Tokenize a held‑out probing corpus (10 k sentences from WikiText‑103 + C4) with each model’s tokenizer; store token IDs. <br>• Assemble benchmark suite (HumanEval, GSM8K, SIQA, WinoGrande) and implement min‑max normalisation to \([0,1]\) across all conditions. <br>• Enable activation‑checkpointing and 8‑bit quantisation (`bitsandbytes`) for the 6.74B DiffuLLaMA so it fits in 96 GB GPU with batch‑size ≥ 4. <br>• Split work among the three GPU‑enabled team members (model/step‑budget subsets); write results to a shared CSV/Parquet store. | One RTX 6000 Pro Blackwell (96 GB), 8‑bit quantisation + activation checkpointing, CPU‑only work for data prep/eval. |
| **1. Quantify AR‑representation preservation via RSA** | Obtain a scalar preservation score \(P_i\) that reflects how well the *relational* geometry of a DLM matches its AR backbone, without gradients. | **1.1 Feature extraction** – For each sentence in the probing corpus, run the DLM **inference‑only** (no gradient) and collect the post‑attention hidden‑state tensor \(\mathbf{H}^{\text{DLM}}_\ell \in \mathbb{R}^{N\times D}\) for each Transformer layer \(\ell\). Do the same with the frozen AR base model (same tokenizer, same sequence length) to obtain \(\mathbf{H}^{\text{AR}}_\ell\). <br>**1.2 Dissimilarity matrices** – For each layer, compute the pairwise cosine dissimilarity: \(\mathbf{D}^{\text{DLM}}_\ell = 1 - \frac{\mathbf{H}^{\text{DLM}}_\ell (\mathbf{H}^{\text{DLM}}_\ell)^\top}{\|\mathbf{H}^{\text{DLM}}_\ell\|\;\|\mathbf{H}^{\text{DLM}}_\ell\|^\top}\) (similarly for AR). <br>**1.3 RSA correlation** – Vectorise the upper‑triangular part of each dissimilarity matrix and compute the Spearman correlation \(\rho_\ell\) between \(\mathbf{D}^{\text{DLM}}_\ell\) and \(\mathbf{D}^{\text{AR}}_\ell\). <br>**1.4 Aggregation** – Weight deeper layers more (exponential decay with depth) and average: \(P_i = \sum_\ell w_\ell \rho_\ell\). This yields a training‑free similarity measure ranging from –1 (opposite relational structure) to +1 (perfect relational preservation). | Only forward passes; RSA computation (correlation of vectors of size ≤ N(N‑1)/2) is done on CPU. No gradient updates. |
| **2. Multi‑candidate generation** | Produce a diverse set of outputs for each prompt at several denoising‑step budgets. | • Choose a prompt set covering the three benchmark families (e.g., 200 HumanEval prompts, 200 GSM8K questions, 200 SIQA/WinoGrande items). <br>• For each DLM \(i\) and each denoising‑step budget \(S \in \{16,32,64,128\}\): <br> - Sample **K = 8** candidates per prompt using nucleus sampling (top‑p = 0.9). <br> - Store raw token strings and, optionally, the denoising trace for later analysis. <br>• Use a fixed random seed across models to make candidate sets comparable. | Pure inference; batch size limited by GPU memory (≈ 4‑8 sequences per step). |
| **3. Lightweight, training‑free meta‑scoring** | Combine token‑level likelihood and RSA‑based relational similarity into a candidate quality score. | **3.1 AR scorer likelihood** – Load **GPT‑2‑small (124 M)** (frozen). For each candidate, compute the average negative log‑likelihood (or perplexity) under the scorer; lower = higher likelihood. Denote this feature \(L^{\text{cand}}\). <br>**3.2 Candidate‑level RSA similarity** – For each candidate, repeat the RSA procedure of Step 1 but using the *candidate‑specific* hidden states: <br> - Run the DLM (the denoising pass that produced the candidate) and collect \(\mathbf{H}^{\text{DLM}}_\ell\). <br> - Feed the candidate token sequence through the frozen AR base model (teacher‑forcing) to obtain \(\mathbf{H}^{\text{AR}}_\ell\). <br> - Compute layer‑wise Spearman correlation \(\rho_\ell^{\text{cand}}\) between the two dissimilarity matrices as in 1.2‑1.3. <br> - Aggregate with the same depth‑weights to obtain a candidate RSA score \(R^{\text{cand}} \in [-1,1]\). <br>**3.3 Meta‑learner (frozen ridge regression)** – On a small held‑out validation split (e.g., 5 % of the probing corpus), fit a ridge regression model to predict the *oracle* Unified Quality Score (UQS) from the two features \([L^{\text{cand}}, R^{\text{cand}}]\). The ridge solution is analytic; store the weight vector \(\mathbf{w}\) and bias \(b\). No gradients flow into the DLM or AR model. <br>**3.4 Final candidate score** – Compute \(\hat{y}^{\text{cand}} = \mathbf{w}^\top [L^{\text{cand}}, R^{\text{cand}}] + b\). Rank candidates descending by \(\hat{y}^{\text{cand}}\); the top‑1 after this sort is the *meta‑reranked* output. <br>**3.5 Cost measurement** – Record forward‑pass time of the AR scorer (< 0.5 ms for GPT‑2‑small) and the extra RSA computation (CPU‑only, negligible). Total reranking cost per prompt is \(K \times (t_{\text{scorer}} + t_{\text{RSA}})\), still far < 1 % of a denoising step. | Only forward passes through AR scorer and AR base model; RSA and ridge regression are CPU‑only. No training of DLMs. |
| **4. Quality measurement** | Translate raw outputs into a unified, comparable metric. | • For each benchmark compute: <br> - HumanEval: pass@k for k = 1,10. <br> - GSM8K: exact‑match accuracy. <br> - SIQA & WinoGrande: accuracy (multiple‑choice). <br>• Normalise each raw score to \([0,1]\) across *all* model‑step‑budget‑reranking conditions (min‑max scaling). <br>• Define the **Unified Quality Score (UQS)** as the arithmetic mean of the four normalised components (HumanEval@1, HumanEval@10, GSM8K, (SIQA+WinoGrande)/2). <br>• Also compute the *oracle* UQS (best candidate among the K generated) to quantify the accuracy headroom for each condition. | All metrics computed offline on CPU; no extra GPU load. |
| **5. Experimental contrasts** | Isolate the two sources of improvement: (a) meta‑reranking vs. (b) more denoising steps. | For each DLM \(i\) and low step budget \(S_{\text{low}}=16\): <br> - **Meta‑reranking gain**: \(\Delta^{\text{meta}}_{i,S_{\text{low}}} = \text{UQS}_{i,S_{\text{low}}}^{\text{meta‑ranked}} - \text{UQS}_{i,S_{\text{low}}}^{\text{top‑1}}\). <br> - **Denoising‑step gain** (no reranking): \(\Delta^{\text{step}}_{i} = \text{UQS}_{i,128}^{\text{top‑1}} - \text{UQS}_{i,16}^{\text{top‑1}}\). <br>• Compute the *relative efficiency* \(\eta_i = \frac{\Delta^{\text{meta}}_{i,S_{\text{low}}}}{\Delta^{\text{step}}_{i}}\) (values > 1 indicate meta‑reranking yields more quality per unit of added compute). | Requires only the four step budgets; no additional model runs. |
| **6. Statistical modelling** | Test whether RSA‑based preservation predicts meta‑reranking advantage and whether this differs by architecture or step budget. | Fit a **linear mixed‑effects model** (LME) with UQS as the dependent variable: <br>\[
\text{UQS}_{ijk\ell}= \beta_0 + \beta_1 P_i + \beta_2 S_j + \beta_3 M_k + \beta_4 (P_i \times S_j) + \beta_5 (P_i \times M_k) + \beta_6 (S_j \times M_k) + u_i + \epsilon_{ijk\ell}
\] <br>where: <br>• \(P_i\) = RSA preservation score (continuous, from Step 1). <br>• \(S_j\) = denoising‑step budget (log‑scaled continuous). <br>• \(M_k\) = meta‑reranking indicator (0 = top‑1, 1 = meta‑ranked). <br>• \(u_i\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2_{\text{model}})\) captures model‑specific random intercepts. <br>Key hypotheses: <br> - **H1**: \(\beta_5 > 0\) (meta‑reranking yields larger gains when preservation is high). <br> - **H2**: \(\beta_6 \neq 0\) (the benefit of extra steps varies with preservation). <br> - **H3**: Interaction with architecture (add dummy \(A_i\) for GPT‑2‑ vs LLaMA‑based DLMs and test \(\beta_{5,\text{arch}}\)). <br>• Additionally, compute Spearman’s \(\rho\) between \(P_i\) and the relative efficiency \(\eta_i\) to directly address the core research question. <br>Inference performed with `statsmodels` (REML) or `lme4` on CPU. | All modelling performed on CPU; no extra GPU time required. |
| **7. Efficiency accounting** | Translate quality gains into comparable compute cost. | • Measure wall‑clock time for a single denoising step (averaged over 100 runs) for each DLM (with activation checkpointing & 8‑bit quant). <br>• Compute **cost per step**: \(C^{\text{step}}_i = t^{\text{step}}_i\). <br>• Compute **cost of meta‑reranking**: \(C^{\text{meta}}_i = K \times (t^{\text{scorer}}_i + t^{\text{RSA}}_i)\) (scorer time + CPU RSA time). <br>• Define **efficiency ratio**: \(\displaystyle \epsilon_i = \frac{\Delta^{\text{meta}}_{i,S_{\text{low}}} / C^{\text{meta}}_i}{\Delta^{\text{step}}_{i} / (C^{\text{step}}_i \times (128-16))}\). <br>Values > 1 indicate meta‑reranking gives more quality per FLOP/second than adding denoising steps. | Timing measurements are cheap (< 5 min total). |
| **8. Ablation & robustness checks** | Verify that findings are not artefacts of specific choices. | • Vary the RSA layer‑weighting scheme (uniform, linear, exponential) to test sensitivity of \(P_i\). <br>• Replace Spearman correlation in RSA with **Pearson** or **Kendall‑τ** as alternative similarity measures. <br>• Change nucleus sampling p (0.8, 0.95) and K (4, 16) to test sensitivity to candidate diversity. <br>• Run a *control* where the AR scorer is replaced by a random scorer to ensure gains are not due to chance ordering. <br>• Test alternative meta‑learners (LASSO, decision stump) to confirm that a simple linear model suffices. | All ablations reuse the same generated candidate pool; no extra generation needed. |
| **9. Reporting & reproducibility** | Ensure transparent dissemination. | • Archive all scripts, random seeds, and environment specifications (Dockerfile or Conda yaml). <br>• Publish the extracted RSA features (optional) and the final results table on an open repository (e.g., HuggingFace datasets + Zenodo). <br>• Provide a short “re‑run” notebook that reproduces the key figures (preservation vs. meta‑reranking gain, efficiency ratio). | Fits the ten‑week timeline: weeks 1‑2 for setup, weeks 3‑5 for generation & scoring, week 6 for metric computation, weeks 7‑8 for statistical analysis, weeks 9‑10 for write‑up and reproducibility packaging. |

### Why RSA‑MR is substantively different  

- **Metric of preservation** – Uses **Representational Similarity Analysis (Spearman correlation of dissimilarity matrices)** rather than direct vector alignment (CKA/linear probing) or subspace angles (Procrustes). RSA captures *relational* structure, which is orthogonal to the prior approaches.  
- **Meta‑learning fusion** – Combines token likelihood and relational similarity via a **frozen ridge‑regression meta‑learner** trained on a tiny validation split, producing a candidate‑level quality score that is not a simple hand‑crafted weighted sum. This adds a lightweight, data‑driven coupling step absent from Methods 1 and 2.  
- **Candidate‑level RSA** – Computes RSA *per candidate* (not just per model) to provide a fine‑grained signal that varies with the generated text, enabling the meta‑learner to weigh relational fidelity dynamically.  
- **Efficiency focus** – Explicitly measures the FLOP/second efficiency of the meta‑reranking step versus extra denoising steps, directly addressing the “more efficient than increasing denoising‑step budget” clause of the research problem.  

By grounding the analysis in relational representation preservation and employing a lightweight, train‑free meta‑scorer, RSA‑MR offers a fresh mechanistic lens on whether and how a DLM’s retained AR geometry can be exploited at inference time to recover accuracy headroom more efficiently than simply running more denoising steps.
