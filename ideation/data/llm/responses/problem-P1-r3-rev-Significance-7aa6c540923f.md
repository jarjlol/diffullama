Review:
The research problem addresses a relevant and practical question in the DLM space: whether accuracy headroom in diffusion language models stems from insufficient denoising or from suboptimal use of AR-derived representations, and whether a lightweight training-free reranking step can more efficiently recover this gap than additional denoising steps. The problem is well-grounded in the existing literature (target paper's accuracy headroom observation, REPR-ALIGN's representation alignment, Jacobi Forcing's distillation approach, TESS 2's reward guidance) and is feasible within the stated constraints. The mechanistic framing—linking representation preservation to inference-time effectiveness—is a thoughtful contribution that goes beyond pure empirical optimization.

However, the significance is tempered by several concerns: (1) the core question (reranking vs. more denoising steps) is a relatively natural and obvious comparison given the existing methods, making the novelty incremental rather than groundbreaking; (2) the unified metric averaging across heterogeneous benchmarks (code, math, commonsense) risks conflating incommensurable signals and may not yield clear insights; (3) the study is fundamentally empirical and comparative, with limited theoretical depth to support claims of "fundamental insight"; (4) the practical impact is narrow—optimizing inference-time strategies for existing DLMs—without broad transformative implications for the field; and (5) the predictive relationship between CKA scores and reranking gains is speculative and may not generalize across model families or tasks.

Feedback:
The problem would benefit from sharpening its theoretical contribution—e.g., deriving a principled criterion for when reranking should outperform additional denoising based on representation geometry—rather than relying solely on empirical correlation. The unified metric should be reconsidered; reporting per-benchmark results would be more transparent and informative. Clarifying what constitutes a "meaningful" headroom recovery (absolute vs. relative thresholds) would strengthen the practical significance. Despite these limitations, the problem is well-scoped, feasible, and addresses a genuine need in the DLM community.
