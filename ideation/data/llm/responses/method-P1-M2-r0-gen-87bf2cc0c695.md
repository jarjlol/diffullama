**Method:**  
**Representation‑Guided Reranking (ReGUIDE)** – an inference‑only pipeline that quantifies how much a diffusion language model (DLM) retains the representational geometry of its autoregressive (AR) backbone, uses that geometry to rerank generation candidates, and then compares the resulting quality gain to the gain obtained by simply increasing the denoising‑step budget.

| Step | Goal | Concrete actions | Resources & Constraints |
|------|------|------------------|--------------------------|
| **0. Preparation** | Build a reproducible, inference‑only workflow that respects the hardware and timeline limits. | • Download the released DLM checkpoints (DiffuGPT‑S/M, DiffuLLaMA‑6.74B, Dream‑7B, LLaDA‑8B, DiffuCoder‑7B) and their corresponding AR base models (GPT‑2‑small/medium for GPT‑2‑based DLMs; LLaMA‑7B for LLaMA‑based DLMs). <br>• Tokenize a held‑out probing corpus (10 k sentences drawn from WikiText‑103 + C4) with each model’s tokenizer; store the token IDs. <br>• Assemble the benchmark suite (HumanEval, GSM8K, SIQA, WinoGrande) and implement a normalisation script that maps every raw metric to \([0,1]\) across all experimental conditions (min‑max scaling). <br>• Enable activation‑checkpointing and 8‑bit quantisation (via `bitsandbytes`) for the 6.74B DiffuLLaMA so that it fits in the 96 GB GPU with batch‑size ≥ 4. <br>• Split work among the three GPU‑enabled team members: each member owns a subset of models/step‑budgets and writes results to a shared CSV/Parquet store. | One RTX 6000 Pro Blackwell (96 GB), 8‑bit quantisation + activation checkpointing, CPU‑only work for data preparation and evaluation. |
| **1. Quantify AR‑representation preservation** | Obtain a scalar “preservation score” \(P_i\) that reflects the similarity of the DLM’s hidden‑state subspace to that of its AR backbone, **without** any gradient‑based training. | **1.1 Feature extraction (frozen forward pass)** – For every sentence in the probing corpus, run the DLM **inference‑only** (no gradient) and collect the post‑attention hidden‑state tensor \(\mathbf{H}^{\text{DLM}}_\ell \in \mathbb{R}^{N\times D}\) for each Transformer layer \(\ell\). Do the same with the frozen AR base model (same tokenizer, same sequence length) to obtain \(\mathbf{H}^{\text{AR}}_\ell\). <br>**1.2 Subspace alignment metric** – For each layer \(\ell\): <br> • Center the matrices (subtract column means). <br> • Compute thin SVD: \(\mathbf{H}^{\text{DLM}}_\ell = \mathbf{U}^{\text{DLM}}_\ell \mathbf{\Sigma}^{\text{DLM}}_\ell (\mathbf{V}^{\text{DLM}}_\ell)^\top\) and similarly for AR. <br> • Retain the top‑\(r\) left singular vectors (e.g., \(r=20\)) to form orthonormal bases \(\mathbf{Q}^{\text{DLM}}_\ell,\mathbf{Q}^{\text{AR}}_\ell\). <br> • Compute the principal angles \(\theta_{1\ldots r}\) between the two subspaces via the singular values of \((\mathbf{Q}^{\text{DLM}}_\ell)^\top \mathbf{Q}^{\text{AR}}_\ell\). <br> • Derive a layer‑wise preservation score as the average cosine of the principal angles: \(s_\ell = \frac{1}{r}\sum_{k=1}^{r}\cos\theta_k\). <br>**1.3 Aggregation** – Weight deeper layers more heavily (exponential decay with depth) and average across layers to obtain the final preservation score \(P_i = \sum_{\ell} w_\ell s_\ell\). This yields a training‑free similarity measure that captures how much of the DLM’s representational subspace is spanned by the AR subspace. | No gradient updates; only forward passes and lightweight linear algebra (SVD on matrices of size ≤ \(N\times D\), with \(N\) ≈ #tokens in corpus, \(D\) ≤ 4096). All steps run on CPU or the same GPU used for generation. |
| **2. Multi‑candidate generation** | Produce a diverse set of outputs for each prompt at several denoising‑step budgets. | • Select a prompt set that covers the three benchmark families (e.g., 200 HumanEval prompts, 200 GSM8K questions, 200 SIQA/WinoGrande items). <br>• For each DLM \(i\) and each denoising‑step budget \(S \in \{16,32,64,128\}\): <br> - Sample **K = 8** candidates per prompt using nucleus sampling (top‑p = 0.9) to ensure diversity while staying close to the model’s distribution. <br> - Store the raw token strings and, optionally, the denoising trace for later analysis. <br>• Use a fixed random seed across models to make candidate sets comparable. | Pure inference; batch size limited by GPU memory (≈ 4‑8 sequences per step). |
| **3. Lightweight, training‑free reranking using AR‑representation similarity** | Re‑order the candidates based on how closely their DLM hidden states align with the AR model’s hidden states, then compute the quality gain from this selection. | **3.1 AR hidden‑state extraction for each candidate** – For every generated candidate string, feed it through the frozen AR base model (teacher‑forcing: the AR model sees the candidate token sequence and predicts the next token at each position). Collect the post‑attention hidden states \(\mathbf{H}^{\text{AR}}_\ell\) for each layer \(\ell\). <br>**3.2 Candidate‑level similarity score** – For each layer \(\ell\): <br> • Center the DLM hidden states \(\mathbf{H}^{\text{DLM}}_\ell\) (obtained during the denoising pass that produced the candidate) and the AR hidden states \(\mathbf{H}^{\text{AR}}_\ell\). <br> • Compute the **Procrustes similarity** after optimal orthogonal alignment: \(\displaystyle \text{Sim}_\ell = \frac{\| \mathbf{H}^{\text{DLM}}_\ell \mathbf{Q}_\ell^\top \mathbf{H}^{\text{AR}}_\ell \|_*}{\| \mathbf{H}^{\text{DLM}}_\ell \|_F \, \| \mathbf{H}^{\text{AR}}_\ell \|_F}\), where \(\| \cdot \|_*\) is the nuclear norm and \(\mathbf{Q}_\ell\) is the orthogonal matrix obtained from the SVD of \((\mathbf{H}^{\text{DLM}}_\ell)^\top \mathbf{H}^{\text{AR}}_\ell\). This metric ranges from 0 (no alignment) to 1 (perfect alignment up to orthogonal transformation). <br> • Average \(\text{Sim}_\ell\) across layers (using the same depth‑weights as in Step 1) to obtain a single similarity score \(\text{Sim}^{\text{cand}}\). <br>**3.3 Ranking** – Sort the K candidates in descending order of \(\text{Sim}^{\text{cand}}\); the top‑1 after this sort is the *representation‑guided reranked* output. <br>**3.4 Cost measurement** – Record the forward‑pass time of the AR base model for one candidate (negligible compared to a denoising step; for GPT‑2‑small it is < 0.5 ms, for LLaMA‑7B with 8‑bit quantisation ≈ 2 ms). The total reranking cost per prompt is \(K \times t_{\text{AR}}\). | Only forward passes through the AR base model (no training). The AR model can be run in 8‑bit quantisation with activation‑checkpointing to keep memory footprint low. |
| **4. Quality measurement** | Map raw outputs to a unified, comparable score. | • For each benchmark compute: <br> - HumanEval: pass@k for k = 1,10. <br> - GSM8K: exact‑match accuracy. <br> - SIQA & WinoGrande: accuracy (multiple‑choice). <br>• Normalise each raw score to \([0,1]\) across **all** model‑step‑budget‑reranking conditions (min‑max scaling). <br>• Define the **Unified Quality Score (UQS)** as the arithmetic mean of the four normalised components (HumanEval@1, HumanEval@10, GSM8K, (SIQA+WinoGrande)/2). <br>• Also compute the *oracle* UQS (the best candidate among the K generated) to quantify the accuracy headroom for each condition. | All metrics computed offline on CPU; no extra GPU load. |
| **5. Experimental contrasts** | Isolate the two sources of improvement: (a) representation‑guided reranking vs. (b) more denoising steps. | For each DLM \(i\) and low step budget \(S_{\text{low}}=16\): <br> - **Reranking gain**: \(\Delta^{\text{rank}}_{i,S_{\text{low}}} = \text{UQS}_{i,S_{\text{low}}}^{\text{ranked}} - \text{UQS}_{i,S_{\text{low}}}^{\text{top‑1}}\). <br> - **Denoising‑step gain** (no reranking): \(\Delta^{\text{step}}_{i} = \text{UQS}_{i,128}^{\text{top‑1}} - \text{UQS}_{i,16}^{\text{top‑1}}\). <br>• Compute the *relative efficiency* \(\eta_i = \frac{\Delta^{\text{rank}}_{i,S_{\text{low}}}}{\Delta^{\text{step}}_{i}}\) (values > 1 indicate reranking yields more quality per unit of added compute). | Requires only the four step budgets; no additional model runs. |
| **6. Statistical modelling** | Test whether preservation predicts reranking advantage and whether this differs by architecture or step budget. | Fit a **hierarchical Bayesian linear model** (or frequentist LME if preferred) with UQS as the outcome: <br>\[
\text{UQS}_{ijk\ell}= \beta_0 + \beta_1 P_i + \beta_2 S_j + \beta_3 R_{k} + \beta_4 (P_i \times S_j) + \beta_5 (P_i \times R_{k}) + \beta_6 (S_j \times R_{k}) + \beta_7 A_i + \beta_8 (P_i \times A_i) + u_i + \epsilon_{ijk\ell}
\] <br>where: <br>• \(P_i\) = preservation score (continuous). <br>• \(S_j\) = denoising‑step budget (log‑scaled continuous). <br>• \(R_k\) = reranking indicator (0 = top‑1, 1 = representation‑guided reranked). <br>• \(A_i\) = architecture dummy (0 = GPT‑2‑based, 1 = LLaMA‑based). <br>• \(u_i\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2_{\text{model}})\) captures model‑specific random intercepts. <br>Key hypotheses: <br> - **H1**: \(\beta_5 > 0\) (reranking yields larger gains when preservation is high). <br> - **H2**: \(\beta_8 \neq 0\) (the preservation‑reranking relationship differs between GPT‑2‑ and LLaMA‑based DLMs). <br> - **H3**: \(\beta_4 > 0\) (the benefit of extra steps grows with preservation). <br>• Additionally, compute Spearman’s \(\rho\) between \(P_i\) and the relative efficiency \(\eta_i\) to directly address the core research question. <br>Inference performed with `brms`/`rstanarm` (Bayesian) or `statsmodels`/`lme4` (frequentist) on CPU. | All modelling done on CPU; no extra GPU time required. |
| **7. Efficiency accounting** | Translate quality gains into comparable compute cost. | • Measure wall‑clock time for a single denoising step (averaged over 100 runs) for each DLM (with activation checkpointing & 8‑bit quant). <br>• Compute **cost per step**: \(C^{\text{step}}_i = t^{\text{step}}_i\). <br>• Compute **cost of reranking**: \(C^{\text{rank}}_i = K \times t^{\text{AR}}_i\) (AR forward pass per candidate). <br>• Define **efficiency ratio**: \(\displaystyle \epsilon_i = \frac{\Delta^{\text{rank}}_{i,S_{\text{low}}} / C^{\text{rank}}_i}{\Delta^{\text{step}}_{i} / (C^{\text{step}}_i \times (128-16))}\). <br>Values > 1 indicate reranking gives more quality per FLOP/second than adding denoising steps. | Timing measurements are cheap (< 5 min total). |
| **8. Ablation & robustness checks** | Verify that findings are not artefacts of specific choices. | • Vary the subspace rank \(r\) (10, 20, 50) in Step 1 to test sensitivity of preservation score. <br>• Replace Procrustes similarity with **Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA)** or **SVCCA** as alternative alignment metrics in Step 3. <br>• Change nucleus sampling p (0.8, 0.95) and K (4, 16) to test sensitivity to candidate diversity. <br>• Run a *control* where the AR model is replaced by a random projection matrix (same dimensions) to ensure gains are not due to chance ordering. <br>• Test alternative scorers (DistilGPT‑2, MiniLM) to confirm the < 1 % FLOP claim. | All ablations reuse the same generated candidate pool; no extra generation needed. |
| **9. Reporting & reproducibility** | Ensure transparent dissemination. | • Archive all scripts, random seeds, and environment specifications (Dockerfile or Conda yaml). <br>• Publish the extracted hidden‑state features (optional) and the final results table on an open repository (e.g., HuggingFace datasets + Zenodo). <br>• Provide a short “re‑run” notebook that reproduces the key figures (preservation vs. reranking gain, efficiency ratio). | Fits the ten‑week timeline: weeks 1‑2 for setup, weeks 3‑5 for generation & scoring, week 6 for metric computation, weeks 7‑8 for statistical analysis, weeks 9‑10 for write‑up and reproducibility packaging. |

**Rationale:**  
The existing proposal evaluates AR‑representation preservation with static similarity measures (CKA or linear probing) and uses a lightweight AR scorer to rerank candidates by likelihood. While informative, this approach conflates two distinct notions of preservation: (i) how well the DLM’s *hidden‑state geometry* matches the AR geometry, and (ii) how well the DLM’s *output likelihood* aligns with the AR distribution.  

ReGUIDE decouples these ideas by **(1)** measuring preservation as the *subspace alignment* between DLM and AR hidden‑state spaces—a training‑free, geometry‑centric metric that directly quantifies the extent to which the DLM reuses the AR representational basis. **(2)** It then reranks candidates not by AR likelihood but by the *procrustes similarity* between the DLM’s hidden states (produced during denoising) and the AR model’s hidden states for the same candidate. This makes the reranking step explicitly sensitive to the degree of representational preservation captured in Step 1, providing a sharper test of the hypothesis that models with higher preservation benefit more from a lightweight, AR‑guided selection mechanism.  

By comparing the quality gain from this representation‑guided reranking to the gain obtained by simply increasing the denoising‑step budget—and by expressing both gains in comparable compute‑cost units—we can directly answer whether the accuracy headroom is primarily a consequence of insufficient denoising or of suboptimal exploitation of AR‑derived representations. The hierarchical Bayesian (or LME) model further tests whether this relationship varies across architectures (GPT‑2‑ vs. LLaMA‑based) and step budgets, offering a nuanced, generalizable insight into the interplay between representation preservation and inference‑time efficiency.  

All steps respect the inference‑only constraint, rely solely on released checkpoints and publicly available base models, and are engineered to run within the memory and time limits of a single RTX 6000 Pro Blackwell, making the method feasible, rigorous, and readily extensible to other DLM families.
