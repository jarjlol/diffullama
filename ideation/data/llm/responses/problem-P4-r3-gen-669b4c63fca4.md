Problem:  
How does the choice of the [MASK] token (reused vocabulary token vs. newly added token) influence the infilling performance and overall generation quality of diffusion language models (DLMs) adapted from autoregressive checkpoints, and does this effect vary across model families (GPT‑2‑based vs. LLaMA‑based) and scales (≈127 M, ≈355 M, ≈7 B parameters)?

Rationale:  
The target paper shows that continual pre‑training of autoregressive (AR) checkpoints yields competitive DLMs capable of “filling in the middle without prompt re‑ordering.” However, its ablation studies reveal a potential confound: the [MASK] token is implemented inconsistently across model sizes—DiffuGPT‑S and DiffuLLaMA reuse existing vocabulary tokens (IDs 10541 and 811), while DiffuGPT‑M introduces a genuinely new token (ID 50257) (Limitation L3). This inconsistency is noted as a possible factor behind DiffuGPT‑M’s superior performance at its scale, yet the paper does not isolate the impact of the [MASK] token choice.  

Because we are restricted to inference‑only experiments on released checkpoints (DiffuGPT‑S/M, DiffuLLaMA, Dream‑7B, LLaDA‑8B, DiffuCoder‑7B), we can directly compare models that differ only in their [MASK] token treatment while keeping architecture, scale, and adaptation recipe otherwise constant. By evaluating these models on a suite of infilling benchmarks—including single‑token infilling (HumanEval single‑line), multi‑line code infilling (HumanEval whole‑function pass@k, MBPP), and longer‑context narrative infilling (ROCStories cloze, WikiPlot summarization gaps)—we can quantify whether a reused or novel [MASK] token yields systematically better infilling accuracy, lower unigram entropy, and higher MAUVE scores.  

Controlling for inference‑time compute (e.g., fixing the denoising‑step budget at 64 steps and measuring wall‑clock time/FLOPs) ensures a fair quality‑efficiency comparison. The study will also probe whether any observed token‑type effect interacts with model family or scale, revealing whether the [MASK] token choice is a scale‑dependent hyperparameter or a family‑specific artifact.  

Findings will clarify a concrete implementation detail that may affect reproducibility and deployment of adapted DLMs, offer guidance for practitioners selecting or designing mask tokens when converting AR checkpoints to diffusion models, and address a notable gap (L3) in the target paper’s empirical validation. This investigation is feasible within the ten‑week timeline using a single RTX 6000 Pro Blackwell GPU, leverages only publicly released checkpoints, and provides a novel, inference‑centric perspective on the adaptation pipeline.
