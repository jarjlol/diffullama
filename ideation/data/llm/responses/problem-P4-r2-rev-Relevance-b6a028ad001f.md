Review: The research problem is well-positioned within the current landscape of diffusion language modeling. It directly identifies a concrete limitation of the target paper (narrow single-line infilling evaluation) and proposes a systematic empirical investigation across more realistic infilling scenarios. The problem connects meaningfully to multiple related works—Dream 7B, TESS 2, PreDiff-LM, and Jacobi Forcing—all of which study bidirectional generation, arbitrary-order sampling, and AR-to-DLM adaptation. The proposed benchmarks (HumanEval whole-function pass@k, MBPP, ROCStories) are standard and appropriate, and the inference-only constraint makes the study feasible and reproducible. The problem also correctly highlights the practical significance of understanding whether adapted DLMs sustain their theoretical advantages (bidirectional context leverage, parallel generation) when infill regions grow beyond single tokens.

Feedback: The problem is clearly relevant and well-grounded, but there are several areas for refinement. First, the framing is primarily evaluative—an extension of the target paper's empirical scope—rather than introducing a novel methodological contribution, which may limit its perceived significance in a field that increasingly values architectural or training innovations. Second, the problem statement references models like DiffuCoder-7B and LLaDA-8B that are not part of the target paper's released suite, which could dilute the focus on the specific adaptation recipe being studied. Third, the mention of "WikiPlot summarization gaps" as a story-completion benchmark is somewhat tangential to the core "fill-in-the-middle" capability and could be replaced with a more directly aligned task (e.g., ROCStories cloze-style completion). Fourth, the claim that the study "offers a novel investigation" overstates the contribution slightly; it is a well-designed extension rather than a novel line of inquiry. Finally, the problem could more explicitly articulate how its findings would inform the broader DLM community—for instance, by identifying failure modes of the adaptation recipe at larger infill scales, which would guide future methodological improvements.
