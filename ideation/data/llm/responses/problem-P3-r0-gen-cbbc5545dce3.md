**Problem:**  
Can test‑time candidate aggregation and selection strategies (e.g., majority voting, likelihood‑weighted voting, or lightweight verifier reranking) substantially close the gap between single‑answer accuracy and the observed candidate hit‑rate of diffusion language models adapted from autoregressive checkpoints, and how does their effectiveness compare to that of the original autoregressive models on reasoning and commonsense benchmarks?

**Rationale:**  
The target paper’s evaluation reveals a notable discrepancy: the hit‑rate over generated candidates far exceeds the models’ single‑answer accuracy on reasoning tasks (Limitation L6), suggesting a large untapped performance headroom that the authors left unexplored. Diffusion language models inherently support parallel, bidirectional generation, enabling efficient production of multiple diverse candidates per prompt without additional training. By leveraging this property, we can apply inexpensive test‑time selection mechanisms to harness the model’s full generative capacity.  

This investigation directly addresses the identified gap (L6) while staying strictly within the inference‑only constraint (no retraining or continual pre‑training). It builds on related work that explores diffusion models as thought proposers (e.g., “Diffuse Thinking”) and on techniques for candidate aggregation in autoregressive settings (e.g., self‑consistency, weighted voting). The study will use the released checkpoints (DiffuGPT‑S/M, DiffuLLaMA, Dream‑7B, etc.) on a single NVIDIA RTX 6000 Pro Blackwell, implementing various sampling budgets and selection strategies, and evaluating on standard reasoning (GSM8K, BBH, WinoGrande) and commonsense (SIQA) benchmarks.  

If successful, the work will demonstrate a simple, compute‑efficient way to boost the performance of adapted diffusion models, highlight the practical value of their parallel generation capability, and provide clear guidance for deploying DLMs in latency‑sensitive, high‑accuracy applications. This contributes both empirically (new performance baselines) and conceptually (a deeper understanding of how to exploit the generative richness of diffusion language models).
