Review: The research problem is well-defined and directly grounded in the target paper's identified limitations, particularly the unclear role of attention-mask annealing at scale. It builds meaningfully on PreDiff-LM's demonstration that attention patterns (causal vs. bidirectional) significantly affect adaptation quality, and it extends the concept of context-adaptive scheduling (seen in Dream 7B and UNIFUSION) from noise rescheduling to attention-mask scheduling. The problem identifies a genuine gap: the temporal pattern of attention masking during diffusion sampling has not been systematically studied, despite its architectural relevance to the causal inductive bias learned during autoregressive pretraining. The operationalization of quality and efficiency metrics is concrete, and the inference-only feasibility argument is sound given publicly available checkpoints.

Feedback: The problem is strongly positioned within the current DLM research landscape and makes a clear, actionable contribution. However, a few refinements would strengthen it: (1) The framing as providing "mechanistic understanding" is somewhat overstated—the study is primarily empirical, and the mechanistic claim would benefit from more explicit theoretical grounding or ablation designs that distinguish mechanism from correlation. (2) Some of the cited limitations (L4, L8, L9) feel tangentially connected; the rationale would be tighter if it focused more narrowly on the core attention-mask question rather than trying to address too many secondary concerns. (3) The problem could better distinguish itself from PreDiff-LM's hybrid attention work by clarifying that it studies a *temporal* schedule (how the mask evolves across denoising steps) rather than a *spatial* mask pattern (which tokens attend to each other at a single step). Despite these points, the problem is pertinent, feasible, and addresses a real gap in the literature.
