**Problem:**  
How does the performance of diffusion language models (DLMs) adapted from autoregressive (AR) checkpoints on *multi‑line code infilling* and *long‑context story completion* vary across model families (GPT‑2‑based vs. LLaMA‑based) and scales (127 M – 7 B), and how does this performance compare to that of their AR counterparts when measured under comparable inference‑time compute budgets?

**Rationale:**  
The target paper demonstrates that continual pre‑training of AR checkpoints yields diffusion models capable of “filling in the middle without prompt re‑ordering,” yet its empirical support for this claim is limited to HumanEval single‑line infilling with both prefix and suffix provided (L8). This narrow evaluation leaves open whether the models can handle more realistic, multi‑token infill scenarios—such as completing a function body given only its signature, or generating missing sentences in a narrative given surrounding context.  

Recent related work (Dream 7B, TESS 2, DiffuCoder) highlights arbitrary‑order and bidirectional generation as key advantages of DLMs, but systematic studies of how well adapted DLMs sustain quality when the infill region grows longer or more syntactically complex are missing. Moreover, the entities list emphasizes *bidirectional context* and *parallel generation*, suggesting that the models’ ability to leverage context from both sides of a gap is central to their utility.  

By evaluating on multi‑line code benchmarks (e.g., HumanEval whole‑function pass@k, MBPP) and story‑completion tasks (e.g., ROCStories, WikiPlot summarization gaps) we can directly test the breadth of the “fill‑in‑the‑middle” capability claimed in the target paper. Because the study relies solely on released checkpoints (DiffuGPT‑S/M, DiffuLLaMA, Dream‑7B, LLaDA‑8B, DiffuCoder‑7B) and manipulates only the input masking pattern during inference, it satisfies the inference‑only constraint (D‑2026‑09‑21‑a). Experiments can be performed on a single NVIDIA RTX 6000 Pro Blackwell GPU, with latency and FLOPs estimated from the fixed denoising‑step budget used for each model, enabling a quality‑efficiency comparison against AR baselines (GPT‑2, LLaMA) under matched compute.  

The outcomes will reveal whether the adaptation recipe preserves the DLM’s theoretical strength for arbitrary‑order, bidirectional generation at realistic infill lengths, identify any family‑ or scale‑dependent degradation, and provide concrete guidance for practitioners seeking to deploy adapted DLMs for tasks such as code synthesis or story writing where the missing span is not limited to a single token. This addresses L8, extends the evaluation scope of the target paper, and offers a novel, feasible, and significant investigation into the practical limits of adaptation‑based diffusion language modeling.
