Review: The proposed method is a well-structured, rigorous empirical study that systematically addresses the quality-compute trade-off in diffusion language models. Its strongest innovative element is the **adaptive two-stage compute allocation strategy**, which uses lightweight AR scoring to dynamically redistribute denoising steps from low-quality to high-quality candidates — a creative inference-time technique that goes beyond static (N, S) grid search. The GAM-based marginal gain analysis and architecture-specific FLOP profiling are also methodologically sophisticated contributions. However, the overall framework is largely an extension of known techniques (candidate sampling, reranking, Pareto frontier construction) applied in a comprehensive empirical setting. The UQM's z-score normalization choice is debatable, the AR NLL-as-quality-proxy has a fundamental validity concern (likelihood ≠ correctness), and the sequence-length truncation approach is technically problematic. The method does not introduce new architectures, training paradigms, or theoretical frameworks — its contribution is primarily an empirical systems analysis with one creative adaptive heuristic.

Feedback: (1) Justify the switch from min-max to z-score+CDF normalization more clearly, or revert to min-max for stability. (2) Address the fundamental limitation of AR NLL as a proxy for semantic correctness — consider adding a task-specific reward signal for coding benchmarks. (3) Fix the sequence-length truncation issue by either generating exactly 128 tokens or fully evaluating at 256 tokens. (4) Consider whether the adaptive strategy's hyperparameters (S₀, k) could be optimized via a small validation set rather than only sensitivity sweeps. (5) Add an ablation on UQM composition (e.g., coding-only vs. full metric) to test robustness.

Rating (1-5): 3
