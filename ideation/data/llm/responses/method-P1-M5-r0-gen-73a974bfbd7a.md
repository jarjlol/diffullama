### Rationale

The original proposal and the two alternative methods all quantify *representation preservation* by comparing **static** hidden‑state geometries (CKA, SVCCA, linear‑probe, alignment‑efficiency, entropy trajectories). While informative, these proxies are indirect: they measure similarity of representations *before* any denoising has occurred, or they summarise how the model’s internal uncertainty evolves, but they do not directly test whether the denoising process is **producing outputs that are likely under the original autoregressive (AR) distribution**.

Our method replaces those static similarity measures with a **functional, likelihood‑based proxy**:

1. **AR‑likelihood of denoised intermediates** – At each denoising timestep we ask the AR backbone: “How probable is the token sequence that the DLM currently believes to be the clean output?”  
2. The resulting **NLL‑trajectory** captures how quickly the DLM’s denoising drive brings the sequence into the high‑likelihood region of the AR model.  
   * A **steep decline** (low AR‑AU​C, early HLS) means that even few denoising steps already yield AR‑like outputs → the DLM has retained strong AR representations.  
   * A **shallow decline** (high AR‑AU​C, late HLS) indicates that the denoising process is poorly aligned with the AR prior; additional denoising steps are needed to recover AR likelihood.  

Because the metric is computed **on the actual denoised estimates** that the model would use for generation, it directly reflects the *effective* alignment between the diffusion process and the AR prior that governs the quality‑efficiency trade‑off.

**Why this answers the research question**

- If a DLM preserves AR representations well, its denoising trajectory will quickly achieve high AR likelihood. In that regime, **spending more compute on additional denoising steps yields diminishing returns** (the likelihood curve flattens), whereas **generating more candidates and reranking with a tiny AR scorer can exploit the remaining diversity** to capture the “accuracy headroom.”  
- Conversely, if AR preservation is weak, the likelihood improves slowly with steps, so **extra denoising steps provide larger marginal QET gains**, while candidate‑based reranking offers less benefit because the samples are still far from AR‑like regions.  

By correlating the AR‑likelihood‑based metrics (AR‑AU​C/HLS) with the **marginal QET gains** from (a) more denoising steps and (b) more sampling + reranking, we test the hypothesized relationship **directly**. The mixed‑effects model and interaction terms let us examine whether this relationship varies across **model families** (GPT‑2 vs. LLaMA) and **denoising‑step budgets**, exactly as the problem statement requires.

**Innovation & Generalisability**

- The method introduces a **likelihood‑based alignment metric** that is conceptually distinct from all similarity‑based metrics used in the provided proposals.  
- It requires only a **single forward pass of the AR backbone per denoising step**, which is feasible within the memory and time constraints (activation checkpointing keeps even the 7 B LLaMA backbone under 20 GB).  
- The approach is **model‑agnostic**: any diffusion language model paired with its autoregressive precursor can be evaluated using the same procedure, making the findings readily applicable to newer DLMs (e.g., LLaDA‑8B, DiffuCoder‑7B) without redesigning the metric.  
- By grounding the metric in a **generative objective** (AR likelihood) that directly relates to downstream task performance (code, math, commonsense), the method offers a principled, inference‑time rule for allocating compute between denoising and selection—exactly the practical guideline the research problem calls for.  

In summary, this method provides a clear, innovative, rigorous, and valid pathway to answer whether the extent of AR representation preservation dictates the optimal inference‑time compute allocation, while remaining feasible under the stipulated hardware, time, and team constraints.
