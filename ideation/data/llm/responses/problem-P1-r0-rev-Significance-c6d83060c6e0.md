Review: The research problem is clearly defined and addresses genuine gaps in the diffusion language modeling literature, particularly the lack of systematic, compute-normalized analysis of how denoising-step budgets affect the quality-efficiency trade-off across different model families and scales. The problem is well-motivated by specific limitations identified in the target paper (narrow infilling evaluation, non-compute-normalized efficiency claims, unexplored accuracy headroom) and proposes a feasible, empirically grounded approach. The study design—including sweeping denoising steps, evaluating across five diverse DLMs, and analyzing structured code generation—demonstrates methodological rigor.

However, the problem's significance is constrained by its essentially incremental and descriptive nature. The core question—whether more inference steps yield better quality with diminishing returns—is broadly intuitive, and the answers are likely to confirm expectations rather than reveal surprising insights. The work functions as a careful empirical characterization (benchmarking/sensitivity analysis) rather than introducing new methods, theoretical frameworks, or architectural innovations. While the practical guidance on denoising budgets is useful for practitioners, it is unlikely to catalyze transformative shifts in how the field approaches diffusion language modeling. The scope, though well-defined, is narrow enough that its broader implications remain limited.

Feedback: To elevate this problem's significance, consider framing it within a deeper question—for instance, whether the step-quality frontier reveals fundamental architectural differences between GPT-2-based and LLaMA-based diffusion adaptations that inform future model design, or whether the observed trade-offs expose structural limitations of the continual pre-training approach that could motivate new training objectives. Additionally, incorporating a qualitative analysis of failure modes at low step budgets, or connecting the findings to theory about convergence properties of diffusion processes in discrete spaces, would add intellectual depth. The problem would also benefit from explicitly stating what novel insight or decision-making capability the community will gain that they currently lack—going beyond "we will provide the first systematic study" to articulate why such a study is expected to change understanding or practice.
