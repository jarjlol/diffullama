Review: The research problem is clearly articulated and addresses a genuine, identified gap in the diffusion language modeling literature — specifically, the systematic study of attention-mask schedules during inference-time sampling. The rationale is well-structured, linking directly to reported limitations (L1, L4, L8, L9) of the target paper and leveraging publicly available checkpoints for an entirely inference-only investigation. The operational definitions of quality (perplexity, infilling accuracy, reasoning accuracy) and efficiency (forward passes per token, wall-clock time per token) are concrete and measurable, and the feasibility analysis is realistic given available hardware and existing model checkpoints.

However, the core idea — manipulating attention-mask schedules during diffusion sampling — while underexplored, is not entirely unprecedented. Dream 7B has demonstrated context-adaptive rescheduling; PreDiff-LM has studied hybrid attention patterns (causal within prompt, bidirectional within masked targets); and UNIFUSION has explored unified reverse-rate objectives across corruption kernels. The proposed work sits at the intersection of these threads but does not introduce a fundamentally new paradigm, architectural innovation, or theoretical framework. The mechanistic hypothesis — that early denoising steps benefit from causal masking while later steps can safely incorporate bidirectional context — is plausible and interesting, but remains speculative without preliminary evidence, and the proposed remedy (simple linear mask annealing) is straightforward and architecture-agnostic. Much of the study's value lies in systematic empirical characterization rather than in a groundbreaking conceptual advance.

Feedback: The problem is well-motivated and clearly defined, with strong practical significance for deploying scaled diffusion language models. To strengthen the originality profile, the authors should more sharply foreground the mechanistic contribution — specifically, the hypothesis that the interaction between attention-mask schedules and model scale reveals something fundamental about how causal inductive bias transfers across generation orders in diffusion processes. This would elevate the work beyond a routine empirical sweep of inference-time hyperparameters. Additionally, the authors should clarify what constitutes a meaningful negative result (e.g., if mask annealing proves ineffective) and how that would still advance understanding. Explicitly distinguishing the proposed work from PreDiff-LM's hybrid attention and Dream 7B's context-adaptive rescheduling would also sharpen the differentiation claim.

Rating (1-5): 3
