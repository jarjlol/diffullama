Review: The research problem is well-defined and directly motivated by identified gaps (L6, L8, L9) in the target paper. It asks a clear, answerable question about the denoising-step–quality–efficiency relationship across model families and scales. The inference-only constraint is a pragmatic and well-justified design choice that aligns with available resources. The methodology—sweeping step counts, recording quality and efficiency metrics, plotting curves, and analyzing structured infilling—is sound and standard. The problem is firmly grounded in the existing literature (target paper and related works like Dream 7B, dLLM, UNIFUSION, Jacobi Forcing), and the seven-person team provides adequate capacity for the scope.

However, several concerns temper the assessment: (1) The "shared and contended" single-GPU constraint is a real bottleneck—running five models across seven step settings with multiple benchmarks (perplexity, pass@k on HumanEval single-line and whole-function, GSM8K, SIQA/WinoGrande, latency measurements) amounts to thousands of inference passes, and contention could significantly inflate wall-clock time within a ten-week window. (2) Cross-model comparison introduces technical friction: different models may rely on different inference frameworks (e.g., HuggingFace transformers, diffusers, custom code), making standardized FLOPs estimation and latency measurement non-trivial. (3) The assumption that all five named checkpoints are readily accessible, loadable, and support arbitrary step-count sweeping at inference time is not verified; some may require specific loading procedures or have fixed step defaults. (4) Whole-function code generation evaluation (addressing L8) requires careful prompt engineering and a reliable execution harness, which adds complexity not fully addressed. (5) The problem overlaps with recent work on inference-time compute scaling (e.g., Jacobi Forcing, TESS 2 reward guidance), so novelty must be carefully framed as a systematic empirical characterization rather than a methodological contribution.

Feedback: The problem is fundamentally sound and the resources are broadly commensurate with the scope. To strengthen feasibility, the team should: (a) pre-validate checkpoint accessibility and inference compatibility for all five models in the first week; (b) develop a unified inference harness early to avoid framework fragmentation; (c) estimate total GPU-hours required and negotiate guaranteed scheduling on the contended GPU; (d) prioritize the most impactful benchmarks to stay within compute budget; (e) explicitly state how step counts are set for each model (some may not support arbitrary step counts natively). These steps would mitigate the main risks and keep the project on track.
