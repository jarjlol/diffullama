Review: The research problem addresses a timely and practically relevant gap in the evaluation of diffusion language models (DLMs). By shifting focus from the single-line infilling benchmarks used in the target paper (DiffuGPT/DiffuLLaMA) to multi-line code completion and long-context story generation, the study tests whether the claimed "fill-in-the-middle" capability generalizes to realistic, syntactically complex scenarios. The systematic comparison across model families (GPT-2 vs. LLaMA) and scales (127M–7B), combined with compute-matched baselines, provides a rigorous framework for assessing the practical utility of adapted DLMs.

Feedback: The problem is well-motivated by the target paper's limited empirical scope—specifically its reliance on HumanEval single-line infilling—and aligns with recent related work (Dream 7B, TESS 2, DiffuCoder) that emphasizes bidirectional generation as a key DLM advantage. The inference-only design ensures feasibility while directly addressing whether adaptation preserves the theoretical strength of arbitrary-order generation at realistic infill lengths. However, the problem is primarily empirical and extensionary rather than proposing novel theoretical frameworks or architectural innovations; its significance lies in providing crucial deployment guidance rather than advancing fundamental understanding. Additionally, the rapid evolution of the field (e.g., Jacobi Forcing, MoE-FM approaches) means findings may have a limited shelf-life, and the problem would benefit from a more specific hypothesis regarding how performance degrades with infill length or syntactic complexity to move beyond exploratory benchmarking.

Rating (1-5): 4
