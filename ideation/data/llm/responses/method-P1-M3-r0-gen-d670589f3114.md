**Method: Diversity‑Guided Compute Allocation for Diffusion Language Models**  
**Rationale:**  
If a diffusion language model (DLM) retains the autoregressive (AR) backbone’s representations, its denoising trajectory will quickly converge to token distributions that are already close to the AR prior. In that regime, additional denoising steps produce only marginal improvements in quality, but the generated candidates will be *similar* to one another (low diversity). Conversely, when the DLM’s internal representations drift far from the AR prior, the denoising process explores a broader region of the output space, yielding a set of candidates that is more *diverse* but also contains many low‑quality samples. In this case, spending compute on sampling more candidates and reranking them with a tiny AR scorer can recover the “accuracy headroom” more efficiently than simply running extra denoising steps.  

Thus, **the diversity of the candidate set produced at a fixed denoising budget serves as an observable, inference‑time proxy for the degree of representation preservation**. By measuring candidate diversity and linking it to the marginal quality‑efficiency trade‑off (QET) gains of (a) more denoising steps versus (b) more sampling + reranking, we can test the hypothesis without directly probing internal hidden‑state geometry. This approach is orthogonal to the two previously proposed methods (which relied on CKA/SVCCA or alignment‑efficiency metrics) and provides a simple, output‑based diagnostic that can be computed with negligible overhead during deployment.

### Why This Method Is Substantively Different  

1. **Proxy Based on Output Diversity** – Rather than measuring internal hidden‑state similarity (CKA, SVCCA, linear probe, or alignment‑efficiency curves), we infer representation preservation from the *semantic spread* of the model’s own generations. This flips the causal direction: we use the model’s behavior (diversity of candidates) as a readable signal of how well its denoising process aligns with the AR prior.  

2. **Decision‑Oriented Metric** – The diversity score can be computed on-the‑fly after generating a tiny pilot set (e.g., N = 2 or 4) and used to gate the allocation of the remaining inference budget (more denoising steps vs. more sampling + reranking). This yields a practical, inference‑time rule that the baseline methods do not provide.  

3. **Orthogonal Validation** – By additionally computing the traditional CKA‑based preservation scores in a validation step (not used for the main hypothesis), we can confirm whether diversity truly tracks representation preservation without biasing the primary test.  

4. **Focus on Candidate Utility** – The method directly links the *utility* of generating more candidates (their diversity) to the effectiveness of lightweight reranking, addressing the core of the research problem: “does allocating compute to more denoising or to more candidates + reranking give a larger QET improvement, and does this depend on AR knowledge retention?”  

5. **Minimal Overhead** – All extra computations (sentence‑embedding, pairwise cosine distance) are performed on CPU and add < 5 % to the total runtime, keeping the study comfortably within the resource envelope.  

In summary, this method offers a fresh, output‑centric perspective on the trade‑off between denoising steps and candidate selection, provides a deployable heuristic for compute allocation, and rigorously tests the hypothesized relationship between representation preservation (via diversity) and QET gains across model families and denoising budgets.
