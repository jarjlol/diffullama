Review: The research problem is well-defined, clearly motivated, and tightly aligned with the inference-only resource constraint. The core methodology—generating multiple candidates from released DLM checkpoints at varying denoising step budgets, then reranking them with a lightweight AR scorer—is technically straightforward and does not require any training, adaptation, or continual pre-training. The problem builds naturally on the target paper's findings and several related works (Jacobi Forcing, TESS 2, REPR-ALIGN), and the research questions (selection vs. steps, model-family differences, step-budget dependence) are sharply focused and answerable with the available checkpoints. The team composition (7 people, 3 with GPU access) and division of labor (GPU-bound generation/reranking vs. CPU-bound evaluation/analysis) is reasonable.

However, several feasibility concerns warrant attention. First, the "shared and contended" status of the single RTX 6000 Pro Blackwell introduces meaningful risk: if actual usable GPU time is substantially less than nominal, the volume of generation required across 5+ model checkpoints × 7 step budgets × multiple benchmarks × many prompts could exceed the 10-week window. Second, running candidates at step counts up to 1000 for a 6.74B model is extremely slow and may dominate the compute budget disproportionately; the study would benefit from a more carefully prioritized step-range design. Third, the logistical details around accessing certain checkpoints (e.g., LLaMA-7B licensing, DiffuCoder-7B availability) and the exact identity/specs of the "small AR scorer" need clarification. Finally, while the scientific question is sound, the experimental design should include concrete estimates of candidate volume per prompt and per benchmark to verify that the total FLOPs fit within the constrained GPU budget—the paper currently asserts feasibility without this quantification.

Feedback: The problem is fundamentally sound and the inference-only constraint is a genuine advantage. To strengthen the feasibility case, the authors should: (1) provide a concrete estimate of total generation tokens/candidates and expected wall-clock time per experiment to validate the 10-week timeline under contended GPU conditions; (2) prioritize step budgets (e.g., focus on ≤128 steps where marginal returns are most interesting, and treat 512–1000 as optional); (3) confirm access and licensing for all intended checkpoints and the small AR scorer; (4) define a clear experimental priority matrix so that the most critical comparisons (e.g., selection at low step budgets) are guaranteed completion even if GPU time is reduced. With these refinements, the problem becomes highly feasible.

Rating (1-5): 4
