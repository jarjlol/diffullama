**Review:**

The ReGUIDE method presents a well-structured inference-only pipeline that thoughtfully combines several existing techniques to investigate a timely research question. The most novel element is the use of Procrustes similarity between DLM and AR hidden states as a reranking criterion, which is a creative application of geometric alignment methods to inference-time candidate selection. The decoupling of preservation measurement (Step 1) from reranking scoring (Step 3) using the same geometric framework provides a coherent and internally consistent approach. The comparative framework—quantifying reranking gains against denoising-step gains in compute-equivalent units—is a rigorous experimental design that goes beyond simple empirical optimization.

However, the method's innovativeness is limited in several respects. The subspace alignment metric (SVD + principal angles) is essentially a variant of CKA/SVCCA that already exists in the literature. The overall pipeline—extract features, compute similarity, rerank, compare against baseline—is a standard empirical methodology. The core idea of using hidden-state similarity as a proxy for quality is a natural extension of reward-based reranking approaches (cf. TESS 2's reward guidance), and the inference-only constraint, while practically motivated, limits the scope of possible contributions. The method does not introduce new training objectives, architectural modifications, theoretical frameworks, or conceptual reframings of the problem. Compared to Jacobi Forcing's progressive distillation or REPR-ALIGN's representation alignment during training, ReGUIDE operates at the inference level with purely geometric metrics, which is a narrower contribution.

The statistical modeling (Step 6) and efficiency accounting (Step 7) are thorough but standard for empirical studies of this type. The ablation studies (Step 8) add robustness but do not constitute innovation per se.

**Feedback:**

The method would benefit from a clearer articulation of what is genuinely new versus what is a novel combination of existing techniques. The Procrustes-based reranking is the strongest novel element—consider motivating it more explicitly against simpler alternatives (e.g., cosine similarity on pooled hidden states) to demonstrate why the orthogonal alignment matters. The hypothesis that preservation predicts reranking gain is well-motivated, but the method could be strengthened by a theoretical or empirical argument for *why* Procrustes similarity (rather than, say, centered kernel alignment or simple CCA) is the right metric for this specific question. Additionally, the method conflates two distinct geometric comparisons (subspace alignment in Step 1 vs. sample-level Procrustes in Step 3)—clarifying the relationship between these two levels of analysis would enhance conceptual clarity. Finally, while the inference-only constraint is important for practicality, acknowledging what the method *cannot* address (e.g., whether representation alignment during training could further improve results) would provide a more complete picture.

**Rating (1-5):** 3
