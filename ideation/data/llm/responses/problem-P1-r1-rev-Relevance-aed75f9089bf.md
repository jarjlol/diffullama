Review: The research problem is clearly situated within the active and rapidly growing field of diffusion language models (DLMs). It directly builds on the target paper's released checkpoints (DiffuGPT, DiffuLLaMA) and addresses a genuine evaluation gap—the target paper focuses on single-answer accuracy at fixed denoising budgets without investigating whether post-hoc candidate selection could recover observed accuracy headroom more efficiently than additional denoising steps. The problem also connects meaningfully to several related works (TESS 2's reward guidance, Jacobi Forcing's compute-quality trade-offs, Dream 7B's tunable quality-speed paradigms) and engages with the deeper question of how autoregressive knowledge is preserved and utilized in diffusion models. The experimental design is concrete, feasible, and well-scoped within the stated constraints.

Feedback: The problem is well-defined and directly motivated, with clear practical significance for DLM deployment. However, there are several areas for refinement: (1) The framing could more explicitly engage with the representation-preservation literature (e.g., REPR-ALIGN, PreDiff-LM) to deepen the theoretical grounding of why candidate selection might or might not work; (2) The novelty of lightweight reranking could be sharpened by distinguishing it from existing inference-time methods like TESS 2's reward guidance or Jacobi Forcing's trajectory distillation—clarifying what specific gap this work fills relative to those approaches; (3) The reference to "accuracy headroom (L6)" would benefit from full contextualization for readers unfamiliar with the target paper's specific findings; (4) The problem is primarily empirical in nature, so articulating how the results would advance *fundamental understanding* (beyond practical guidance) would strengthen its case; (5) The scope could be tightened by explicitly defining what constitutes "lightweight" selection and how it differs from existing candidate generation paradigms.
