**Review:**
The proposed method demonstrates a systematic approach to investigating the quality-compute trade-off in diffusion language models, with clear procedural steps and mathematical formulations. The method addresses the research problem directly by varying denoising steps and candidate counts, employing a unified quality metric, and constructing Pareto fronts. The inclusion of adaptive compute allocation, bootstrap significance testing, and sensitivity analyses reflects thoughtful experimental design. However, several aspects warrant scrutiny: (1) the z-score normalization of quality metrics within experimental conditions risks circularity, as the normalization depends on the very conditions being compared; (2) the FLOP estimation relies on simplified cycle-to-FLOP conversions that may not capture hardware-specific optimizations or memory bandwidth limitations; (3) the tokenizer alignment strategy for cross-model AR scoring requires clearer justification regarding structural differences in tokenization schemes; (4) the GAM analysis omits potential interaction effects between candidate count and step budget; (5) reproducibility details (library versions, random seeds for benchmark evaluation) are incomplete. The method is well-structured but falls short of exemplifying the highest rigor due to these methodological approximations and unvalidated assumptions.

**Feedback:**
To enhance rigorousness, consider: (1) replacing condition-dependent z-score normalization with absolute metrics or population-normalized baselines to avoid circularity; (2) validating FLOP estimates against hardware performance counters rather than theoretical cycle counts; (3) providing detailed tokenizer alignment protocols and testing cross-model scoring robustness; (4) including interaction terms in the GAM or using factorial designs; (5) specifying software versions and complete random seeds for full reproducibility; (6) addressing how degenerate outputs are handled in candidate generation; (7) justifying the equal weighting of disparate benchmarks in the unified metric.
