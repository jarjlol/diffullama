Review:
The research problem is well-motivated and clearly defined, targeting a genuine gap left by the target paper (which ablates step budgets and shift operations but not noise schedule shape). The problem systematically proposes comparing linear, cosine, and context-adaptive noise schedules across adapted DLMs of different scales and architectures, with a compute-normalized efficiency lens. The feasibility analysis is thorough, and the connections to the target paper's reported limitations (L1, L4, L9) provide useful conceptual framing.

However, the originality of the core question is constrained by several factors. First, the influence of noise schedule shape on diffusion model quality is a well-established concept in the broader diffusion literature (e.g., linear vs. cosine schedules in DDPM). Second, Dream 7B already employs context-adaptive token-level noise rescheduling, and UNIFUSION explores uniform-noise diffusion with generalized reverse-rate objectives—meaning the idea that noise schedule matters in DLMs is not entirely unexplored. Third, the problem is fundamentally an empirical ablation study on a known hyperparameter dimension rather than introducing a new method, theoretical framework, or paradigm shift. The novelty resides in the specific experimental design (cross-model-family, cross-scale systematic comparison), which is valuable but incremental rather than groundbreaking.

Feedback: The problem would benefit from more explicitly articulating what specific *new insight* or *mechanism* it expects to uncover beyond confirming that noise schedules matter (which is largely assumed). Strengthening the originality could involve: (a) hypothesizing specific interactions between noise schedule shape and model architecture (e.g., why LLaMA-based models might respond differently than GPT-2-based ones), (b) proposing a novel schedule design informed by the findings rather than only comparing existing ones, or (c) framing the study as a diagnostic tool that reveals structural properties of adapted DLMs rather than merely an ablation. As stated, the problem is a well-crafted empirical investigation but does not set a new research direction.
