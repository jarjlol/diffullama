**Review:**
The research problem is clearly formulated and directly motivated by specific, identified limitations in the target paper (L7, L8, L9, L10). It proposes a systematic, inference-only empirical investigation into how masking strategy choices reshape the quality-efficiency frontier of scaled diffusion language models across model families (GPT-2 vs. LLaMA) and scales (127M–7B). The problem is well-scoped, feasible under stated constraints, and includes a thoughtful comparison to alternative approaches (AR-MLM hybrids). The framing is practical and could yield actionable insights for practitioners.

**Feedback:**
While the problem is well-motivated and addresses genuine gaps, its originality is moderate rather than high. The core investigation — how masking strategies affect DLMs — is not entirely unexplored; related work such as Dream 7B (block-wise masking), PreDiff-LM (hybrid attention strategies), and Jacobi Forcing (adapting AR models for parallel decoding) have already touched on related concepts. The proposed study is essentially a comprehensive empirical ablation and benchmarking effort rather than a conceptual or methodological innovation. To elevate originality, the authors could consider: (1) developing a theoretical or geometric framework that predicts *when* and *why* specific masking strategies outperform others, rather than merely cataloging empirical trade-offs; (2) proposing a novel adaptive or hybrid masking mechanism that goes beyond the four static strategies listed; or (3) leveraging the masking strategy analysis to uncover deeper insights into the AR-to-DLM representation transfer process itself, which could contribute a more fundamental understanding to the field. The problem as stated is a strong empirical contribution but does not yet set a new research direction.

**Rating (1-5): 3**
