Review: The research problem investigates whether the degree to which a Diffusion Language Model (DLM) preserves the autoregressive (AR) representations of its pretrained backbone determines the effectiveness of a lightweight post-hoc selection mechanism (e.g., reranking with a small AR scorer) for improving the quality-efficiency trade-off. 

Feedback: The problem is well-motivated by the "accuracy headroom" identified in the target paper and the inference-time compute trade-offs explored in recent works like Jacobi Forcing and TESS 2. However, its originality is moderate. The proposed methodology closely mirrors existing techniques: using centered kernel alignment (CKA) or linear probing to measure representation preservation directly parallels the objectives of REPR-ALIGN (Paper 5), while the post-hoc reranking with a lightweight AR scorer is a variation of the inference-time guidance and distillation methods explored in TESS 2 (Paper 6) and Jacobi Forcing (Paper 10). The study essentially synthesizes these existing threads into an empirical correlation analysis. While it offers a useful lens for understanding DLM behavior, it does not introduce a novel algorithmic paradigm, a new training objective, or a fundamentally unexplored challenge. It represents a logical and valuable incremental analysis rather than a groundbreaking perspective.

Rating (1-5): 3
