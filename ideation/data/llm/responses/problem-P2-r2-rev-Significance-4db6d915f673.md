The research problem is well-motivated and directly addresses several acknowledged limitations of the target paper (DiffuGPT/DiffuLLaMA), particularly the narrow infilling evaluation, the lack of compute-normalized efficiency metrics, and the question of architectural generality across model families. Feedback:
Despite its solid framing, the problem's significance is tempered by several considerations. First, the core contribution is fundamentally an empirical ablation study—systematically varying one hyperparameter (masking strategy) and measuring its impact. While thorough characterization is valuable, it does not introduce new methods, architectures, or theoretical insights. Second, related work (e.g., Dream 7B's context-adaptive noise rescheduling, PreDiff-LM's hybrid attention, and Jacobi Forcing's parallel decoding) already touches on masking and efficiency from different angles, meaning the incremental novelty of a systematic comparison—while useful—may not constitute a transformative contribution. Third, the assumption that masking strategy is a high-leverage "simple knob" remains empirically unvalidated; it is plausible that masking has limited impact relative to other factors such as denoising schedules or model architecture. Fourth, the inference-only constraint, while pragmatic, inherently limits the depth of contribution: without modifying training, the study can characterize but not fundamentally improve the models. That said, the compute-normalized efficiency metric and the cross-family/scale comparison are genuinely useful additions to the literature, and the findings could meaningfully inform practical deployment and future DLM design. The problem sits firmly in the territory of rigorous empirical contribution rather than groundbreaking innovation.
