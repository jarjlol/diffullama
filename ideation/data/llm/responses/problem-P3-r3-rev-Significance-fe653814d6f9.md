Review: The research problem addresses a highly relevant and timely bottleneck in the diffusion language model (DLM) landscape: the reasoning accuracy gap and the underexplored test-time compute strategies for adapted models. By systematically investigating the quality-efficiency trade-off of candidate generation and autoregressive (AR) verification across different scales and families, the problem targets a practical deployment hurdle that directly impacts the viability of DLMs in complex reasoning tasks.

Feedback: The problem is well-motivated by the target paper's observations regarding the mismatch between hit-rate and single-answer accuracy, as well as the undertraining of adapted DLMs. It offers clear practical implications by seeking Pareto-optimal configurations for resource-sensitive settings, potentially providing a viable workaround for the reasoning limitations of DLMs without requiring costly retraining. However, the core methodology—generating multiple candidates and reranking them with an external verifier—is an incremental application of existing paradigms (such as the "Diffuse Thinking" framework mentioned in related paper #7 and standard pass@k strategies prevalent in autoregressive models). While empirically valuable, the problem lacks a fundamentally novel conceptual contribution or transformative theoretical breakthrough. It primarily maps an existing test-time compute paradigm onto a new class of models (adapted DLMs), which limits its significance to an important but incremental empirical and engineering contribution rather than a groundbreaking one.

Rating (1-5): 3
