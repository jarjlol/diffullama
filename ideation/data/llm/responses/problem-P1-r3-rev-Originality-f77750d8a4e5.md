Review: The research problem presents a well-motivated and systematic empirical inquiry that bridges two active subfields: representation alignment (e.g., REPR-ALIGN) and inference-time efficiency (e.g., Jacobi Forcing, TESS 2). By proposing to measure representation preservation (via CKA) and link it to the optimal allocation of inference compute (more denoising steps vs. candidate sampling with AR reranking), the problem offers a novel diagnostic angle. However, the core components—measuring CKA, utilizing AR reranking, and varying denoising step budgets—are entirely composed of existing, established techniques. The hypothesis itself is highly intuitive: if a DLM closely preserves its AR backbone, its latent space is well-aligned with the AR prior, making candidate reranking effective; if preservation is low, additional denoising is required to compensate. Because the problem is fundamentally an empirical synthesis of known methodologies to answer a logical, predictable question rather than introducing a new paradigm, architecture, or theoretical framework, it lacks the groundbreaking nature required for a higher score.

Feedback: To elevate the originality of this problem, the authors could consider moving beyond merely correlating existing metrics (CKA) with standard inference strategies. For instance, they could investigate whether *specific types* of representation misalignment (e.g., layer-wise vs. attention-head-wise) require fundamentally different inference strategies, or whether the proposed diagnostic can predict when a DLM fails entirely and requires architectural modifications rather than just compute reallocation. Additionally, while the problem correctly identifies a gap in the literature regarding compute allocation, the proposed methodology is strictly inference-only and incremental. Clarifying how this diagnostic fundamentally changes the design of future DLMs (rather than just guiding practitioners on how to run existing ones) would help distinguish the work from a standard empirical ablation study.
