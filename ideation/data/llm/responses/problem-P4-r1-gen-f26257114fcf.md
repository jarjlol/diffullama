Problem:  
How does applying a constant shift to the logits (i.e., the shift operation) during denoising steps of diffusion language models adapted from autoregressive checkpoints affect the quality‑efficiency trade‑off, and does this relationship vary across model families (GPT‑2‑based vs. LLaMA‑based) and scales (127 M – 7 B)?

Rationale:  
The target paper shows that autoregressive (AR) checkpoints can be converted into competitive diffusion language models (DLMs) via continual pre‑training, yet several limitations point to under‑examined components of the adaptation. Limitation L2 highlights that the shift operation—a constant added to the model’s logits that originates from AR training dynamics—has a large impact on accuracy (‑11.9 to ‑15.2 points when removed) but its behavior at scale has never been studied. Because the shift is a simple post‑hoc modification of the logits, it can be applied at inference time without any retraining, making it an ideal candidate for an inference‑only study under the given resource constraints.

Quality will be measured using standard language‑modeling perplexity on WikiText‑103, zero‑shot reasoning performance on ARC and Hellaswag, and code‑infilling ability on HumanEval (single‑line infill with gold prefix/suffix). Efficiency will be captured by wall‑clock latency per generated token and an estimate of floating‑point operations (FLOPs) derived from the number of denoising steps and model size; since the shift operation does not alter the model architecture, any efficiency changes will stem solely from shifts in the convergence speed of the denoising process (e.g., fewer steps needed to reach a target perplexity).

The experiment will run inference on the publicly released checkpoints that span the required families and scales: DiffuGPT‑S/M (GPT‑2‑based, 127 M/355 M), DiffuLLaMA (LLaMA‑based, 6.74 B), Dream‑7B, LLaDA‑8B, and DiffuCoder‑7B. For each checkpoint we will sweep a small set of shift values (e.g., ‑2, ‑1, 0, +1, +2) applied uniformly at every denoising step, while keeping the denoising‑step budget fixed (e.g., 18 steps as used in the target paper). We will also compute the shift value that yields the best quality‑efficiency Pareto frontier for each model, enabling a cross‑family and cross‑scale comparison.

This study directly addresses L2 by quantifying how the shift operation influences performance at scale, tests whether the effect is consistent across architectures, and provides actionable guidance for practitioners who wish to tune diffusion LM output quality without retraining. Because it relies solely on released checkpoints and manipulates only the logits during inference, it fits comfortably within the inference‑only constraint, a single RTX 6000 Pro Blackwell GPU, a ten‑week timeline, and a seven‑person team (three GPU‑enabled for inference runs, four for harness construction, analysis, and write‑up). The results will clarify whether the shift operation is a universal lever for improving the quality‑efficiency trade‑off of adapted diffusion LMs or whether its benefits are architecture‑ or scale‑specific, thereby filling a notable gap in the current literature on diffusion language modeling.
