Review: The method demonstrates moderate generalizability within the diffusion language model paradigm, supported by explicit generalization checks (unseen DLM architectures, alternative scorers, cross-family validation, sequence-length ablation). The inference-only, model-agnostic design allows application across diverse DLM checkpoints without retraining. However, the approach remains constrained by its reliance on discrete token-level logit access and the specific product-of-experts fusion mechanism, limiting transfer to continuous-state diffusion models or non-autoregressive scoring paradigms. The cross-family scoring test and alternative scorer validation provide evidence of adaptability, but the fundamental interface requirements (exposing intermediate logits, tokenizer compatibility) restrict deployment to models with compatible architectures.

Feedback: While the generalization checks are commendable, they remain superficial—testing one additional DLM (3B) and two alternative scorers is insufficient to claim broad architectural robustness. The cross-family scoring test does not address distribution shift between AR and DLM training corpora. The sequence-length ablation is promising but limited to short sequences (≤512 tokens), leaving scalability to long-context generation unverified. The method assumes trivial tokenizer replacement, which may fail with incompatible segmentation schemes (e.g., BPE vs. SentencePiece). Finally, the fixed fusion strength λ may require per-architecture tuning, limiting zero-shot transfer across heterogeneous model families without calibration.

Rating (1-5): 3
