Feedback:
The problem's principal strength is its tight integration with the target paper and the broader DLM literature: it identifies a genuine gap (the interaction between schedule shape and architectural inductive biases) and proposes a practical, inference-only intervention (the hybrid linear-cosine schedule) to address a known limitation (missing attention-mask annealing). However, there are areas for improvement. First, the novelty is somewhat incremental—multiple recent papers (Dream 7B, UNIFUSION) have already established that noise schedules matter, so the contribution hinges on the architectural-interaction angle, which is speculative and could benefit from a stronger theoretical motivation for *why* rotary and absolute embeddings would respond differently to noise distributions. Second, the claim that a noise schedule can "emulate" attention-mask annealing lacks a mechanistic justification; the two operate on fundamentally different principles (timestep allocation vs. attention pattern modification), and this equivalence should be argued more rigorously. Third, the problem could engage more broadly with efficiency-focused works like Jacobi Forcing and MoE-Flow Matching, which address similar quality-efficiency trade-offs through different paradigms, to better contextualize the proposed approach's competitive positioning. Finally, the mechanistic hypothesis about positional embeddings, while compelling, should be framed as a testable conjecture rather than an assumed premise, and the experimental design should include ablation studies that can potentially falsify it.
