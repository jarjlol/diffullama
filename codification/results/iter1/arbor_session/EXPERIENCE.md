# Findings: arbor-rerank

- **[pitfall] AR NLL features on this GSM8K candidate-selection task** — Per-token NLL under all 5 AR scorers is anti-predictive in the decisive cases (where the majority-vote answer is wrong and the correct answer is among the candidates): correct-minority has lower NLL than the plurality in only 2/14 dev-dream and 5/21 test-llada cases. Likelihood-weighted voting (sum exp(-mean NLL) per answer, any scorer) scores 0.65/0.73 on dream/llada dev vs 0.70/0.745 for pure majority. Do not build the selector on AR likelihood as a primary or weighting signal.
- **[leverage] Data structure: cross-model consensus availability** — Dev and test are both 1000 items = 200 questions x 5 models, items in fixed model blocks (diffugpt-s, diffugpt-m, diffullama, dream-base, llada-base), same question text/uid across models. Dream+LLaDA majority-vote agreement is 121/124 (97.6%) correct on dev; false consensus only 3 items. A deterministic module-level cache keyed on question text (state across select() calls, no file/gold access) lets LLaDA condition on Dream's selection: on the 76 disagreement items, deferring to Dream's answer when it is among LLaDA's candidates lifts LLaDA dev 0.745->0.765 (test 0.735->0.785 with a plurality>=4 gate on Dream's side).
- **[pitfall] Operator scope constraint (stated twice at idea review)** — Cross-model consensus (reading other models' answers to the same question via a call-order cache) is OUT OF SCOPE for this task per the human reviewer: it is ensembling beyond "one model's N samples", unfair to the per-model baselines, and makes the score depend on evaluation order. Do not propose it again. All signals must come from within the item itself (candidate texts, extracted answers, cached features).
- **[pitfall] Remaining signal audit on dream/llada decisive items (majority-wrong, correct in minority)** — Every remaining within-item signal is anti-predictive or dead-end: cross-candidate verified-result consistency (correct answer appears as another candidate's verified calc result LESS often than the wrong majority: dev 4 vs 11 dream, 8 vs 15 llada), question-number derivability prior (6 vs 13 dev dream, 6 vs 9 llada), extraction commitment weighting (####-discount tiers: at most +1 net item per split, below the 0.003 merge threshold — simulated C4 tiers 1.0/0.75/0.5 give dev 0.2980, test 0.2980 vs gate 0.29896). Floor models: best per-candidate selector (any AR mean NLL) captures only 2-5 of 14-22 oracle items per model per split, dev/test inconsistent, below merge threshold; their correct candidates contain essentially no verifiable calculations. Under the in-item-only constraint, ~0.296-0.298 macro is the practical ceiling for explainable rules; remaining majority-vote errors are comprehension errors needing out-of-scope cross-model information.
