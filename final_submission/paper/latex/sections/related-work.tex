\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

Where does test-time selection sit within the diffusion language model literature? We group prior work into diffusion adaptation, answer selection, and evaluation controls.

\lead{Diffusion language models and AR adaptation}
Early text diffusion models operated in continuous embedding or simplex spaces \citep{li2022diffusionlm,han2023ssdlm}. Discrete masked diffusion simplifies training via reparameterized or Rao--Blackwellized objectives that approach AR perplexity \citep{zheng2023reparam,sahoo2024mdlm}, and idealised masked diffusion transformers match chain-of-thought expressivity \citep{svete2025reasoning}. At billion-parameter scale, LLaDA 8B trains masked diffusion from scratch \citep{nie2025llada}, whereas continual pre-training \citep{ke2023continual} adapts existing AR checkpoints into DLMs: DiffuLLaMA and DiffuGPT adapt GPT-2 and LLaMA-2 \citep{gong2025diffullama}, TESS~2 adapts and instruction-tunes an AR backbone \citep{tae2025tess2}, Dream 7B adapts Qwen2.5-7B \citep{ye2025dream7b}, and Dream-Coder 7B targets code \citep{xie2025dreamcoder}. Most closely related to our preservation test, Repr-Align enforces layer-wise alignment to the AR base to speed adaptation training \citep{peng2026repralign}; we instead test whether unforced post-adaptation representation similarity predicts test-time AR reranking gain.

\lead{Test-time selection and verifiers}
Sampling multiple solutions and aggregating by majority vote (self-consistency; \citealp{wang2023selfconsistency}) is a strong training-free baseline for numeric tasks, while trained verifiers rank GSM8K reasoning traces with supervised correctness labels \citep{cobbe2021gsm8k}. Recent DLM-specific methods vote across decoding block sizes (HEX; \citealp{lee2025hex}), couple diffusion proposers with AR evaluators or latent projectors \citep{shao2025diffusethinking,berrayana2025planner}, or rerank DLM outputs with task-trained reward models (75.13\% vs.\ 60.05\% majority on Dream-Instruct; \citealp{zhan2026prm}) and bidirectional consistency (BMC, 77.8\% vs.\ 74.3\% on LLaDA-Instruct; \citealp{ruan2026bmc}). Those studies use Instruct checkpoints and different prompts, so their absolute scores are not directly comparable to our Base-checkpoint four-shot setting. Our decision-model selector requires no task-specific training, reads all candidates jointly in a single forward pass, and handles both math and code.

\lead{Code evaluation and contamination controls}
Code generation is evaluated by hidden unit tests on HumanEval \citep{chen2021humaneval}; EvalPlus (HumanEval+) expands test coverage roughly $80\times$ to expose false passes \citep{liu2023evalplus}. Because GSM8K and HumanEval have been public since 2021, selectors can exploit answer familiarity rather than checking solutions; perturbation benchmarks such as GSM-Symbolic \citep{mirzadeh2024gsmsymbolic} motivate our diagnostic controls and ongoing fresh-data follow-up.
