\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Majority vote works while samples agree. When they do not, it cannot recover a minority answer, and for code it has no definition. AR likelihood does not fill the gap: it trails majority at every budget, and its apparent preservation signal disappears after the cut-text correction. A decision model fits the selection problem better. It reads the question and all candidates in one pass and returns a choice, which is the operation the task requires. In our experiments it beats majority by 5.2 points at 64 steps (78.0\% vs.\ 72.8\%) and by 4.0 at 256 (85.7\% vs.\ 81.7\%; difference-in-differences +0.5, $p=0.82$). Weak-vote gating reaches 78.5\% using 47.8\% of calls. On code it hits the 40.3\% HumanEval oracle and selects 51/53 HumanEval+ solvable tasks. Controls bound the claim to selection, not verified reasoning.
