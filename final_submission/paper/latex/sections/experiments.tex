\section{Experiments and Results}
\label{sec:experiments}

We ask whether decision-model selection survives a higher denoising budget and a switch from math to code, and what the controls show.

\lead{Pipeline fidelity, step budget, and the AR negative baseline}
At the published greedy settings ($K=256$, $T=0$, $n=100$), our pipeline reproduces the reported scores and is slightly higher: 81.0\% vs.\ 77.2\% on Dream and 73.0\% vs.\ 70.3\% on LLaDA. The same-item first samples at $K=64$, $T=0.7$ are 41.0\% and 51.0\%, so the low 64-step baseline comes from the sampling budget, not a harness bug. Across denoising budgets on 500 GSM8K questions, raising $K$ from 16 to 32 adds 9.8 points to the Dream first sample (2.6 to 12.4\%) and 19.2 to LLaDA (3.6 to 22.8\%). Majority vote at $K=16$ adds only 0.6 and $-0.4$ points (3.2\% on both). It climbs to 23.4\% / 51.0\% at $K=32$ and 72.4\% / 73.2\% at $K=64$, closing 64\% of macro headroom. An automated selector search (Arbor) on 300 held-out questions ties majority within 0.3 points (73.0\% vs.\ 73.3\% / 73.0\%).

Frozen AR likelihood loses at every budget. Cutting text past the answer line raises Dream's Qwen2.5-7B summed-NLL accuracy by 13.0 points (95\% CI $[+9.0,+17.0]$) and leaves LLaDA unchanged. Even with cut text, the best AR selector reaches only 48.8\% on Dream and 52.2\% on LLaDA at $K=64$, 23.6 and 21.0 points below majority (both $p<0.001$; Table~\ref{tab:gsm8k}). Diffusion self-score on the 200-question gate also trails majority (31.5\% Dream, 45.0\% LLaDA vs.\ 71.0\%, 73.5\%). At $K=256$, AR still loses by 8.6 points on Dream (CI $[-14.1,-3.2]$, $p=0.002$) and 2.6 on LLaDA (CI $[-6.8,+1.6]$, $p=0.33$).

\lead{Representation preservation is not supported}
On uncut scores, layer-averaged CKA (DiffuGPT-S 0.890, DiffuGPT-M 0.866, DiffuLLaMA 0.748, Dream 0.509) ordered AR headroom as predicted: Spearman $+1.0$ for 3/10 selectors, $+0.8$ for 6/10, $+0.4$ for 1/10; exact $p\ge 0.083$. Cutting trailing text flips Dream's Qwen2.5-7B-sum headroom from $-9.7\%$ to $+17.7\%$ (LLaDA $+8.9\%$; the three floor models lack cut scores), so the cross-model ordering does not survive. An exploratory per-question CKA test on Dream ($n=500$) is also null: ROC-AUC $=0.469$ (permutation $p=0.32$; odds ratio 0.94 per SD, 95\% CI $[0.75,1.17]$), with no layer significant after Benjamini--Hochberg correction.

\lead{Decision-model reranking beats majority at both budgets}
Table~\ref{tab:gsm8k} shows that one listwise pass reaches 78.0\% macro at $K=64$ (77.0\% Dream, 79.0\% LLaDA). That is 5.2 points above majority (cluster 95\% CI $[+3.0,+7.4]$; $0/10,000$ resamples $\le 0$; per-model McNemar Dream +4.6, 48+/25$-$, $p=0.0095$; LLaDA +5.8, 42+/13$-$, $p=0.00011$) and 27.5 above AR cut-sum (cluster CI $[+24.4,+30.7]$). Both pre-registered comparisons hold. Pointwise scoring reaches 77.5\% (+4.7 vs.\ majority, CI $[+2.4,+7.0]$) but is inconsistent across models (Dream +1.8, $p=0.35$; LLaDA +7.6, $p=4.5\times 10^{-7}$) and needs $10\times$ more calls. Listwise and pointwise differ by 0.5 points (CI $[-1.2,+2.3]$, n.s.). Giving Qwen2.5-7B the same listwise prompt yields only 56.4\% (+5.9 over its own NLL, still 16.4 below majority), so the gain comes from the decision model and not from prompting alone.

The margin holds at higher budgets. On the 377 cached $K=256$ items (100\% coverage), listwise reaches 85.7\% vs.\ 81.7\% majority (+4.0 points, cluster CI $[+0.9,+7.1]$, $p=0.010$; +9.6 over AR cut-sum; per-model Dream +3.2, $p=0.21$; LLaDA +4.7, $p=0.093$; underpowered at $n\approx 190$). Restricting $K=64$ to the same 377 questions gives +3.5 points, so the matched difference-in-differences is +0.5 points (CI $[-4.0,+5.1]$, $p=0.82$). Absolute headroom shrinks as majority rises, but the selector's margin over majority does not shrink measurably. An $N$-sweep on 100 $K=256$ items/model shows +6.5 points already at $N=2$ (77.5\% vs.\ 71.0\%, CI $[+2.6,+10.5]$), so the gain does not depend on long lists.

\lead{Weak-vote hybrid gating halves judge calls}
Listwise selection errs on 25 Dream and 13 LLaDA items that majority got right. Tuning a top-vote gate $T\in\{4,5,6\}$ on dev ($n=200$/model) selects $T=5$ (79.0\% at 42.3\% calls). On test ($n=500$/model), keeping majority when at least 5 candidates agree and calling listwise otherwise reaches 78.5\% macro (Dream 78.0\%, LLaDA 79.0\%) with 47.8\% of judge calls. Dream gains +5.6 over majority ($p=0.00076$) and LLaDA +5.8 ($p=1.5\times 10^{-5}$).

\lead{Code selection on HumanEval and HumanEval+}
On 144 HumanEval problems (Table~\ref{tab:humaneval}), listwise picks a passing Dream candidate on all 58 solvable problems (40.3\%, equal to the pass@10 oracle). It beats the fixed docstring-example filter (40/144 = 27.8\%, +12.5 points, 18+/0$-$, $p=7.6\times 10^{-6}$), the first sample (10.4\%), MBR-exec (6.2\%), and AR NLL (4.9--5.6\%). The split matters. Of the 58 solvable problems, 42 have docstring examples, where the filter gets 36/42 vs.\ 42/42 for listwise (+6, $p=0.031$); on the 16 without examples the filter falls back to candidate 0 (4/16 vs.\ 16/16, $p=4.9\times 10^{-4}$). The aggregate margin is therefore largest where the example filter has no signal, but listwise also leads on the example-bearing problems.

Under HumanEval+ ($n=142$), listwise drops 4.9 points (40.8\% to 35.9\%, 51/142). Most of the drop is the oracle falling from 40.8\% to 37.3\% (53/142) as false passes are rejected. Five of the 7 lost items have no Plus-passing candidate, leaving 2 selection misses. Listwise picks 51/53 = 96.2\% of Plus-solvable items, against 36/53 = 67.9\% for the example filter. LLaDA was dropped after it collapsed to newline runs at $K=64$ (0/10 on \texttt{HumanEval/0}). An MBPP pilot was stopped after Dream produced 0 passing candidates in the first 20 problems.

\lead{Controls, cost, and limitations}
Table~\ref{tab:controls} shows what the decision model uses. Stripping reasoning to distinct candidate answers (judge-alone) drops accuracy from 78.0\% to 42.8\% (428/1,000, below the 45.0\% uniform pick). Traces matter, though we do not attribute the full 35.2-point gap to them. On the 115 oracle-miss items with gold added, gold is chosen 20.9\% (24/115). Two-option closed-book reaches 92.5\% (37/40), largely because its distractor is drawn from a different GSM8K question and often differs in scale. Candidate-order shuffles leave accuracy flat: 74.5\% in both orders on GSM8K dev, with 67\% choosing the same original candidate, and 30/50 on HumanEval. HumanEval recognition separates canonical code from single-token failing mutants on 59/60 items (98.3\%) with docstrings and 16/20 (80.0\%) stripped.

\lead{Limitations}
(1) Public-benchmark contamination remains live for GSM8K and HumanEval; HumanEval+ strengthens tests but does not make them fresh. A GSM-Symbolic \cite{mirzadeh2024gsmsymbolic} evaluation is underway post-deadline. (2) At $K=256$, 94\% (Dream) and 76\% (LLaDA) of cut texts keep same-line continuation after \texttt{\#\#\#\#~N}, which affects AR and decision inputs identically. (3) We test one API snapshot without prompt tuning; a typed-choice endpoint cannot run an open-ended decider-only baseline. (4) Shared-GPU generation timings (36.8--55.0\,s/q at $K=64$; 151--217\,s/q at $K=256$) are advisory. Reranking adds no GPU time and costs about \$0.015 per 1,000 listwise calls (\$0.007 at $T=5$).
