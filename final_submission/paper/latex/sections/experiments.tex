\section{Experiments and Discussion}
\label{sec:experiments}

\lead{Setup}
We use 200 GSM8K questions for selector development and reserve 300 test questions for the final score \citep{cobbe2021gsm8k}. Each model draws $N=10$ candidates per question. The models are DiffuGPT-S, DiffuGPT-M, and DiffuLLaMA \citep{gong2025diffullama}, Dream 7B \citep{ye2025dream7b}, and the from-scratch control LLaDA \citep{nie2025llada}. The baseline uses 64 denoising steps. DiffuGPT-S uses one worked example and 192 new tokens because its 512-token context cannot hold the four-shot prompt. We score candidates with GPT-2, Qwen2.5-0.5B, and TinyLlama. We also use Qwen2.5-7B for Dream and LLaMA-2-7B for DiffuLLaMA. We compute layer-averaged linear CKA \citep{kornblith2019cka} on up to 2,000 WikiText-103 segments.

\lead{Generation fidelity}
With 256 steps and temperature 0, greedy accuracy reaches 81.0\% on Dream with our 4-shot prompt and 73.0\% on LLaDA ($n=100$ each). The published figures are 77.2\% and 70.3\%. The first sample from the original run scored 41.0\% and 51.0\% on these questions. This points to the 64-step, temperature-0.7 sampler as the source of the earlier gap. At 256 steps, the temperature-0.7 first sample is 7.0 points below greedy on Dream and 2.9 points above it on LLaDA (paired, $n=185$ and $137$). The step budget accounts for most of the observed gap.

\lead{Results on the final split}
The best rule Arbor found weights votes by arithmetic verification. Its difference from majority vote is $-0.3$ points on Dream (95\% CI $[-2.0,+1.3]$, $p=1.00$) and 0.0 on LLaDA (95\% CI $[-1.7,+1.7]$, $p=1.00$). It improves on the first sample by $+32.0$ and $+24.3$ points, respectively. The development-set gain on Dream did not replicate. Majority vote alone recovers 61 to 69\% of available headroom on the two models, consistent with the role of agreement in self-consistency \citep{wang2023selfconsistency}.

\lead{AR reranking}
At 64 steps, scoring only through the final answer yields 48.8\% on Dream and 52.2\% on LLaDA (Table~\ref{tab:budget}). Both trail majority vote by 23.6 and 21.0 points, respectively ($p<0.001$ for both). Excluding the trailing text raises Dream's accuracy under the summed Qwen2.5-7B score by 13.0 points (95\% CI $[9.0,17.0]$) and leaves LLaDA's essentially unchanged.

\lead{Denoising steps}
Table~\ref{tab:budget} holds $N=10$ and temperature fixed. At 16, 32, and 64 steps, majority accuracy is 3.2\%, 23.4\%, and 72.4\% for Dream and 3.2\%, 51.0\%, and 73.2\% for LLaDA. At 64 steps it adds 32.0 and 24.6 points over the first sample. At 256 steps the gains are smaller, 10.3 and 6.8 points, on the random subsets ($n=185$ and $192$). At that budget the best AR scorer trails majority by 8.6 points on Dream (95\% CI $[-14.1,-3.2]$, $p=0.002$). The gap on LLaDA is 2.6 points (95\% CI $[-6.8,+1.6]$, $p=0.33$). Relative to one greedy sample, voting adds $+3.2$ points on Dream ($p=0.24$) and $+8.0$ on LLaDA ($n=137$, $p=0.007$). The $T=0.7$ results use a different sampler from the published greedy results. Generation times from shared GPUs do not establish a controlled cost comparison.

\lead{Preservation and gain}
The original scores give the predicted rank order (Table~\ref{tab:preservation}). Spearman's $\rho$ is $+1.0$ for 3 scorers, $+0.8$ for 6, and $+0.4$ for 1 (exact $p\geq 0.083$). Scoring only through the final answer changes Dream's recovered headroom under the summed Qwen2.5-7B score from $-9.7$\% to $+17.7$\%. We cannot recompute the four-model correlation because the three models with low oracle accuracy lack corrected scores. The corrected sign for Dream removes the main support for the original ordering. An exploratory per-question test on Dream also leaves the hypothesis unsupported. Over 500 questions it gives AUC 0.469, permutation $p=0.32$, and an odds ratio of 0.94 per standard deviation (95\% CI $[0.75,1.17]$). No layer is significant after Benjamini--Hochberg correction.
