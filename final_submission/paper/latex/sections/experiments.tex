\section{Experiments and Results}
\label{sec:experiments}

Does decision-model selection overcome the failure of AR reranking across math, higher denoising budgets, and code---and what do the controls reveal?

\lead{Pipeline fidelity, step budget, and AR negative baseline}
At published greedy settings ($K=256$, $T=0$, $n=100$), our pipeline reproduces and slightly exceeds reported scores: 81.0\% vs.\ 77.2\% on Dream and 73.0\% vs.\ 70.3\% on LLaDA (vs.\ 41.0\% and 51.0\% for our $K=64$, $T=0.7$ first sample on the same items), confirming the low 64-step first-sample baseline reflects the sampling budget rather than a harness bug. Across denoising budgets on 500 GSM8K questions, raising $K$ from 16 to 32 adds +9.8 points (Dream: 2.6$\to$12.4\%) and +19.2 points (LLaDA: 3.6$\to$22.8\%) to the first sample, while majority vote at $K=16$ adds only +0.6 and $-0.4$ points (3.2\% on both). Majority climbs to 23.4\% / 51.0\% at $K=32$ and 72.4\% / 73.2\% at $K=64$ (closing 64\% of macro headroom), while an automated selector search (Arbor) on 300 held-out questions ties majority within 0.3 points (73.0\% vs.\ 73.3\% / 73.0\%).

Frozen AR likelihood fails across every budget. Cutting trailing text past \texttt{\#\#\#\#~N} raises Dream's Qwen2.5-7B summed-NLL accuracy by +13.0 points (95\% CI $[+9.0,+17.0]$) and leaves LLaDA unchanged, yet even with cut text the best AR selector reaches only 48.8\% (Dream) and 52.2\% (LLaDA) at $K=64$---23.6 and 21.0 points below majority (both $p<0.001$; Table~\ref{tab:gsm8k}). Diffusion self-score on the 200-question gate similarly trails majority (31.5\% Dream, 45.0\% LLaDA vs.\ 71.0\%, 73.5\%). At $K=256$, AR still loses to majority by 8.6 points on Dream (CI $[-14.1,-3.2]$, $p=0.002$) and 2.6 on LLaDA (CI $[-6.8,+1.6]$, $p=0.33$).

\lead{Representation preservation is unsupported}
On uncut scores, layer-averaged CKA ($\rho_m$: DiffuGPT-S 0.890, DiffuGPT-M 0.866, DiffuLLaMA 0.748, Dream 0.509) ordered AR headroom as predicted (Spearman $\rho=+1.0$ for 3/10 selectors, $+0.8$ for 6/10, $+0.4$ for 1/10; exact $p\ge 0.083$). However, cutting trailing text flips Dream's Qwen2.5-7B-sum headroom from $-9.7\%$ to $+17.7\%$ (LLaDA $+8.9\%$; the three floor models lack cut scores), invalidating the cross-model ordering. An exploratory per-question CKA test on Dream ($n=500$) likewise shows null association: ROC-AUC $=0.469$ (permutation $p=0.32$; odds ratio 0.94 per SD, 95\% CI $[0.75,1.17]$), with no layer significant after Benjamini--Hochberg correction.

\lead{Decision-model reranking beats majority at $K=64$ and $K=256$}
Table~\ref{tab:gsm8k} shows that a single listwise pass of \texttt{pplx-decider-flash-v1.3} reaches 78.0\% macro accuracy at $K=64$ (77.0\% Dream, 79.0\% LLaDA), beating majority vote by \textbf{+5.2 points} (cluster 95\% CI $[+3.0,+7.4]$, $0/10,000$ resamples $\le 0$; per-model McNemar Dream +4.6, 48+/25$-$, $p=0.0095$; LLaDA +5.8, 42+/13$-$, $p=0.00011$) and AR cut-sum by \textbf{+27.5 points} (cluster CI $[+24.4,+30.7]$). Both H1 and H2 hold. Pointwise scoring reaches 77.5\% (+4.7 vs.\ majority, CI $[+2.4,+7.0]$), but is inconsistent across models (Dream +1.8, $p=0.35$; LLaDA +7.6, $p=4.5\times 10^{-7}$) and requires $10\times$ more calls; listwise vs.\ pointwise differs by +0.5 points (CI $[-1.2,+2.3]$, n.s.). Giving Qwen2.5-7B the same listwise prompt yields only 56.4\% (+5.9 over its own NLL, still $-16.4$ below majority), confirming the gain comes from the decision model rather than prompting alone.

Crucially, the decision model's margin survives higher denoising budgets. On the 377 cached $K=256$ items (100\% coverage), listwise reaches \textbf{85.7\%} vs.\ 81.7\% majority (\textbf{+4.0 points}, cluster CI $[+0.9,+7.1]$, $p=0.010$; +9.6 over AR cut-sum; per-model Dream +3.2, $p=0.21$, LLaDA +4.7, $p=0.093$, underpowered at $n\approx 190$). Restricting $K=64$ to those exact 377 questions gives +3.5 points, yielding a matched diff-in-diff of \textbf{+0.5 points} (CI $[-4.0,+5.1]$, $p=0.82$): absolute headroom shrinks as majority rises, but the selector's margin over majority does not shrink measurably. An $N$-sweep on 100 $K=256$ items/model shows +6.5 points already at $N=2$ (77.5\% vs.\ 71.0\%, CI $[+2.6,+10.5]$), so the gain does not depend on long lists.

\lead{Weak-vote hybrid gating halves judge calls}
Listwise selection errs on 25 Dream and 13 LLaDA items that majority got right. Tuning a top-vote gate $T\in\{4,5,6\}$ on dev ($n=200$/model) selects $T=5$ (79.0\% at 42.3\% calls). Scored once on test ($n=500$/model), keeping majority when $\ge 5$ candidates agree and calling listwise otherwise reaches \textbf{78.5\%} macro (Dream 78.0\%, LLaDA 79.0\%) with only \textbf{47.8\%} of judge calls (Dream +5.6 vs.\ majority, $p=0.00076$; LLaDA +5.8, $p=1.5\times 10^{-5}$).

\lead{Code selection: HumanEval, HumanEval+, and the 42/16 split}
On 144 HumanEval test problems (Table~\ref{tab:humaneval}), listwise selection picks a passing Dream candidate on all 58 solvable problems (\textbf{40.3\%} = pass@10 oracle), beating the fixed docstring-example filter (40/144 = 27.8\%, +12.5 points, 18+/0$-$, $p=7.6\times 10^{-6}$), first sample (10.4\%), MBR-exec (6.2\%), and AR NLL (4.9--5.6\%). \emph{Key caveat:} of the 58 solvable problems, 42 contain docstring examples, where the filter gets \textbf{36/42} vs.\ \textbf{42/42} for listwise (+6, $p=0.031$); on the 16 solvable problems without examples, the filter falls back to candidate 0 (\textbf{4/16} vs.\ \textbf{16/16}, $p=4.9\times 10^{-4}$). Thus the aggregate margin concentrates where the example filter has no signal.

Under stricter \textbf{HumanEval+} tests ($n=142$), listwise drops 4.9 points (40.8\%$\to$\textbf{35.9\%}, 51/142), but most of this drop is the oracle falling 40.8\%$\to$37.3\% (53/142) as false passes are rejected: 5 of 7 lost items have zero Plus-passing candidates, leaving only 2 selection misses (\textbf{51/53 = 96.2\%} of Plus-solvable items vs.\ 36/53 = 67.9\% for the example filter). LLaDA was dropped after collapsing to newline runs at $K=64$ (0/10 on \texttt{HumanEval/0}); an MBPP pilot was stopped after Dream yielded 0 passing candidates in the first 20 problems.

\lead{Controls, cost, and limitations}
Table~\ref{tab:controls} bounds what the decision model uses. Stripping reasoning to show only distinct candidate answers (\emph{judge-alone}) drops accuracy from 78.0\% to \textbf{42.8\%} (428/1,000, below uniform pick at 45.0\%), so traces matter, though we do not attribute the full 35.2-point gap to them. On the 115 oracle-miss items with gold injected, gold is chosen \textbf{20.9\%} (24/115) of the time. By contrast, two-option closed-book reaches 92.5\% (37/40) largely because its distractor is drawn randomly from a \emph{different} GSM8K question (often mismatched in scale). Candidate-order shuffles leave accuracy flat (74.5\% both orders on GSM8K dev, 67\% choosing the same original candidate; 30/50 on HumanEval). On HumanEval recognition, the decider separates canonical code from single-token failing mutants on 59/60 items (98.3\%) with docstrings and 16/20 (80.0\%) stripped.

\lead{Limitations}
(1) Public-benchmark contamination remains live for GSM8K and HumanEval (HumanEval+ strengthens tests, not freshness); fresh \textbf{GSM-Symbolic} \cite{mirzadeh2024gsmsymbolic} evaluation is underway post-deadline. (2) At $K=256$, 94\% (Dream) and 76\% (LLaDA) of cut texts keep same-line continuation junk after \texttt{\#\#\#\#~N}, affecting AR and decision inputs identically. (3) We test one API snapshot without prompt tuning; a typed-choice endpoint cannot run an open-ended decider-only baseline. (4) Shared-GPU generation timings (36.8--55.0\,s/q at $K=64$; 151--217\,s/q at $K=256$) are advisory; reranking adds zero GPU time and costs $\approx\$0.015$/1,000 listwise calls ($\approx\$0.007$ at $T=5$).
