Diffusion language models (DLMs) generate text by iteratively unmasking a sequence in parallel instead of left to right. DiffuLLaMA (Gong et al., 2025) showed that such models need not be trained from scratch: continual pre-training converts autoregressive (AR) models such as GPT-2 and LLaMA-2 into DLMs, and the authors hypothesise that "the adapted model retains some of the abilities from the base AR model". The same paper also exposes a large gap between what these models can produce and what they output. On SATMath, DiffuLLaMA answers 23.6% correctly few-shot and 27.7% with self-consistency (majority vote over three samples; Wang et al., 2023), yet at least one of three samples is correct 57.7% of the time. The authors attribute this to "high uncertainty about its responses", but they do not attempt verifier-based selection or calibration to close the gap. Our limitation analysis of the paper flags this as an open gap (L8), together with the narrow model coverage of the adaptation recipe (L10).

This suggests a question that is specific to adapted DLMs. If an adapted model keeps its AR base's representations, a cheap, frozen AR model should be able to judge its candidates, so selection should recover more of the gap for models that preserve more. We study the testable core of this question (research problem P1 of our ideation stage, idea 1.1). Each DLM samples $N$ candidates per question, and a training-free selector picks one. The goal is to beat majority vote and to test whether a model's representation preservation $\rho_m$, measured as layer-wise CKA against its AR base (Kornblith et al., 2019), predicts how much of the oracle headroom an AR scorer recovers (see the Problem Formulation section).

**Contributions.**

1. **A controlled selection testbed.** It covers five released DLMs: DiffuGPT-S/M and DiffuLLaMA (Gong et al., 2025) and Dream-7B (Ye et al., 2025), all adapted from an AR base, plus LLaDA-8B (Nie et al., 2025), trained from scratch, as a control. Each model has $N = 10$ cached candidates per GSM8K question (Cobbe et al., 2021). A protected harness scores every selector on the same candidates against first-sample, majority-vote, AR-likelihood and oracle references, and blocks access to gold answers.
2. **A direct test of AR-likelihood selection, which loses to majority vote.** Even with a 7B AR scorer (Qwen2.5-7B, Dream's own base), and after re-scoring only the text up to each candidate's final answer (trailing generated text had distorted the iteration-1 scores; removing it raised Dream's AR accuracy by 13 points), the most AR-likely candidate trails majority vote by 23.6 points on Dream and 21.0 on LLaDA at 64 steps (500 test questions), and by 8.6 (Dream, $p = 0.002$) and 2.6 (LLaDA, interval includes zero) at 256 steps ($n = 185$ and $192$); at 64 steps it beats the first sample (48.8% vs 40.4% on Dream, 52.2% vs 48.6% on LLaDA) but recovers far less than voting. CKA preservation and AR-recoverable headroom had the predicted rank order on the original scores (Spearman $+1.0$ for 3 of the 10 AR selectors, $+0.8$ for 6), but with four adapted models ($p \ge 1/12$) this is not evidence; the corrected scores remove even that order for the Qwen2.5-7B scorer, and a per-question test on Dream does not support it either (AUC 0.469, $p = 0.32$).
3. **An agent-driven search over selectors.** It uses the Arbor agent, with a human reviewing each idea it proposed (unanswered ideas were auto-approved after a timeout). The best rule it found, arithmetic-verification-weighted voting, is indistinguishable from majority vote on 300 held-out questions (−0.3 and 0.0 points on Dream and LLaDA). Majority vote already closes 61–69% of the first-sample-to-oracle gap, and the remaining ~15 points of oracle accuracy were not reached by any signal computed from the candidates.
4. **An iteration-2 audit of our own pipeline.** Greedy decoding at the published settings reaches 81.0% on Dream and 73.0% on LLaDA (published 77.2% and 70.3%), so the low first-sample accuracy of iteration 1 comes from its decoding settings (mostly the 64-step budget: at 256 steps the temperature-0.7 first sample is within 7 points of greedy), not from a pipeline bug; and at 16 steps more denoising adds 9.8 and 19.2 points while majority vote adds 0.6 and −0.4.
