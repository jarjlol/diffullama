**Setting.** Let $\mathcal{M}$ be a set of masked diffusion language models (DLMs). A model $m \in \mathcal{M}$ is either adapted from an autoregressive (AR) base $b(m)$ or, for the control, trained from scratch ($b(m) = \varnothing$). Let $\mathcal{D} = \{(q_j, a_j^\ast)\}_{j=1}^{n}$ be math word problems $q_j$ with numeric gold answers $a_j^\ast$ (GSM8K). For each question, $m$ is prompted with a $k$-shot prompt $x_j$ and draws $N$ candidates independently [unverified: the code draws $N$ samples per question, independence of the samplers not checked],
$$y_{j,1}, \dots, y_{j,N} \overset{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} p_m(\cdot \mid x_j).$$
An extractor $\phi : \mathcal{Y} \to \mathbb{R} \cup \{\bot\}$ maps a candidate to its final number, and $c_{j,i} = \mathbb{1}\big[\,|\phi(y_{j,i}) - a_j^\ast| < 10^{-6}\big]$ marks a correct candidate, with $c_{j,i} = 0$ when $\phi(y_{j,i}) = \bot$.

**Selectors.** A selector $s$ maps an item $(x_j, y_{j,1:N})$ to an index $s(\cdot) \in \{1, \dots, N\}$. It may use only the item's own $N$ candidates and features computed from them, never $a_j^\ast$ or other models' candidates. Its accuracy on $m$ is
$$\mathrm{Acc}_m(s) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{j=1}^{n} c_{j,\, s(x_j, y_{j,1:N})}.$$
Reference selectors: *first*, $s_{\text{first}} = 1$ (one run of the model); *majority vote* (self-consistency), the index of the first candidate whose answer has the most votes $|\{i : \phi(y_{j,i}) = v\}|$ over $v \neq \bot$ (index 1 if every answer is $\bot$); and the *AR scorer*
$$s_{\psi}(\cdot) = \arg\min_{i} \; \mathrm{NLL}_{\psi}(y_{j,i} \mid q_j) \quad \text{or} \quad \arg\min_{i} \; \frac{\mathrm{NLL}_{\psi}(y_{j,i} \mid q_j)}{|y_{j,i}|_{\psi}},$$
the summed and per-token negative log-likelihood under a frozen AR model $\psi$ of the candidate's solution up to its final-answer line (text generated after the answer is excluded). No selector can exceed the *oracle* (hit@$N$), $\mathrm{Acc}_m^{\ast} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_j \max_i c_{j,i}$. The share of the reachable gap that $s$ closes is its *headroom*
$$H_m(s) = \frac{\mathrm{Acc}_m(s) - \mathrm{Acc}_m(s_{\text{first}})}{\mathrm{Acc}_m^{\ast} - \mathrm{Acc}_m(s_{\text{first}})}.$$

**Objective.** Find a training-free selector $s$ that maximises the macro accuracy $\overline{\mathrm{Acc}}(s) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}|}\sum_{m} \mathrm{Acc}_m(s)$ on held-out questions, where success means $\overline{\mathrm{Acc}}(s) > \overline{\mathrm{Acc}}(s_{\text{maj}})$, i.e. beating self-consistency on the macro score (not necessarily on every model).

**Hypothesis (idea 1.1).** For each adapted model, let $Z^{(\ell)}_m, Z^{(\ell)}_{b(m)} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times d}$ be mean-pooled outputs of transformer block $\ell$ of $m$ (bidirectional) and of $b(m)$ (causal) on the same $T$ held-out text segments, and define the preservation score
$$\rho_m = \frac{1}{L}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L} \mathrm{CKA}\big(Z^{(\ell)}_m, Z^{(\ell)}_{b(m)}\big), \qquad \mathrm{CKA}(X, Y) = \frac{\lVert \bar{Y}^{\top}\bar{X} \rVert_F^2}{\lVert \bar{X}^{\top}\bar{X} \rVert_F \, \lVert \bar{Y}^{\top}\bar{Y} \rVert_F},$$
with $\bar{X}, \bar{Y}$ column-centred. The hypothesis is that more preservation means more AR-recoverable accuracy: $\mathrm{Spearman}\big(\rho_m, H_m(s_\psi)\big) > 0$ across $\{m : b(m) \neq \varnothing\}$.

**Assumptions and scope.** Decoding settings (steps, temperature, shots) are fixed per model and differ between models. Dev and test questions are disjoint; selectors are developed on dev, and the held-out test score gates which selector is accepted. With $|\{m : b(m) \neq \varnothing\}| = 4$, the hypothesis test is descriptive only: the smallest attainable two-sided exact permutation $p$ is $1/12$. The broader research problem P1 also asks whether selection is more compute-efficient than raising the denoising-step budget $K$; we compare $\mathrm{Acc}_m(s)$ across $K \in \{16, 32, 64\}$ for Dream and LLaDA, but generation times come from shared GPUs, so a clean compute comparison is not claimed.
