# Abstract

Can an autoregressive (AR) scorer recover useful answers from a diffusion language model, and does preservation of its AR base predict that recovery? We study five released diffusion models on GSM8K with ten cached candidates per question, comparing majority vote, AR likelihood and an agent-designed selector. On 300 held-out questions majority vote reaches 73.3% for Dream and 73.0% for LLaDA; the agent's arithmetic-verification-weighted rule matches it within 0.3 points (73.0% on both). After a cut-text re-score on 500 test questions the best AR selector is 23.6 and 21.0 points below majority at 64 denoising steps, but only 8.6 (Dream, p = 0.002) and 2.6 points (LLaDA, interval includes zero) below at 256 steps ($n = 185$ and $192$). On the original (uncut) scores preservation and AR-recoverable headroom show the predicted rank order, but the corrected scores reverse Dream's sign, so the hypothesis is unsupported. We report a strong self-consistency baseline whose advantage shrinks as the step budget grows, a negative result for AR selection that weakens at 256 steps, and an inconclusive preservation hypothesis.
