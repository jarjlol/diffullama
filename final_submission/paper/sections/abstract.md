# Abstract

Can an autoregressive (AR) scorer recover useful answers from a diffusion language model, and does preservation of its AR base predict that recovery? We study five released diffusion models on GSM8K with ten cached candidates per question, comparing the first sample, majority vote, AR likelihood, the oracle and an agent-designed selector. On 300 held-out questions majority vote reaches 73.3% for Dream and 73.0% for LLaDA; the agent's arithmetic-verification-weighted rule matches it within 0.3 points (73.0% on both), and after a cut-text re-score on 500 test questions the best AR selector remains 23.6 and 21.0 points below majority. On the original (uncut) scores preservation and AR-recoverable headroom show the predicted rank order, but the corrected scores reverse Dream's sign, so the hypothesis is unsupported. Step-budget runs show majority rising from 3.2% at 16 steps to 72.4% at 64 on Dream. We report a strong self-consistency baseline, a negative result for AR selection, and an inconclusive preservation hypothesis.
