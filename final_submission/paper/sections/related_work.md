We organise prior work in four groups: text diffusion language models, adaptation of existing autoregressive (AR) models, selection among sampled answers at test time, and automated research systems, which produced our pipeline. Groups 1 and 2 are filtered from our LLM-generated literature review (12 of its 48 references kept, with the filtering ledger in the repository); groups 3 and 4 add work from outside it.

## 1. Text diffusion language models

**Continuous diffusion.** Diffusion-LM [li2022diffusionlm] denoises Gaussian vectors into word embeddings and uses gradients on the latent variables for fine-grained control. DiffuSeq [gong2022diffuseq] extends this to sequence-to-sequence generation.

**Discrete and masked diffusion.** Reparameterized discrete diffusion [zheng2023reparam] gives a more flexible formulation of discrete sampling. Simple and Effective Masked Diffusion Language Models (MDLM) [sahoo2024mdlm] train with a Rao-Blackwellized masked-diffusion objective that approaches AR perplexity. DiffusionBERT [he2023diffusionbert] trains BERT to reverse a discrete diffusion process, and SSD-LM [han2023ssdlm] generates text block by block and matches or outperforms GPT-2 on its generation benchmarks. Svete and Sabharwal [svete2025reasoning] prove that, for idealised finite-precision transformers of logarithmic width, masked diffusion models are equivalent to padded looped transformers and can solve every problem that chain-of-thought transformers can; this is a theoretical expressivity result, not a measurement of trained checkpoints. Non-autoregressive translation [gu2017nat] is the earlier line of parallel decoding that these models extend: it drops the left-to-right dependency and cuts decoding latency by an order of magnitude at a cost in BLEU.

## 2. Adapting autoregressive models

Continuing to pre-train an existing model is cheaper than training from scratch; Ke et al. [ke2023continual] adapt a language model to a sequence of domain corpora while limiting forgetting. DiffuLLaMA and DiffuGPT [gong2025diffullama] apply continual pre-training to convert GPT-2 and LLaMA models (127M to 7B parameters) into diffusion models; they are among the diffusion models we evaluate. TESS 2 [tae2025tess2] adapts a strong AR model with a diffusion loss and then instruction-tunes it, and its authors find both the adaptation step and the base model to be crucial. Dream-Coder 7B [xie2025dreamcoder] adapts a pre-trained AR checkpoint for code generation.

## 3. Selecting among sampled answers

**Voting and verifiers.** Sampling several solutions and choosing one is a standard way to spend test-time compute. Self-consistency [wang2023selfconsistency] takes the most consistent final answer over sampled reasoning paths, and Cobbe et al. [cobbe2021gsm8k] train a verifier to rank sampled GSM8K solutions.

**Diffusion-specific methods.** Diffusion models have their own variants. HEX [lee2025hex] runs a majority vote over generations made with different block sizes, training-free. Diffuse Thinking [shao2025diffusethinking] has a diffusion model propose candidate thoughts and an LLM evaluate them, and Planner and Executor [berrayana2025planner] couples a discrete diffusion model with an AR model in text space and through a learned latent projector. Post-hoc selectors for diffusion models also exist: an outcome reward model trained on GSM8K reranks eight Dream-Instruct samples to 75.13% against 60.05% for majority vote [zhan2026prm], and the training-free bidirectional-consistency score BMC picks among three LLaDA-Instruct samples at 77.8% against 74.3% for self-consistency [ruan2026bmc]. Both use Instruct checkpoints, zero-shot or with other prompts, so neither number is comparable to our Base-model, four-shot setting.

**Closest to our hypothesis.** Repr-Align [peng2026repralign] argues that preserving the AR model's representations matters during AR-to-diffusion conversion and enforces it with layer-wise alignment; it links preservation to training efficiency, not to test-time selection. Our method also pairs the two model families, but in a different role: an AR model scores several answers sampled from one diffusion model, and we test whether the diffusion model's similarity to its AR base predicts how well this works (see Methodology).

## 4. Automated research systems

Our pipeline is itself produced by LLM research agents. The AI Scientist [lu2024aiscientist] automates idea generation, code, experiments and paper writing. DeepScientist [weng2025deepscientist] casts discovery as Bayesian optimisation over a memory of findings. ResearchAgent [baek2024researchagent] defines problems, methods and experiment designs from the literature and refines them with reviewing agents; we used an implementation of it for ideation. Arbor [jin2026arbor] pairs a long-lived coordinator with short-lived executors over a tree of hypotheses and admits a development-set improvement only if it also holds on held-out data; we used it to codify the selection rule.
